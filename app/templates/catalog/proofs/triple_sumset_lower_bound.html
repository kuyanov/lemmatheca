For finite, nonempty subsets \(A,B,C\) of an additive abelian group,

\[ |A+B+C|\geq\max\{|A|,\,|B|,\,|C|\}. \]

Proof

Set \(D=A+B\). By {% math_ref "nonempty-triple-sumset" %} and its proof, \(D\) is finite and nonempty.

Apply the {% math_ref "sumset-lower-bound" entry_id="thm-sumset-lower-bound" from_block="triple-sumset-lower-bound" %} to \(A\) and \(B\). It gives

\[ |D|\geq\max\{|A|,\,|B|\}. \]

Now apply the same lemma to \(D\) and \(C\):

\[ |D+C|\geq\max\{|D|,\,|C|\}. \]

Thus \(|D+C|\) is at least \(|D|\), hence at least each of \(|A|\) and \(|B|\), and it is also at least \(|C|\). Since \(D+C=A+B+C\),

\[ |A+B+C|\geq\max\{|A|,\,|B|,\,|C|\}. \]

Both applications use the nonemptiness of the summands. □
