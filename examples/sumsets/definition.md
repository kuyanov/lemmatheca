# Sumset

Let \(G\) be an additive abelian group and let \(A,B\subseteq G\). Their
**sumset** consists of every sum formed by choosing one element from each set:

\[
A+B=\{a+b\mid a\in A,\ b\in B\}.
\]

For instance, in the integers, if \(A=\{0,1\}\) and \(B=\{0,2\}\), then
\(A+B=\{0,1,2,3\}\). If instead \(B=\{0,1\}\), then
\(A+B=\{0,1,2\}\): distinct pairs can produce the same sum.

We count the distinct sums, not their representations. This is the starting
point for studying how the additive structure of two sets controls the size of
their sumset.
