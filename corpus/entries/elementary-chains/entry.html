A sequence of structures with compatible interpretations

Fix a language \(L\). An increasing chain of nonempty \(L\)-structures is a sequence

\[M_0\subseteq M_1\subseteq M_2\subseteq\cdots,\]

where each inclusion is a substructure inclusion. Thus interpretations on old tuples stay the same: constants have the same values, functions agree on the smaller domain, and relations on old tuples do not change. Write \(U=\bigcup_{n<\omega}|M_n|\) for the union of the domains.

Every finite tuple from \(U\) belongs to one stage. For each coordinate choose a stage containing it, then take the largest of those finitely many indices. The empty tuple belongs to every stage. This observation lets us interpret every symbol on \(U\), since the language's symbols have finite arity.

The union is a structure

There is a unique \(L\)-structure \(M\) with domain \(U\) for which every \(M_n\) is a substructure. We call \(M\) the union of the chain and write \(M=\bigcup_{n<\omega}M_n\).

The domain is nonempty because it contains \(|M_0|\). Interpret a constant by its value in \(M_0\). To evaluate a function \(f\) at a finite tuple \(\bar a\in U^k\), choose \(n\) containing the tuple and put

\[f^M(\bar a)=f^{M_n}(\bar a).\]

For a relation symbol \(R\), use the rule

\[M\models R[\bar a]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M_n\models R[\bar a].\]

If two stages contain the tuple, both sit inside the stage with their larger index. The substructure conditions make the two function values and the two relation truth values agree there. Thus the definitions are independent of the chosen stage. A zero-argument function is a constant; a zero-argument relation has the same truth value at every stage. The empty tuple therefore causes no exception.

The function values remain in \(U\), and all interpretations restrict to those of each \(M_n\). Hence \(M_n\subseteq M\). These restrictions also force the interpretations on every tuple of \(U\), proving uniqueness. In particular, terms and quantifier-free formulas agree between a stage and the union at parameters from that stage.

Preserving formulas at every enlargement

An increasing chain is elementary if \(M_m\preccurlyeq M_n\) whenever \(m\le n\). Here elementarity means agreement on every formula whose parameters belong to the smaller domain:

\[M_m\models\varphi[\bar a]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M_n\models\varphi[\bar a].\]

It is enough to check \(M_n\preccurlyeq M_{n+1}\) for every \(n\). Composing the successive elementary inclusions gives the inclusion from \(M_m\) to \(M_n\); the identity handles \(m=n\). Notice that this concerns the actual inclusions, not merely the existence of isomorphisms between the stages.

Tarski's elementary chain theorem

If \(M_0\preccurlyeq M_1\preccurlyeq\cdots\) is an elementary chain of nonempty structures in any language \(L\), then every stage is an elementary substructure of its union:

\[M_n\preccurlyeq\bigcup_{r<\omega}M_r\qquad(n<\omega).\]

The language and the individual domains may have any cardinality.

Let \(M\) be the union structure. Induct on formulas, proving agreement between \(M_n\) and \(M\) simultaneously for every \(n\) and every finite parameter tuple from \(M_n\). The gives the atomic cases. The truth rules for the propositional connectives preserve agreement.

Consider \(\exists y\,\psi(\bar x,y)\), and assume the induction statement for \(\psi\). If \(M_n\models\exists y\,\psi[\bar a,y]\), a witness \(b\in|M_n|\) satisfies the body there. Induction gives \(M\models\psi[\bar a,b]\), so the existential formula holds in \(M\).

Conversely, suppose \(M\models\exists y\,\psi[\bar a,y]\), and choose a witness \(b\in U\). There is \(r\ge n\) such that \(b\in|M_r|\); the parameters \(\bar a\) also lie in that stage. Induction gives \(M_r\models\psi[\bar a,b]\), hence \(M_r\models\exists y\,\psi[\bar a,y]\). Because \(M_n\preccurlyeq M_r\), the existential formula is true in \(M_n\). Its witness there need not be the originally chosen \(b\).

Universal formulas follow from quantifier duality, \(\forall y\,\psi\leftrightarrow\neg\exists y\,\neg\psi\). This proves agreement for every formula, so \(M_n\preccurlyeq M\).

The union satisfies the same theories

For any theory \(T\), the union of an elementary chain satisfies \(T\) if and only if any one of its stages satisfies \(T\). In particular, an elementary chain of models of \(T\) has a union that is again a model of \(T\).

Apply to sentences, which have no free parameters. Every stage and the union give the same truth value to each sentence, and hence satisfy exactly the same theories.

Rational tails form an elementary chain

In the language with only the strict-order relation, let

\[D_n=\{q\in\mathbb Q:-n<q\},\]

with the inherited order. Then \(D_n\preccurlyeq D_{n+1}\), and the union is \((\mathbb Q,<)\).

Each \(D_n\) is nonempty, densely linearly ordered and has no endpoints. Between two elements use their midpoint. For an element \(q\), the midpoint of \(-n\) and \(q\) is a smaller element of \(D_n\), while \(q+1\) is a larger one. The inclusion \(D_n\subseteq D_{n+1}\) preserves the strict order. By the elementarity of order embeddings between dense linear orders without endpoints, this inclusion is elementary.

Every rational number is larger than some negative integer, so \(\bigcup_nD_n=\mathbb Q\). The chain theorem therefore shows that every \(D_n\) is elementary in \((\mathbb Q,<)\).

Are isomorphic stages enough?

Suppose an increasing chain has all its stages isomorphic. Must the union satisfy the same sentences as the stages?

Show answerHide answer

No. In the strict-order language, take the integer tails

\[A_n=\{z\in\mathbb Z:-n\le z\}.\]

They form an increasing chain of substructures, and \(z\mapsto z+n\) is an order isomorphism from \(A_n\) to \(A_0\), with inverse \(z\mapsto z-n\). Thus the stages are elementarily equivalent. Each stage has the least element \(-n\), so it satisfies

\[\exists x\,\forall y\,\neg(y<x).\]

The union is \((\mathbb Z,<)\), where every \(z\) has the smaller element \(z-1\). That sentence is false in the union.

The actual inclusions are not elementary. With the old parameter \(x=-n\), the formula \(\exists y\,(y<x)\) is false in \(A_n\) and true in \(A_{n+1}\), witnessed by \(-n-1\). Isomorphisms between the stages move elements; elementarity of an inclusion must preserve formulas at the same old elements.

Two formula shapes with one-way preservation

A universal formula has the form

\[\forall y_1\cdots\forall y_k\,\theta(\bar x,\bar y),\]

and an existential formula has the form

\[\exists y_1\cdots\exists y_k\,\theta(\bar x,\bar y),\]

where \(\theta\) is quantifier-free. The block of quantifiers may be empty, so every quantifier-free formula has both shapes. The tuple \(\bar x\) lists the free parameters. We use these syntactic shapes; a formula equivalent to one of them has the same preservation property by satisfaction of the equivalence.

Universal truth goes down; existential truth goes up

Let \(A\subseteq B\) be nonempty \(L\)-structures and \(\bar a\) a finite tuple from \(A\).

If \(\varphi\) is universal, then

\[B\models\varphi[\bar a]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\models\varphi[\bar a].\]

If \(\psi\) is existential, then

\[A\models\psi[\bar a]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad B\models\psi[\bar a].\]

For a universal formula, choose any tuple \(\bar b\) from \(A\) for its quantified variables. It is also a tuple from \(B\), so the quantifier-free matrix is true in \(B\) at \((\bar a,\bar b)\). Quantifier-free agreement makes it true in \(A\). Since \(\bar b\) was arbitrary, the universal formula is true in \(A\).

For an existential formula true in \(A\), keep its witness tuple \(\bar b\) in the larger domain. Quantifier-free agreement makes the matrix true in \(B\) at the same tuple, giving the required witnesses there. Empty quantifier blocks reduce both arguments to quantifier-free agreement.

Substructures of models of universal axioms

A universal theory is a theory consisting of universal sentences. Every nonempty substructure of a model of a universal theory is again a model of that theory.

Each axiom passes from the larger structure to the substructure by . The substructure therefore satisfies all the axioms.

In a function-free language, retaining finitely many existential witnesses and all constant values gives the bounded-model theorem for existential–universal sentences.

Can the preservation directions be reversed?

Neither direction is valid. Use the integer tails \(A_0\subseteq A_1\) from and the old parameter \(0\). The universal formula \(\forall y\,\neg(y<0)\) is true in \(A_0\), whose least element is \(0\), but false in \(A_1\), which contains \(-1\). The existential formula \(\exists y\,(y<0)\) is true in \(A_1\) and false in \(A_0\).

Elementary substructures preserve every formula in both directions. Ordinary substructures guarantee the one-way assertions of ; those assertions alone do not make their inclusions elementary.
