Triple sumsets

Let \(A,B,C\) be finite subsets of an additive abelian group. We group the sum on the left and write

\[ A+B+C=(A+B)+C=\{(a+b)+c\mid a\in A,\ b\in B,\ c\in C\}. \]

Our bounds below assume that each of the three summands is nonempty.

Nonempty triple sumsets

If \(A,B,C\) are nonempty, then both \(A+B\) and \((A+B)+C\) are nonempty.

Proof

The Nonempty sumsets lemma gives an element of \(A+B\). Applying it again to \(A+B\) and \(C\) gives an element of \((A+B)+C\). Both sets are finite because each sum is formed from finitely many choices. □

Triple sumset lower bound

For finite, nonempty subsets \(A,B,C\) of an additive abelian group,

\[ |A+B+C|\geq\max\{|A|,\,|B|,\,|C|\}. \]

Proof

Set \(D=A+B\). By Nonempty triple sumsets and its proof, \(D\) is finite and nonempty.

Apply the Sumset lower bound to \(A\) and \(B\). It gives

\[ |D|\geq\max\{|A|,\,|B|\}. \]

Now apply the same lemma to \(D\) and \(C\):

\[ |D+C|\geq\max\{|D|,\,|C|\}. \]

Thus \(|D+C|\) is at least \(|D|\), hence at least each of \(|A|\) and \(|B|\), and it is also at least \(|C|\). Since \(D+C=A+B+C\),

\[ |A+B+C|\geq\max\{|A|,\,|B|,\,|C|\}. \]

Both applications use the nonemptiness of the summands. □

Does another summand force growth?

Must adding a third nonempty set strictly increase the size of \(A+B\)?

Show answerHide answer

No. For \(A=B=C=\{0\}\) in the integers,

\[ A+B=(A+B)+C=\{0\}. \]

More generally, A neutral summand shows that choosing \(C=\{0\}\) leaves any sumset \(A+B\) unchanged.
