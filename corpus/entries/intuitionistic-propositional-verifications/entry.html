Distinguish an available use from a verification

Fix an atom set \(P\), the native propositional formulas and a finite set \(\Gamma\) of allowed assumptions. We define two collections of finite proof trees simultaneously. A neutral use, written \(u:\Gamma\vdash A\mathord\downarrow\), starts from an assumption and follows eliminations. A normal verification, written \(v:\Gamma\vdash A\mathord\uparrow\), uses the introduction and branching rules specified below. Every child in either collection must itself be a tree in the indicated collection; these are syntactic certificates, rather than semantic entailments.

A neutral use may be an assumption \(A\in\Gamma\). Its other rules are conjunction projections and the following applications:

\[\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\land B\downarrow}{\Gamma\vdash A\downarrow},\qquad \frac{\Gamma\vdash A\land B\downarrow}{\Gamma\vdash B\downarrow}.\]
\[\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\to B\downarrow\qquad\Gamma\vdash A\uparrow}{\Gamma\vdash B\downarrow},\qquad \frac{\Gamma\vdash\neg A\downarrow\qquad\Gamma\vdash A\uparrow}{\Gamma\vdash\bot\downarrow}.\]

The major premise of every elimination is neutral. In particular, an implication just obtained by introduction cannot immediately be used as the neutral major premise of an application. Primitive negation has its own application rule; it is not silently replaced by an implication tree.

Introduce conclusions and branch only on a neutral use

A neutral use gives a neutral verification of the same formula. The introduction verifications are conjunction introduction, either disjunction introduction, implication introduction from \(\Gamma,A\vdash B\uparrow\), and primitive-negation introduction from \(\Gamma,A\vdash\bot\uparrow\). As usual, \(\Gamma,A\) means insertion of \(A\) into the finite assumption set. The additional rules are

\[\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\lor B\downarrow\quad\Gamma,A\vdash C\uparrow\quad\Gamma,B\vdash C\uparrow}{\Gamma\vdash C\uparrow},\qquad \frac{\Gamma\vdash\bot\downarrow}{\Gamma\vdash C\uparrow}.\]

These define case verification and explosion verification. There is no general rule turning a normal verification into a neutral use. A case conclusion or an explosion conclusion therefore cannot become the major premise of a later elimination. Further eliminations must instead be performed inside its branches, or bypassed by explosion.

Every tree generated by these two rule lists is called normal in the present sense. This is a precise description of its shape, not an assertion about every possible sequence of reductions on unrestricted deductions.

Enlarge the allowed assumptions without changing normal shape

If \(\Gamma\subseteq\Delta\), a neutral use from \(\Gamma\) gives a neutral use of the same formula from \(\Delta\), and a normal verification from \(\Gamma\) gives a normal verification from \(\Delta\).

Recurse simultaneously on both sorts of tree. An assumption remains available by inclusion. Reapply projections, applications and introductions after transforming their children. Under implication or negation introduction, use \(\Gamma,A\subseteq\Delta,A\). For case verification, weaken its neutral major premise and its two branches, using the respective inclusions with \(A\) and \(B\) inserted. Explosion uses the weakened neutral proof of falsum. Every major premise remains neutral, so the operation preserves the stated normal shape.

Supply neutral uses for the assumptions of a sequent tree

For finite contexts \(\Delta,\Gamma\), a neutral environment \(\eta:\Delta\Rightarrow\Gamma\) assigns a neutral use \(\eta(D):\Gamma\vdash D\downarrow\) to every \(D\in\Delta\). The source context lists assumptions requested by a proof; the target context lists assumptions available in the resulting verification. The identity environment \(\mathrm{id}_\Gamma\) supplies each \(D\in\Gamma\) by its assumption leaf.

The restriction to neutral replacements is essential. Ordinary proof replacement permits an arbitrary derivation at an assumption leaf. Such a replacement can put an introduced implication immediately above an elimination. The environment here supplies uses that can still serve as the major premises of the neutral rules.

Extend an environment at a temporary assumption or a left rule

A neutral environment \(\eta:\Delta\Rightarrow\Gamma\) extends to one from \(\Delta,A\) to \(\Gamma,A\): supply the new \(A\) by its assumption and weaken every ambient supplied use. It also extends to \(\Delta,A\Rightarrow\Gamma\) whenever a specified neutral use of \(A\) from \(\Gamma\) is supplied.

For membership in an inserted source context, distinguish the inserted formula from an ambient member. In the first case return the specified new use; in the second return its old use, weakened if the target context has grown. If the inserted formula was already an ambient member, either supplied tree has the same required formula, so the construction remains well typed.

There are three useful sources of the specified new use. A neutral \(A\land B\) supplies neutral projections of both factors. A neutral \(A\to B\), together with a normal verification of \(A\), supplies a neutral use of \(B\). A neutral \(\neg A\), together with a normal verification of \(A\), supplies neutral falsum. These are exactly the neutral rules, so no unproved substitution principle for normal trees is being assumed.

Interpret a cut-free sequent tree in a neutral environment

For every supplied cut-free certificate \(p\) of \(\Delta\Rightarrow C\) and every neutral environment \(\eta:\Delta\Rightarrow\Gamma\), there is a specified normal verification \(V(p,\eta):\Gamma\vdash C\uparrow\). In particular, \(V(p,\mathrm{id}_\Gamma)\) is a normal verification of the endsequent of \(p\).

Define \(V\) by recursion on the supplied finite sequent tree, with its target context and environment arbitrary. An atomic identity leaf becomes the verification of the neutral use supplied by \(\eta\). Falsum-left uses \(\eta(\bot)\) and explosion verification. A right introduction recursively transforms its children and reapplies that introduction. Under implication or negation introduction, extend the environment to the new temporary assumption by .

At conjunction-left, use the environment's neutral principal conjunction to supply its two projections. Extend the same target environment by these two neutral uses, transform the premise, and return its result. At implication-left, first transform the antecedent premise into a normal verification. Apply the environment's neutral implication to this verification, obtaining the neutral consequent needed to extend the environment for the continuation. Transform that continuation and return its result. Negation-left similarly obtains neutral falsum and uses explosion verification.

At disjunction-left, the environment supplies a neutral use of the principal disjunction. Transform its two smaller premise trees in target contexts \(\Gamma,A\) and \(\Gamma,B\), using the respective extended environments. Apply case verification to those two results. Every recursive call uses an original strict subtree; the newly constructed normal trees are never fed back into the recursion. These clauses cover all primitive sequent rules and preserve the indexed end formula.

Supply the input before using the output of an implication

Suppose a cut-free implication-left step uses \(D=A\to B\in\Delta\), a premise \(p:\Delta\Rightarrow A\), and a continuation \(q:\Delta,B\Rightarrow C\). For every \(\eta:\Delta\Rightarrow\Gamma\), its normal interpretation is the interpretation of \(q\) in the environment extended by the neutral use

\[\eta(D)\,V(p,\eta):\Gamma\vdash B\downarrow.\]

The supplied implication is neutral, while \(V(p,\eta)\) is normal, so their application is one of the defining neutral rules. Ambient assumptions of the continuation keep their values under \(\eta\); its new assumption \(B\) receives this application. The continuation is a smaller source tree, so its recursive interpretation is already defined. This explains the implication-left clause of \(V\) without inserting an arbitrary normal proof into a major elimination position.

Translate both normal sorts back into cut-free certificates

A neutral use \(\Gamma\vdash A\downarrow\) gives an actual cut-free certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\), and so does a normal verification \(\Gamma\vdash A\uparrow\). Both translations preserve the actual finite context and native formula.

Recurse simultaneously on both sorts. An assumption becomes the expanded sequent identity. A neutral conjunction projection uses the corresponding right inversion of the translated major premise. For a neutral implication application, invert its translated implication to obtain \(\Gamma,A\Rightarrow B\), then compose the translated argument by the proved cut-free cut operation. Native-negation application uses negation inversion and the same operation to obtain falsum.

A neutral verification keeps its translated certificate. Introductions reapply the corresponding sequent right rules. For case verification, translate the neutral disjunction and the two normal branches, then apply the cut-free disjunction-consumer operation. Explosion uses the syntactic transformation from a falsum certificate to any conclusion. Each child is smaller in the mutually defined tree, and every composition operation already returns a primitive cut-free certificate. This supplies the reverse translation and the certificate operations used below.

Move a later use into the two branches of a case split

Let \(u:\Gamma\vdash A\lor B\downarrow\), and let normal branches under \(A\) and \(B\) verify \(X\land Y\). There is a normal verification of \(X\) from \(\Gamma\), obtained by projecting inside the corresponding cut-free branch trees and then performing the case split.

Convert the two normal branches into cut-free certificates by . The conjunction inversion operation produces certificates of \(X\) in their respective contexts. Interpret these certificates using their identity environments to obtain normal branches verifying \(X\). Case verification with the original neutral \(u\) now gives a normal verification of \(X\).

Thus the final shape is a case split with a normal conclusion in each branch. Applying a conjunction projection directly to the original case verification would violate the neutral-major-premise condition. This construction establishes the displayed normal result; it does not identify these proof trees by an unstated conversion relation.

Rebuild a supplied cut proof as a normal verification

For a supplied certificate with cut \(p:\Gamma\Rightarrow A\), define its normal reconstruction by

\[\mathsf{nf}(p)=V(\operatorname{eliminate}(p),\mathrm{id}_\Gamma).\]

The specified operation returns a normal verification of exactly \(A\) from exactly \(\Gamma\). Moreover, for finite \(\Gamma\), intuitionistic derivability \(\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\) is equivalent to the existence of a normal verification \(\Gamma\vdash A\uparrow\).

Cut elimination returns a cut-free certificate with the same endsequent. Its environment interpretation therefore has precisely the displayed normal type. For the forward implication in the equivalence, the established presentation bridge turns a finite-context intuitionistic derivation into existence of a cut-free certificate; interpret it in its identity environment. Conversely, translate a supplied normal verification into a cut-free certificate and then into the established intuitionistic calculus. These are syntactic proof translations throughout.

The first assertion takes an actual input tree and constructs an actual output tree. The second is an existence equivalence for the established derivability relation. Neither assertion states that the normal reconstruction has the same proof identity as its input, or that every sequence of local reductions terminates.

Keep a finite support when the allowed premise set is infinite

For every set \(\Theta\) of native formulas and formula \(A\), intuitionistic derivability \(\Theta\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\) is equivalent to the existence of some finite \(\Gamma\subseteq\Theta\) with a normal verification \(\Gamma\vdash A\uparrow\).

Apply finite support to a given intuitionistic derivation, and use on that finite context. In the other direction, translate the finite normal verification into intuitionistic deduction and weaken its allowed assumptions along \(\Gamma\subseteq\Theta\). A normal tree remains finite even when its ambient permitted premise set is infinite; its chosen finite support is the context recorded in this statement.

Record every displayed formula in a normal tree

The trace of a neutral use or normal verification is the finite set of all formulas displayed as an antecedent member or a conclusion anywhere in its tree. Its recursive definition starts with the current context and conclusion, then unions the traces of its children. This includes temporary assumptions inside introductions and case branches.

Use the native analytic closure \(S(A)\), which consists of the native subformulas together with \(\bot\). Put \(S(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{D\in\Gamma}S(D)\) and \(S(\Gamma,A)=S(\Gamma)\cup S(A)\). The added falsum permits the explicit premise of primitive-negation introduction.

Neutral spines and normal traces stay inside the end closure

The trace of every normal verification \(\Gamma\vdash A\uparrow\) is contained in \(S(\Gamma,A)\). More generally, let a set \(\Xi\) contain \(\bot\), contain \(\Gamma\), and be closed under the native analytic closures of its members. Then every neutral use from \(\Gamma\) has its conclusion and its whole trace in \(\Xi\); every normal verification from \(\Gamma\) has its whole trace in \(\Xi\) whenever its conclusion belongs to \(\Xi\).

Prove the two stronger assertions simultaneously by induction on trees. An assumption is in \(\Xi\) by \(\Gamma\subseteq\Xi\). A neutral projection has a neutral major conjunction; its induction hypothesis puts that conjunction in \(\Xi\), so closure puts the selected component there too. A neutral application has a neutral major implication. Its induction hypothesis supplies both component formulas in \(\Xi\), so the normal argument hypothesis applies to the required antecedent. Its result and the application's consequent are therefore in \(\Xi\). A primitive-negation application uses its child formula and the included falsum in the same way.

For a normal introduction, closure of the concluding formula supplies its component conclusions and any discharged assumption; the normal child induction hypotheses apply in the enlarged contexts. For a case verification, the neutral major premise puts the disjunction in \(\Xi\), hence both branch assumptions belong to \(\Xi\). The two normal branch hypotheses apply with the common conclusion, already assumed in \(\Xi\). For explosion, the neutral child's trace is covered and the arbitrary concluding formula belongs to \(\Xi\) by the normal assertion's hypothesis. Finally each trace is the union of these child traces with its displayed root formulas, so it stays inside \(\Xi\).

Take \(\Xi=S(\Gamma,A)\). This contains the required root formulas and falsum, and subformula transitivity makes it closed under the same analytic operation. The normal assertion gives the claimed trace bound.

A neutral spine must start from an open assumption

For every native formula \(A\), there is no neutral use \(\varnothing\vdash A\downarrow\).

Follow the neutral major premise backwards through a projection or application. The tree is finite, so this path ends at an assumption leaf. Every neutral rule keeps the same context on that path; a normal argument of an application is not on it. The leaf would therefore require an assumption in the empty set, which is impossible. Equivalently, recurse on the neutral tree, returning the empty-membership contradiction at its assumption leaf.

Extract a theorem disjunct from the normal conclusion

From a supplied normal verification \(\varnothing\vdash A\lor B\uparrow\), a specified operation returns either a normal verification of \(A\) or a normal verification of \(B\), with no assumptions. Composing this operation with \(\mathsf{nf}\) extracts such a disjunct verification from any supplied closed cut certificate of \(A\lor B\).

Inspect the final normal constructor. Neutral inclusion, case verification and explosion would each require a closed neutral use, forbidden by . Conjunction, implication and negation introductions have the wrong outer conclusion. Only the two disjunction introductions remain. Return the child of the actual constructor: the left child verifies \(A\), and the right child verifies \(B\). This is an inspection of a supplied finite proof tree, not a semantic choice between two known existential alternatives.

The reconstruction and exact presentation bridge give the disjunction property for the established intuitionistic calculus as well, for arbitrary atom sets. The reverse direction follows from the relevant disjunction introduction. The claim concerns empty open assumptions; a neutral assumed disjunction can support a case verification without either branch being a closed theorem.

The equality-free first-order extension permits quantified disjuncts and also extracts a term with its proved existential instance. Its public version keeps any auxiliary names in the returned term.

Remove an introduced conjunction used only for its first factor

For atoms \(p,q\), let \(\Gamma=\{p,q\}\). A certificate with cut can first derive \(p\land q\), then use conjunction-left to conclude \(p\). The same endsequent has the normal verification obtained by making the assumption \(p\) a neutral use and then a neutral verification.

The producer has its two atomic identity leaves and ends with conjunction-right. The consumer, under the additional \(p\land q\), uses conjunction-left and the available atomic identity of \(p\). A cut composes these two certificates, giving the specified detour certificate from \(\Gamma\). Independently, \(p\in\Gamma\) gives the stated one-leaf neutral use and its normal inclusion. Both certificates conclude exactly \(p\) from the same permitted context. Normal reconstruction provides a normal output for the detour; no equality between different proof trees is required here.

Can every normal verification be used as a neutral major premise?

Would adding a rule from \(\Gamma\vdash A\uparrow\) to \(\Gamma\vdash A\downarrow\) preserve the defining separation of the two sorts?

Show answerHide answer

No. For every formula \(A\), assume \(A\), use that neutral assumption as a verification, and discharge it. This is a closed normal verification of \(A\to A\). A conversion of every normal verification into a neutral use would therefore give a closed neutral use of this implication, contrary to .

This witness uses no atom, since one may take \(A=\bot\). The restriction is a property of the proof representation, not a claim that implication elimination is invalid in unrestricted intuitionistic deduction.

Why does the normal shape rule out atomic excluded middle?

For an atom \(p\), can there be a closed normal verification of \(p\lor\neg p\)?

Show answerHide answer

No. The extraction operation would give a closed normal verification of \(p\) or of \(\neg p\). Translate that verification into ordinary intuitionistic deduction. The Boolean soundness theorem rules out the first alternative using a valuation making every atom false. It rules out the second using a valuation making every atom true. Thus neither child can be a closed theorem, so the original normal disjunction cannot exist.

This argument uses normal shape together with classical soundness only for the two prospective children. A classical tautology test by itself cannot reject \(p\lor\neg p\), which is true under every Boolean valuation.
