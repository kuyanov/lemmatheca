Translate the expanded arithmetic formula

Use the bounded expression grammar with equality, the defined comparisons \(\le,<\), all four Boolean connectives, and strict or inclusive bounded quantifiers. Its bounding term uses only older names; the newly bound input may occur in the body. Write \(\mathcal E(B)\) for the actual native expansion in the original arithmetic language \(0,S,+,\cdot\).

The translated bounded expansion of \(B\) is \(\mathcal E(B)^{\mathsf N}\), using the fixed Gödel–Gentzen translation. Equations become double-negated equations; falsum is fixed; negation, conjunction, implication and universal quantification keep their outer constructors. The remaining clauses are

\[(P\lor Q)^{\mathsf N}=\neg\neg(P^{\mathsf N}\lor Q^{\mathsf N}),\qquad (\exists xP)^{\mathsf N}=\neg\neg\exists xP^{\mathsf N}.\]

Work over a finite ordered tuple of original names, allowing the empty tuple, and a fixed finite additional context \(\Gamma\). A certificate always means an actual native HA certificate using finitely many of its original arithmetic axioms. Logical equality is enabled. Each new input introduced for a quantifier is protected and fresh for the lifted context.

Term replacement preserves the bound and translation identities

For every specified term substitution \(\sigma\) for the old names, \(B[\sigma]\) is bounded and

\[\mathcal E(B[\sigma])=\mathcal E(B)[\sigma],\qquad \mathcal E(B[\sigma])^{\mathsf N}=\mathcal E(B)^{\mathsf N}[\sigma].\]

The same identities hold for renaming and protected opening. Under a bounded quantifier, lift \(\sigma\) by fixing its newest input; the bound itself receives only the old-name substitution.

The first equality is the established expansion substitution law. Apply the negative-translation scope law to this literal expansion for the second. Both laws fix a protected input when passing under its binder. The bound proviso therefore identifies the resulting bound with the specified old term, even when later bounds depend on earlier inputs. Neither operation inserts a new formula constructor into the bounded expression.

Transport a proof conversion through double negation

Suppose an intuitionistic certificate operation converts a proof of \(P\) to a proof of \(Q\), in every finite additional context. Then it also converts \(\neg\neg P\) to \(\neg\neg Q\). If \(Q\) has a supplied certificate of \(\neg\neg Q\to Q\), this gives a conversion from \(\neg\neg P\) to \(Q\).

Given \(\neg\neg P\), assume \(\neg Q\). Under a further temporary \(P\), apply the specified operation to obtain \(Q\), contradicting \(\neg Q\). Discharge \(P\) to obtain \(\neg P\), contradict the supplied \(\neg\neg P\), and discharge \(\neg Q\). This is an actual certificate of \(\neg\neg Q\). Apply the supplied stability implication for the last assertion. Every new assumption is temporary; the original context remains unchanged.

We also use constructive double-negation introduction: from \(P\), assume \(\neg P\), derive falsum and discharge. A decision certificate \(Q\lor\neg Q\) supplies the required stability implication by case elimination and explosion in its negative branch, as in bounded stability.

The translated order guards return to their original macros

For arbitrary arithmetic terms \(s,t\), HA certificate operations convert each of \(s\le t\) and \(s<t\) to its actual negative translation and back, with the same original names and additional context. In particular the first translated macro is literally

\[(s\le t)^{\mathsf N}=\neg\neg\exists d\,\neg\neg(d+s=t).\]

Recall that \(s\le t\) means \(\exists d(d+s=t)\), not an atomic relation. From a proof of this existential, open its witness \(d\), introduce double negation at the equation, introduce \(\exists d\,\neg\neg(d+s=t)\) at that same witness, and double-negate the result. The final formula is independent of the protected witness, so existential elimination closes the branch.

Conversely, an actual proof of \(\exists d\,\neg\neg(d+s=t)\) converts to \(\exists d(d+s=t)\): open the witness, apply arithmetic equality stability at its two terms, and reintroduce the same witness. Apply to this conversion. It takes a proof of the displayed translated macro to \(\neg\neg(s\le t)\). The order decision at these actual terms removes that last double negation.

Finally \(s<t\) is exactly \(Ss\le t\). Apply the two just constructed operations at \(Ss,t\). All fresh difference witnesses are separate protected inputs; the old terms are lifted unchanged into their branches.

Combine the conversions through the Boolean constructors

Assume that \(P=\mathcal E(B)\) and \(Q=\mathcal E(C)\) already have certificate conversions to and from \(P^{\mathsf N},Q^{\mathsf N}\) in every finite context. Then their negation, conjunction, implication and disjunction have such conversions too. Falsum is fixed, and an equation has its two conversions by double-negation introduction and arithmetic atomic stability.

For \(\neg P\), assume \(P^{\mathsf N}\), convert it back to \(P\), and contradict the supplied negation; discharge. Reverse the child conversion for the converse. A conjunction projects its two factors, converts them, and conjoins them again in either direction.

For \(P\to Q\), assume \(P^{\mathsf N}\), convert backwards to \(P\), apply the supplied implication, convert its output forwards to \(Q^{\mathsf N}\), and discharge. The reverse operation assumes \(P\), converts it forwards, uses the translated implication and converts its output backwards. Thus the antecedent is converted in the opposite direction to the consequent.

For \(P\lor Q\), eliminate the original disjunction. Convert the selected child forwards, introduce \(P^{\mathsf N}\lor Q^{\mathsf N}\), and double-negate. Conversely, eliminate an actual proof of \(P^{\mathsf N}\lor Q^{\mathsf N}\), convert its selected child backwards and introduce \(P\lor Q\). Transport this operation through double negation by . It gives \(\neg\neg(P\lor Q)\) from the supplied translation. The bounded decision for \(B\lor C\) removes that double negation. The factors need not be native quantifier-free formulas.

Retain each bounded witness before removing double negation

With the same guard and body assumptions, put \(V=\exists x(G(x)\land P(x))\). Its actual translation is

\[V^{\mathsf N}=\neg\neg\exists x\,(G(x)^{\mathsf N}\land P(x)^{\mathsf N}).\]

Double-negate that conclusion before closing the original witness branch. The final conclusion has no free occurrence of the protected witness.

For the reverse conversion, first consider an actual proof of \(W=\exists x(G(x)^{\mathsf N}\land P(x)^{\mathsf N})\). Now takes the supplied \(\neg\neg W\) to \(\neg\neg V\). The stability certificate for the whole expanded bounded existential \(V\) yields \(V\).

Allow an unbounded universal prefix over the bounded matrix

A universal-bounded formula is an actual native formula \(F\) obtained from an expanded bounded expression by adding a finite prefix of native universal quantifiers, possibly empty. Each prefix input is protected in its body. Thus a typical closed formula has the specified presentation

\[F=\forall x_0\cdots\forall x_{r-1}\,\mathcal E(B(x_0,\ldots,x_{r-1})).\]

The matrix may contain all the bounded constructors, including nested bounds. We use syntactic Pi-one presentation, written \(\Pi_1\), for this displayed arithmetic prefix convention. It refers to the given formula presentation, not merely to an external equivalence in the standard natural numbers. Additional free parameters are allowed before closing them. Native term substitution and protected opening preserve this class, by the bounded scope laws.

PA and HA prove the same universal-bounded sentences

For every closed universal-bounded native formula \(F\), let \(c(F)\) be its established full-to-small classical expansion. Then

\[\mathrm{PA}\vdash c(F)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{HA}^{\mathrm C}\vdash F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{HA}\vdash F.\]

Thus PA is conservative over HA for the stated syntactic \(\Pi_1\) presentations, including an empty universal prefix.

The native PA correspondence gives the first equivalence for the actual classical expansion \(c(F)\). Transform a supplied \(\mathrm{HA}^{\mathrm C}\) certificate using the proved arithmetic interpretation. Its conclusion is \(F^{\mathsf N}\); its translated additional context is empty. The backward operation in gives an HA certificate of the original \(F\). Conversely, copy an HA certificate into classical mode, retaining its arithmetic axioms. Every transformation uses the unchanged original HA axiom theory.

A nested bounded witness lies within the new conclusion class

Put

\[B(a)=\forall x<a\;\exists y\le x\,(y=x\lor y=Sx),\qquad F=\forall a\,\mathcal E(B(a)).\]

This is a universal-bounded sentence. HA proves \(F\), and hence its negative translation. The inner existential and the order abbreviations in its actual expansion prevent this presentation from having a native quantifier-free matrix.

No. At a protected \(x\), the translated input gives \(\neg\neg\exists y\,\mathcal E(R(x,y))^{\mathsf N}\). The body conversion transports this to \(\neg\neg\exists y\,\mathcal E(R(x,y))\). The bounded-existential step then needs a stability certificate for that whole existential. Our uniform decision and stability constructions apply to a specified bounded search, and provide none for this unbounded existential.

This argument therefore does not cover the general displayed \(\Pi_2\) presentation. A different conservation argument would require additional mathematics. In particular the parameterized Friedman arithmetic application has its own untranslated-axiom preservation requirements; the present Gödel–Gentzen conversions do not establish those requirements.
