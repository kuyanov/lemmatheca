Keep the allowed assumptions and the name universe fixed

Fix a first-order language \(L\) and original free names \(X\). Work in the shared full formula syntax, with native conjunction, disjunction and negation. Every formula in this entry is equality-free: logical equality occurs nowhere in it. A finite allowed context is written \(\Gamma\). A one-slot body \(B\) may use the original names and its one available placeholder; \(B(t)\) denotes its capture-free instance at an actual term \(t\in\operatorname{Term}_L(X)\).

Write \(\mathcal R_X(\Gamma;A)\) for the raw certificate collection of actual cut-free retained-principal sequent trees of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) over exactly \(X\), with pure endpoints. Context commas mean insertion, with the original principal retained. The corresponding cutful collection permits the established pure cut rule. A supplied native input is an actual intuitionistic natural-deduction tree with logical equality rules disabled. These are the same input restrictions as in assumption-free extraction. They do not assert removal of enabled equality rules.

Permit universal formulas and arbitrary implication inputs

The first-order Harrop formulas are given recursively, at every available bound scope. Atomic relation formulas and \(\bot\) are Harrop. Every native negation \(\neg D\) is Harrop. A conjunction is Harrop exactly when both factors are; an implication is Harrop exactly when its consequent is; and a universal formula is Harrop exactly when its body is. Thus the grammar may be displayed as

\[H::=P(\bar t)\mid\bot\mid\neg D\mid(H\land H)\mid(D\to H)\mid\forall xH,\]

where \(D\) is any equality-free formula in the indicated scope. A formula with outermost disjunction or existential quantifier is not Harrop. For unary relation symbols \(P,Q\), the formula \(\forall x((\exists yP(y))\to Q(x))\) is Harrop, although its implication input is not. This extends the propositional grammar by universal quantification; it does not require every subformula to satisfy the grammar.

Capture-free term maps preserve the grammar

For every scope substitution \(\sigma\), if \(H\) is Harrop, then \(H\langle\sigma\rangle\) is Harrop. Consequently free renaming, free term substitution and instantiation of a universal body preserve the Harrop condition.

Induct on the Harrop formula, allowing the source and target scopes to vary. Substitution changes only the terms of an atomic relation, and leaves falsum unchanged. A native negation remains a native negation, regardless of its transformed argument. For conjunction use the two induction hypotheses. For implication only the consequent needs the induction hypothesis; its antecedent remains an allowed arbitrary formula.

At \(\forall xC\), the protected lifted substitution acts on \(C\). It keeps the newly bound slot separate from all old images. The induction hypothesis makes that transformed body Harrop, and universal quantification preserves the condition. These cases cover the grammar. Equality-freeness is preserved by the established syntax laws as well. The argument substitutes terms into argument positions; it makes no claim about replacing predicate atoms by arbitrary formulas.

Require every allowed assumption to be Harrop

A finite context \(\Gamma\) is a Harrop context over \(X\) if every \(D\in\Gamma\) is Harrop. The condition concerns the whole allowed context, including unused assumptions. Images under the term maps in remain Harrop contexts.

Harrop does not mean consistent: \(\{\bot\}\) is Harrop and has a falsum-left certificate of \(\bot\). Define raw consistency over \(X\) by

\[\operatorname{Con}_X(\Gamma)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal R_X(\Gamma;\bot)=\varnothing.\]

The subscript records the actual available names. The extraction constructions will first work without assuming this consistency condition.

The permitted left continuations remain Harrop

Let \(\Gamma\) be Harrop. If \(C\land D\in\Gamma\), then \(\Gamma,C,D\) is Harrop. If \(C\to D\in\Gamma\), then \(\Gamma,D\) is Harrop. If \(\forall xC\in\Gamma\), then \(\Gamma,C(s)\) is Harrop for every supplied original-name term \(s\). No outer disjunction or existential formula belongs to \(\Gamma\).

The conjunction grammar supplies both \(C,D\), and the implication grammar supplies \(D\), without restricting \(C\). For a universal principal the body \(C\) is Harrop, so its instance \(C(s)\) is Harrop by . Each retained-principal rule keeps the original \(\Gamma\), whose old members already satisfy the condition. The two excluded outer forms fail the grammar by definition.

Therefore disjunction-left and existential-left cannot occur on a conclusion-following path whose current allowed context is Harrop. An antecedent subproof of implication-left need not satisfy such a restriction at all of its internal nodes; the constructions below retain that supplied subproof instead of recursively extracting from it.

Record exact factor proofs and the possible contradiction

For a finite Harrop \(\Gamma\), define the disjunct packet and witness packet by

\[\mathsf D_\Gamma(A,B)=\mathcal R_X(\Gamma;A)\sqcup\mathcal R_X(\Gamma;B),\]
\[\mathsf W_\Gamma(B)=\{(t,q):t\in\operatorname{Term}_L(X),\ q\in\mathcal R_X(\Gamma;B(t))\}.\]

The two tags of a disjunct packet retain its chosen side. Define the witness-or-contradiction packet to be

\[\mathsf{Out}_\Gamma(B)=\mathsf W_\Gamma(B)\sqcup\mathcal R_X(\Gamma;\bot).\]

Write its tags as \(\operatorname{wit}(t,q)\) and \(\operatorname{contra}(r)\). The certificate in every component has exactly the original allowed \(\Gamma\). These are collections of proof data, not merely assertions that a suitable deduction exists.

Rebuild the same left rule on each returned component

Each conjunction-left, implication-left or universal-left ending has a specified continuation lift \(\Lambda\), taking a proof of any conclusion \(D\) in its continuation context to a proof of \(D\) in \(\Gamma\). For implication-left fix its original antecedent tree \(u\); for universal-left fix its original instantiation term \(s\). These lifts act on packets without changing their tags or stored witness terms.

For conjunction-left, apply that very rule using the same membership \(C\land D\in\Gamma\). For implication-left, use the same membership \(C\to D\in\Gamma\), the same supplied \(u:\Gamma\Rightarrow C\), and the returned continuation proof. For universal-left, use the same membership \(\forall xC\in\Gamma\) and the same \(s\), rebuilding the rule above its returned \(\Gamma,C(s)\) proof.

On a disjunct packet apply this lift to the selected certificate and retain its side. On \(\operatorname{wit}(t,q)\) return \(\operatorname{wit}(t,\Lambda(q))\); on \(\operatorname{contra}(r)\) return \(\operatorname{contra}(\Lambda(r))\). The rules permit any conclusion, so they apply to \(A\), \(B\), \(B(t)\) and \(\bot\) alike. Thus the output proof has the required unchanged context in each case.

Extract a definite side in the same Harrop context

For every finite Harrop \(\Gamma\) and supplied raw cut-free tree \(p:\Gamma\Rightarrow A\lor B\), there is a specified structural construction

\[\mathsf E_\Gamma(p)\in\mathsf D_\Gamma(A,B).\]

It retains the selected child or rebuilds the original left-rule continuation; it does not choose a new proof solely from derivability.

There are seven possible final-rule shapes. A left disjunction introduction with child \(q\) returns the left tag of that exact \(q\); a right introduction returns the right tag of its exact child. Falsum-left returns the left tag of falsum-left concluding \(A\), with the same falsum membership. Negation-left returns the left tag of negation-left concluding \(A\), with its same principal membership and antecedent tree.

At conjunction-left, implication-left and universal-left, recursively extract from the supplied continuation, whose context is Harrop by , then apply the corresponding packet lift from . In symbols, with that same fixed lift,

\[\mathsf E_\Gamma(\Lambda(q))=\Lambda_*\bigl(\mathsf E_{\Gamma'}(q)\bigr).\]

Here \(\Gamma'\) is the actual continuation context; implication's other child and universal's term remain fixed. Atomic identity and the other right introductions have a different outer conclusion. Disjunction-left and existential-left are excluded by the Harrop principal condition. This exhausts the primitive calculus. Every recursive call uses a proper continuation subtree, so the finite tree recursion terminates. The packet type and the exact selected or reconstructed tree are preserved in all seven cases.

Read a term or an actual contradiction from an existential proof

For every finite Harrop \(\Gamma\), equality-free body \(B\), and supplied raw cut-free tree \(p:\Gamma\Rightarrow\exists xB\), there is a specified construction

\[\mathsf O_\Gamma(p)\in\mathsf{Out}_\Gamma(B).\]

At existential-right it returns the exact stored original-name term and its exact child proof.

Recurse along the conclusion's left-rule continuation. If the tree ends in existential-right at \(t\), with child \(q:\Gamma\Rightarrow B(t)\), return \(\operatorname{wit}(t,q)\). If it ends in falsum-left, return \(\operatorname{contra}(r)\), where \(r\) is falsum-left concluding \(\bot\) with that same membership. If it ends in negation-left, return the contradiction tag of negation-left concluding \(\bot\), retaining its same principal and supplied antecedent proof.

For conjunction-left, implication-left and universal-left, recurse only on the supplied continuation and use the lifts in . A witness branch keeps its term and rebuilds its instance proof; a contradiction branch rebuilds its falsum proof. In each case the original principal is retained, and implication's other child or universal's instantiation term is unchanged.

These are all six possible endings. Atomic identity and every other right rule have the wrong outer conclusion, while the two excluded left principals cannot be in a Harrop context. The same strict-subtree termination argument applies. No eigenparameter rule is followed on this continuation, so every returned term remains in the original \(\operatorname{Term}_L(X)\), even when \(B\) itself contains further quantifiers.

Discard a contradiction or fill it with a supplied term

For the input \(p\) of , either of two extra hypotheses yields an actual witness packet: raw consistency \(\operatorname{Con}_X(\Gamma)\), or a supplied term \(r\in\operatorname{Term}_L(X)\).

Apply \(\mathsf O_\Gamma\) to that exact \(p\). Under consistency, its contradiction tag is impossible; return its witness tag. This specifies the consistency-based operation without choosing an unrelated certificate.

Alternatively define \(\mathsf W_r(p)\) to retain every witness tag unchanged, and to send a contradiction tag \(\operatorname{contra}(q)\) to

\[(r,\operatorname{explode}_{B(r)}(q)).\]

The established syntactic explosion operation transforms the supplied \(q:\Gamma\Rightarrow\bot\) into a cut-free proof of \(B(r)\) with exactly the same \(\Gamma\). Equality-freeness of the instance follows from that of \(B\). Thus this second operation needs no consistency hypothesis, but it really uses an available original term.

Apply the constructions to the actual normalized input

Disjunct and witness-or-contradiction extraction extend to supplied pure raw cutful trees and equality-disabled intuitionistic raw native trees. Their contexts and original names stay unchanged.

Let \(\mathsf{elim}\) be the actual cut-removal construction. Define the cut-input operations literally by

\[\mathsf E^{\mathrm{cut}}(p)=\mathsf E(\mathsf{elim}(p)),\qquad\mathsf O^{\mathrm{cut}}(p)=\mathsf O(\mathsf{elim}(p)).\]

The normalized tree has the same context and conclusion. For a native input, let \(\mathsf N(p)\) be the established native-to-cut-free operation: it purifies intermediate formulas, translates the actual tree to cutful sequents, then removes its cuts. Set \(\mathsf E^{\mathrm{nat}}(p)=\mathsf E(\mathsf N(p))\) and \(\mathsf O^{\mathrm{nat}}(p)=\mathsf O(\mathsf N(p))\).

Apply the consistency or supplied-term operation after the same normalization when a witness alone is required. Reading any returned sequent certificate back as natural deduction gives the corresponding actual factor, instance or contradiction deduction. Existence of an input therefore gives existence of these outputs. This Prop-valued consequence makes no additional choice of definite proof data; the specified constructions are the operations on supplied trees.

Keep the reserved context when extracting a public disjunct

Put \(Y=X\sqcup U\), where \(U=\{u_0,u_1,\ldots\}\), and let \(\iota:X\to Y\) be the left inclusion. Write \(\widehat A=A^\iota\) and \(\widehat\Gamma=\{D^\iota:D\in\Gamma\}\). A supplied public certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\lor B\), with finite Harrop \(\Gamma\), gives a public certificate of one original disjunct, retaining that same allowed \(\Gamma\).

By the public certificate definition, the supplied record is a raw proof over \(Y\) with context \(\widehat\Gamma\) and conclusion \((A\lor B)^\iota=\widehat A\lor\widehat B\). Term-map closure makes \(\widehat\Gamma\) Harrop. Apply \(\mathsf E_{\widehat\Gamma}\) to that very record. Its selected raw certificate of \(\widehat A\) or \(\widehat B\), with context \(\widehat\Gamma\), is exactly the corresponding public certificate over \(X\). Reinterpret that component without changing its tree or tag.

For a public cut or native input, first apply its actual public cut-free normalization and then this operation. Thus, with \(\vdash_{\rm pub}\) denoting existence of a public certificate,

\[\Gamma\vdash_{\rm pub}A\lor B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Gamma\vdash_{\rm pub}A\ \text{or}\ \Gamma\vdash_{\rm pub}B.\]

The forward direction follows the returned tag; the reverse direction is the matching disjunction introduction. The established pure public/native correspondence gives the same factor-existence assertion for equality-disabled intuitionistic natural deduction.

A public proof always has an available reserved witness term

For a supplied public certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow\exists xB\), with finite Harrop \(\Gamma\), one can return an actual term \(t\in\operatorname{Term}_L(Y)\) and a raw proof over \(Y\) of \(\widehat\Gamma\Rightarrow\widehat B(t)\). The term may contain reserved names. The assertion also holds for supplied public cutful and equality-disabled native inputs with pure endpoints.

Regard the supplied public record as its actual reserved raw tree, transporting only the outer renaming identity for the existential. Apply the supplied-term operation \(\mathsf W_{u_0}\) to this tree, with the variable term \(u_0\) in \(Y\). A witness branch retains its exact stored term and reconstructed instance proof; a contradiction branch uses \(u_0\) and explosion of its actual \(\widehat\Gamma\Rightarrow\bot\) proof. Thus an available reserved term makes the operation total even without consistency.

For cutful or native public inputs, first use their established actual public normalization, then apply this same reserved operation. If one instead assumes consistency to exclude the contradiction branch, the required hypothesis is \(\operatorname{Con}_Y(\widehat\Gamma)\), in exactly the name universe of the normalized raw tree. Raw consistency over \(X\) is a different hypothesis. Existence of a public input yields existence of the stated reserved term and instance certificate; it does not delete any reserved variable from that term.

Substitute through the proof while fixing every original assumption

Suppose an original term \(r\in\operatorname{Term}_L(X)\) is supplied. From a reserved witness \((t,q)\) in , one obtains the original-name packet

\[(t[\sigma_r],q[\sigma_r])\in\mathsf W_\Gamma(B),\]

where the reserve-removal substitution \(\sigma_r\) fixes each original name as a variable and sends every \(u_i\) to \(r\). The same substitution takes any reserved contradiction proof to an original-name contradiction proof.

Apply the established actual proof substitution to \(q\), including all of its allowed assumptions. Capture-free substitution and renaming give

\[\widehat\Gamma[\sigma_r]=\Gamma,\qquad(\widehat B(t))[\sigma_r]=B(t[\sigma_r]).\]

The first identity holds because every original free name is fixed, so every original assumption is fixed, whether or not it is closed. The second is the instance-substitution identity, with all internal binders protected. Transporting these two identities yields the asserted certificate in exactly \(\Gamma\). The term substitution also descends through every function argument.

For a reserved falsum proof the same context identity applies and falsum is unchanged. Consequently, when \(r\) is available, \(\operatorname{Con}_X(\Gamma)\) implies \(\operatorname{Con}_Y(\widehat\Gamma)\): a contradiction in the latter context would substitute to one in the former. Without \(r\), this particular substitution has no original term to assign to the reserve.

Retain a universal and implication spine while extracting the given term

Let \(P,Q\) be unary relation symbols and let \(c\in\operatorname{Term}_L(X)\) be supplied. Put \(U=\forall x(P(x)\to Q(x))\), \(\Gamma=\{U,P(c)\}\), and \(\Delta=\Gamma,P(c)\to Q(c)\). This is a Harrop context. There is a specified raw proof \(p:\Gamma\Rightarrow\exists xQ(x)\) whose extracted packet is exactly

\[\mathsf O_\Gamma(p)=\operatorname{wit}\bigl(c,\forall L_c(\to L(u,v))\bigr),\]

where \(u:\Delta\Rightarrow P(c)\) and \(v:\Delta,Q(c)\Rightarrow Q(c)\) are the atomic identity leaves.

Existential-right at \(c\) applied to \(v\) gives a continuation of \(\Delta,Q(c)\Rightarrow\exists xQ(x)\). Apply implication-left with the actual \(u\) and principal \(P(c)\to Q(c)\in\Delta\), then universal-left at the same \(c\) with principal \(U\in\Gamma\). Thus the supplied input is

\[p=\forall L_c\bigl(\to L(u,\exists R_c(v))\bigr).\]

Extraction reaches that existential-right and returns its exact \(c,v\). The implication lift keeps the identical \(u\), and the universal lift keeps its identical instantiation term \(c\). Rebuilding them gives precisely the displayed instance proof of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow Q(c)\), including its same retained principals. No existential assumption or protected witness name was used.

An inconsistent Harrop context need not supply any original term

In the empty language and with \(X=\varnothing\), put \(\Gamma=\{\bot\}\). This Harrop context has a raw cut-free proof of \(\exists x\bot\), but \(\mathsf W_\Gamma(\bot)\) is empty.

Falsum-left concludes \(\exists x\bot\) directly from its allowed falsum assumption; it needs no term. There is no term over the empty language and empty name set: neither the variable case nor the function-symbol case can occur. Hence there cannot be a pair in \(\mathsf W_\Gamma(\bot)\).

The witness-or-contradiction construction applied to this particular falsum-left input returns the contradiction tag of falsum-left concluding \(\bot\). Thus its total output remains well-defined. Raw consistency fails in this example, and no original default term is available. An unconditional original-term existence property for arbitrary inconsistent Harrop contexts would therefore be false.

Extract a witness before discharging a Harrop premise

Let every formula in the finite allowed context \(\Gamma\) be Harrop, let \(H\) be Harrop, and let \(B(x)\) be an equality-free one-slot body. The bound name \(x\) is fresh for \(H\) and for every formula in \(\Gamma\). Take an actual equality-disabled raw intuitionistic deduction over the original names \(X\) of

\[\Gamma\vdash_X H\to\exists xB(x).\]

If either an original-name term \(r\) is supplied or \(\operatorname{Con}_X(\Gamma,H)\) holds, the following construction returns an original-name term \(t\), a deduction of \(\Gamma\vdash_X H\to B(t)\), and hence a deduction of

\[\Gamma\vdash_X\exists x\,(H\to B(x)).\]

Weaken the supplied implication into \(\Gamma,H\), assume \(H\), and apply implication elimination. This gives a specified deduction of \(\exists xB(x)\) under \(\Gamma,H\), whose whole allowed context is Harrop. Apply native witness extraction to this exact deduction after its specified cut-free normalization. With the supplied original term, use the default-term operation. With consistency, use the consistency operation for the enlarged context \(\Gamma,H\). Either returns a term \(t\in\operatorname{Term}_L(X)\) and an actual instance deduction under that same enlarged context.

Read its returned sequent proof back as native deduction and discharge \(H\). The result proves \(H\to B(t)\) under \(\Gamma\). Introduce \(t\) into the body \(H\to B(x)\). Because \(H\) has no occurrence of the new bound slot, its instance at \(t\) is literally \(H\to B(t)\). Thus the last operation yields the displayed existential formula. All operations retain the supplied proof or its specified normalization; no unrelated witness is chosen from mere derivability. A supplied raw cutful or cut-free implication proof is handled by its existing native translation before this construction.

The public reserve supplies the term needed by independence of premise

Under the same Harrop and equality-free hypotheses, a supplied public intuitionistic deduction of \(\Gamma\vdash_{\rm pub}H\to\exists xB(x)\) transforms into a public deduction of

\[\Gamma\vdash_{\rm pub}\exists x\,(H\to B(x)).\]

This public transformation requires no consistency hypothesis or original-name default term.

Use the actual reserved universe \(Y=X\sqcup\mathbb N\) and the renamed context \(\widehat\Gamma\). Add \(\widehat H\) and apply the supplied implication there. Harrop closure holds in this renamed enlarged context. The preceding raw construction now has the literal term \(u_0\in\operatorname{Term}_L(Y)\) available as its default. It returns a term over \(Y\), discharges \(\widehat H\), and introduces the existential in the same reserved universe. Renaming commutes with the body \(H\to B(x)\), so the resulting raw deduction over \(Y\) is exactly a public deduction of the displayed original formula.

A public cut-free or cutful sequent input may first be read as public native deduction; translating the final deduction back yields the corresponding pure public sequent certificate. The extracted term itself can contain reserved names. If an original term \(r\) is supplied, the specified reserve substitution fixes every original assumption and replaces each reserved name by \(r\). It therefore also gives an original-name instance proof and existential conclusion. Without that extra term, the public conclusion alone does not promise an original-name witness.

Is consistency of the old context enough for the raw construction?

Can \(\operatorname{Con}_X(\Gamma,H)\) be replaced by \(\operatorname{Con}_X(\Gamma)\), even when no original term exists?

Show answerHide answer

No. Take the empty language, \(X=\varnothing\), \(\Gamma=\varnothing\), \(H=\bot\), and \(B(x)=\bot\). The empty context is raw consistent. Native falsum elimination under the temporary assumption \(\bot\), followed by implication introduction, proves \(\bot\to\exists x\bot\); this uses no witness term.

The proposed conclusion is \(\exists x(\bot\to\bot)\). There is no raw cut-free proof from the empty context: every left rule lacks a principal, atomic identity has the wrong conclusion, and existential-right would require an original-name term, of which there is none. Raw cut removal and the established equality-disabled raw/native correspondence rule out cutful and native proofs as well. Here the enlarged context \(\{\bot\}\) is inconsistent, exactly as the preceding extraction test detects. This is the same term-availability boundary that the default-term and public-reserve versions address.

Must an implication input also be Harrop?

Can a Harrop assumption have a disjunction or an existential formula inside the input of an implication?

Show answerHide answer

Yes. For atomic \(R\), the formula \(((\exists xP(x))\lor Q)\to R\) is Harrop: only its consequent is tested by the implication clause. Its input is not Harrop. Universal quantification and conjunction with other Harrop formulas can surround such an implication without changing this fact.

At implication-left the extractor retains the supplied proof of that whole input. It recurses only on the conclusion-following continuation, where the added assumption is \(R\), which is Harrop. Thus it never needs to extract a witness or disjunct from the input proof. Requiring every subformula to be Harrop would be a strictly stronger restriction than the grammar used here.

Term-map closure traverses atomic argument terms without changing their predicate or any logical constructor. The propositional substitution counterexample concerns replacing an atom by a whole formula, which is a different operation.

Does raw consistency automatically survive adding the reserve?

Without an original term, must \(\operatorname{Con}_X(\Gamma)\) imply \(\operatorname{Con}_Y(\widehat\Gamma)\)?

Show answerHide answer

No. Use the empty language, \(X=\varnothing\), and \(\Gamma=\{\forall x\bot\}\). This context is Harrop. It has no raw proof of falsum over \(X\). In a cut-free tree concluding falsum, atomic identity and right rules have the wrong conclusion. Falsum-left and the propositional or existential left rules have no matching principal in this singleton context. Universal-left would require a term over the empty original scope, and there is none. These cases exhaust the raw calculus, proving \(\operatorname{Con}_X(\Gamma)\).

Over the reserved scope, however, instantiate that same universal assumption at \(u_0\). Its continuation allows \(\bot\), so falsum-left followed by universal-left gives \(\widehat\Gamma\Rightarrow\bot\). Therefore \(\operatorname{Con}_Y(\widehat\Gamma)\) fails. This is the same availability of reserved terms that underlies the constant-free public/raw boundary.

The consistency version of extraction must consequently use the actual context and name universe of its input. When an original term is supplied, provides the missing proof-substitution bridge and rules out this discrepancy.
