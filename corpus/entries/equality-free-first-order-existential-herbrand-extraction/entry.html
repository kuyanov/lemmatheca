Put every quantifier in one leading existential block

Fix a language \(L\), free names \(X\), and the scoped equality-free signed formulas. A quantifier-free matrix contains only signed relation atoms, \(\bot,\top,\land,\lor\). For a matrix \(A\) with available slots \(z_0,\ldots,z_{n-1}\), write \(\exists^n A\) for \(\exists z_0\cdots\exists z_{n-1}A\). The outermost variable is slot zero: close the newest last slot first, then continue outward. For \(n=0\), this is \(A\). The same notation can close the last \(n\) slots while leaving any earlier slots available.

A weak existential formula is such a leading existential block with a quantifier-free matrix. It is proper when its block has positive length. Free names in \(X\) may occur in the matrix, and bound slots may be unused. A supplied term tuple \(\bar t\) over \(X\) gives the matrix instance \(A[\bar t]\) by simultaneous capture-free filling of those slots.

This fragment excludes a quantifier inside a conjunction or disjunction and excludes universal quantifiers. Removing an outer existential at an actual term leaves another weak existential formula. Filling slots preserves quantifier-freeness and the order of every remaining slot, by the shared scope-substitution laws.

Keep the complete term tuple in each propositional atom

Use the relation atoms over \(X\): an atom records its arity, relation symbol, and complete tuple of actual terms over \(X\). A signed propositional reading \(A^{\mathrm p}\) of a complete quantifier-free matrix preserves \(\bot,\top,\land,\lor\), and replaces \(R(\bar s)\) and its negative literal by the positive and negative versions of that same atom.

This is the existing propositional NNF language, with its five primitive proof rules. A propositional factor certificate is an actual finite tree in that calculus, with concluding alternatives the indicated matrix readings. Reading a negative literal gives primitive propositional negation of its atom; reading \(\top\) gives \(\neg\bot\). Different term trees remain different atom names. For a finite matrix context \(\Pi\), its read context \(\Pi^{\mathrm p}\) is the image \(\{C^{\mathrm p}:C\in\Pi\}\).

Translate quantifier-free proof trees in both directions

A supplied first-order cut-free tree with a quantifier-free endsequent gives a propositional factor certificate of its readings. Conversely a supplied propositional tree on these relation atoms gives a first-order cut-free tree of the corresponding quantifier-free matrices. These are operations on the supplied trees, with the exact concluding alternatives preserved.

In the forward direction recurse on the first-order tree, retaining the assertion that every formula of its concluding context is quantifier-free. Atomic identity and truth become the corresponding propositional leaves. At conjunction, both components are quantifier-free; recursively translate its two children and reapply conjunction. Disjunction and bottom are treated by their respective one-premise rules. A quantified rule cannot be last, because its principal quantified formula belongs to the concluding context. This also justifies the quantifier-free assertion at every recursive call.

For the reverse direction replace each propositional atom by its recorded relation application and copy the five constructors. A positive and negative occurrence still use the identical tuple. Formula induction shows that reading and reinserting a quantifier-free matrix returns its literal tree; the canonical identification of \(X\sqcup\varnothing\) with \(X\) removes the empty slot tag. In either direction a larger allowed context is obtained by adding its alternatives at every node, as in actual propositional weakening. No validity test supplies either proof tree.

Record each existential choice as data

A witness record for a weak existential formula \(F\) is built by two clauses. If \(F\) is quantifier-free, its terminal record has value \(F\). If \(F=\exists xB\), a term \(t\) over the current free names and a witness record for \(B(t)\) give a record for \(F\), with the same terminal value. Thus a record stores actual terms in the order in which the outer existential quantifiers are consumed. Its value, written \(v(w)\), is the terminal quantifier-free formula.

For a finite family \(\Sigma\), an origin-labelled witness list is a finite list of records, each carrying a specified origin \(F\in\Sigma\). Repetitions are allowed. Write \(V(\mathcal W)\) for the finite set of their values. Keeping a list of records preserves the chosen terms even when equal values merge in this set.

A record for a fixed block gives its literal matrix instance

For a quantifier-free \(A\) with \(n\) slots, every witness record \(w\) for \(\exists^n A\) specifies a tuple \(\bar t_w\in\operatorname{Term}(X)^n\), and \(v(w)=A[\bar t_w]\). Every supplied tuple conversely gives such a record. This includes the unique empty tuple when \(n=0\).

Induct on \(n\), keeping all free names and matrices arbitrary. At zero the formula is quantifier-free, so the record is terminal and its value is \(A\). At a positive length the formula has outer constructor \(\exists\), so the record begins with a term \(t_0\) and a record for its instantiated body. That body has the remaining existential prefix and a quantifier-free matrix with slot zero filled by \(t_0\). Apply the induction hypothesis to obtain \(t_1,\ldots,t_{n-1}\).

The scope-composition identities identify these successive fillings with the single simultaneous filling \(A[t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1}]\): old slots retain their relative order, and substitution under each binder protects its newest last slot. This proves the value identity. Reading the same recursion backwards constructs a record from a supplied tuple. In particular, later terms in this record are already terms over \(X\), rather than terms with an unfilled earlier witness slot.

Separate fixed matrix alternatives from existential offers

Let \(\Pi\) be a finite quantifier-free context and \(\Sigma\) a finite context of proper weak existential formulas. An extraction result for these contexts consists of an origin-labelled witness list \(\mathcal W\) for \(\Sigma\), together with a propositional factor certificate of

\[\vdash\{C^{\mathrm p}:C\in\Pi\}\ \cup\ \{v(w)^{\mathrm p}:w\in\mathcal W\}.\]

The recursive input is an actual first-order cut-free tree of a context \(\Xi\subseteq\Pi\cup\Sigma\). The larger contexts remain arbitrary during recursion. In particular, a retained existential formula can belong to \(\Sigma\) before and after one of its instances is used. Temporary quantifier-free components belong to \(\Pi\); they are not mislabelled as witness values of a compound matrix.

Combine the factor trees at the five propositional rules

Assume extraction results are available for the strict children of an input tree under the invariant above. Its atomic-identity, truth, conjunction, disjunction and bottom cases then give an extraction result at the root.

An identity leaf's two literals must lie in \(\Pi\), since every member of \(\Sigma\) has outer constructor \(\exists\). Take the empty witness list and the propositional identity leaf in \(\Pi^{\mathrm p}\). The truth case is identical, using its truth leaf.

For a conjunction principal \(B\land C\), that principal lies in \(\Pi\), and its children use \(\Pi,B\) and \(\Pi,C\) with the same \(\Sigma\). Obtain lists \(\mathcal W_B,\mathcal W_C\). Concatenate them. Weaken each supplied factor tree by the other list's values, so both have common side context \(\Pi^{\mathrm p}\cup V(\mathcal W_B\mathbin{+}\mathcal W_C)^{\mathrm p}\). Their extra alternatives are respectively \(B^{\mathrm p}\) and \(C^{\mathrm p}\). Propositional conjunction introduction gives the required factor tree, since its principal already belongs to \(\Pi^{\mathrm p}\).

At a disjunction principal \(B\lor C\), put both components into the child's fixed matrix context, keep its witness list, and apply propositional disjunction introduction to its factor tree. Again the introduced principal already lies in \(\Pi\). At bottom introduction the child's extraction with the same allowed \(\Pi,\Sigma\) already supplies the result, including any unused bottom alternative. Membership inclusions justify all these calls even when principals or components coincide with old context members. Each construction uses only the five actual propositional rules and their proved context enlargement.

Move an actual instance into its parent witness record

The existential-rule case also preserves the extraction invariant. It uses the actual supplied witness term, without choosing a term from semantic truth.

Suppose the root introduces \(E=\exists xB\) at term \(t\), with child offering \(B(t)\). Since \(E\) is not quantifier-free, its membership in the allowed endcontext puts it in \(\Sigma\). If \(B(t)\) is still proper weak existential, run extraction on the child with allowed existential context \(\Sigma,B(t)\) and unchanged \(\Pi\). For each returned record whose origin already lies in \(\Sigma\), keep that label and record. For any other origin, its formula must be \(B(t)\); prepend the stored term \(t\), and relabel its origin as \(E\). All terminal values remain identical, so the factor tree remains the same supplied tree.

If \(B(t)\) is quantifier-free, instead extract the child with fixed context \(\Pi,B(t)\) and unchanged \(\Sigma\). Append one new record for \(E\): choose its first term \(t\) and then the terminal record for \(B(t)\). Its value is exactly that additional matrix alternative, so the child's factor tree has precisely the required new concluding set. Appending is allowed even if this value was already present or was unused.

These two cases exhaust a weak existential body after its outer instance. An original retained copy of \(E\) remains an allowed origin in either case. Formula membership can coincide, but the records retain their specified origins, and concatenation never requires identifying two different witness choices.

Obtain a nonempty finite list of tuples

Let \(A\) be a quantifier-free matrix with \(n\) slots. From any supplied cut-free or cutful raw certificate of \(\{\exists^n A\}\), one can construct a finite nonempty list of term tuples \(\bar t_1,\ldots,\bar t_m\) over the same free names, and a propositional factor certificate of

\[\vdash\{A[\bar t_1]^{\mathrm p},\ldots,A[\bar t_m]^{\mathrm p}\}.\]

First use the actual cut-removal operation if needed. An equality-disabled native classical proof of the full reading also supplies this signed singleton by the exact native correspondence: positive normalization of a signed reading returns its original tree. For \(n>0\), apply with \(\Pi=\varnothing\) and \(\Sigma=\{\exists^n A\}\). Each output origin is that exact block, so converts each record into its tuple and identifies its value with the displayed matrix instance.

If the list were empty, its factor certificate would prove the empty propositional sequent, contrary to primitive-rule consistency. Thus the actual list is nonempty. For \(n=0\), use the single empty tuple and translate the supplied quantifier-free tree by . This handles the zero-length prefix without pretending that there is a positive existential offer.

Turn the factor alternatives into one proved disjunction

Define the list disjunction by \(D([])=\bot\) and \(D(C::\mathcal L)=C\lor D(\mathcal L)\). An actual propositional proof of \(\Gamma\cup\{C:C\in\mathcal L\}\) gives one of \(\Gamma,D(\mathcal L)\). In particular the extracted list has a singleton-sequent proof of its matrix disjunction.

Induct on the list with arbitrary \(\Gamma\). At the empty list apply bottom introduction to the supplied proof of \(\Gamma\). At \(C::\mathcal L\), apply the induction hypothesis with side context \(\Gamma,C\). It returns a proof of \(\Gamma,C,D(\mathcal L)\). One disjunction introduction produces \(\Gamma,C\lor D(\mathcal L)\), as required. Repeated formulas cause no problem because each step concerns this finite set of alternatives. The final harmless \(\bot\) is part of the explicit folding convention.

Rebuild the quantified conclusion from a factor proof

Conversely, for a quantifier-free \(A\), a nonempty list of supplied tuples and a factor certificate of their matrix instances give an actual raw cut-free certificate of \(\{\exists^n A\}\). Thus the two kinds of supplied certificates admit transformations in both directions, with no change of free-name language.

Translate the factor tree back to its actual first-order matrix alternatives by . A witness record \(w\) for \(F\) transforms a proof of \(\Gamma,v(w)\) into one of \(\Gamma,F\): its terminal clause changes nothing; its step clause first applies the child transformation and then the genuine existential rule at the stored term. This is recursion on the record.

Apply this operation to the first list member while retaining all remaining matrix alternatives. Then apply it to the second, retaining the first resulting existential and all remaining matrices; continue through the finite list. Every introduced formula is the identical \(\exists^n A\). Its repeated insertions merge, leaving the singleton concluding context because the list is nonempty. A zero-length prefix uses terminal records and the same finite-set calculation. No cut, completeness theorem or semantic selection of a successful disjunct is required.

Open leading universals as arbitrary parameters

Suppose the supplied raw certificate concludes a singleton \(\forall z_0\cdots\forall z_{k-1}\exists^n A\), where the remaining matrix is quantifier-free. The matrix has these \(k+n\) available slots in prefix order. Rename its original free names into \(X\sqcup\{a_0,\ldots,a_{k-1}\}\). There are a nonempty finite tuple list of terms over these names and a factor certificate of the matrix instances with \(z_i=a_i\). Conversely such a factor certificate reconstructs the universally closed conclusion.

Eliminate cuts first. Apply the actual universal-instance inversion at \(a_0\), then at \(a_1\), and so on. That operation removes retained copies of its universal principal, so it really produces the required singleton instance at each step. Merely inspecting a last universal introduction would not justify discarding those copies. After all \(k\) operations the endformula is the weak existential block with these arbitrary parameters. Keep the public reserve visible in the extracted terms

The extraction and reconstruction theorems hold for public certificates with the same countable reserve. Their extracted terms may use original names, opened universal parameters and reserve names. The factor certificate records all of them.

Apply the raw constructions to the underlying tree over \(X\sqcup U\). For a universal prefix, enlarge this set by its finitely many protected names. A permutation of the disjoint tags identifies this set with \((X\sqcup\{a_0,\ldots,a_{k-1}\})\sqcup U\), so the preceding tuple statement has precisely those allowed terms.

In reconstruction use the inverse permutation, keeping the reserve among the old names and universally closing only the protected \(a_i\). Existential introduction removes the displayed witness terms from the conclusion; universal introduction removes the opened parameters. The final context is the reserved copy of the original formula, with no reserve name occurring in that formula. These are actual renamings of supplied trees; they do not erase reserve variables from their internal witnesses.

Remove the reserve when an original term is supplied

If a term \(r\) over the original names and opened universal parameters is supplied, the extracted tuple list and factor tree can be transformed so that none of their terms uses a reserve name. In particular this is possible when an opened universal parameter is available, or when the original language has a ground term.

Substitute each original or opened name by itself and every reserve name by \(r\). Apply this substitution to every term stored in every tuple. In the factor tree rename each relation atom by substituting in its whole argument tuple. Identity still uses an identical atom with opposite signs, and the other four propositional rules reapply; context images and inserted formulas commute. This gives an actual factor tree even when several atom names collapse.

Formula induction identifies its end alternatives with the original matrix instances at the substituted tuples: substitution changes only the actual terms at atoms and commutes with the matrix's binary connectives and constants. The matrix itself contains no reserve name. Finiteness and the list's positive length are unchanged. One may take \(r=a_0\) when an opened universal parameter exists. With no displayed parameters, a supplied ground term gives ground tuples. Without such a term, this argument supplies no original-language ground witnesses.

Two complementary intermediate atoms give a factor proof

Take one constant \(c\), one unary function \(f\), and one unary relation \(P\), with no other symbols. Put \(M(x)=\neg P(x)\lor P(fx)\). There is an actual factor certificate for the two instances \(M(c),M(fc)\), and hence an equality-free raw proof of \(\exists xM(x)\).

Start with the propositional identity at the complete atom \(P(fc)\), and weaken by \(\neg P(c)\) and \(P(f(fc))\). Its alternatives are

\[\neg P(c),\ P(fc),\ \neg P(fc),\ P(f(fc)).\]

Use disjunction introduction on the first two alternatives, then on the last two. The result is exactly the factor sequent \(M(c),M(fc)\). Reconstruction first introduces \(\exists xM(x)\) at \(c\), retaining \(M(fc)\), and then introduces it at \(fc\), retaining the already present existential. Its final duplicate is identified by finite-set insertion. Both witness terms and the three-rule propositional core are explicit.

No. Every ground term in this language is \(f^j(c)\) for some finite \(j\): a term starts with \(c\), or with \(f\) applied to a smaller term. Consequently \(t\) and \(ft\) are different term trees. Give the complete atom \(P(t)\) value true and \(P(ft)\) value false; assign all other atoms arbitrarily. This valuation makes \(\neg P(t)\lor P(ft)\) false, so propositional soundness excludes its singleton factor certificate.

The two-instance factor proof is valid because it offers complementary occurrences of the shared intermediate atom \(P(fc)\). A classical existential theorem can therefore require a finite disjunction of instances even though every individual ground instance can fail under some valuation. The proof does not select a single disjunct valid under every valuation.
