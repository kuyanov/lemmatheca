Allow one conclusion in the full equality-free language

Fix a language \(L\), free parameter set \(X\), and the native full formula trees. A pure context is a finite set \(\Gamma\) of equality-free complete formulas. A pure sequent \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) has such a context and one equality-free complete conclusion \(A\). Write \(\Gamma,B\) for insertion of \(B\). Assumptions can be reused and unused assumptions are permitted.

The connectives \(\bot,\neg,\land,\lor,\to,\forall,\exists\) remain primitive constructive constructors. Equality atoms and equality rules are excluded from this calculus. A raw cut-free certificate is an actual finite tree generated by the rules below. Its parameter set may grow in an eigenparameter branch; the displayed conclusion remains a formula over the original set. There is no cut or weakening constructor.

Retain the principal assumptions in constructive left rules

The propositional rules are the native cut-free rules with relation atoms in place of propositional atoms. Atomic identity gives \(\Gamma\Rightarrow R(\bar t)\) when that atom belongs to \(\Gamma\); falsum-left gives \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\) when \(\bot\in\Gamma\). Conjunction-right combines proofs of both factors. Either disjunction-right rule introduces its chosen disjunct. Implication-right and negation-right discharge a temporary antecedent, with respective premises \(\Gamma,A\Rightarrow B\) and \(\Gamma,A\Rightarrow\bot\).

For left rules the principal formula remains among the allowed assumptions. If \(A\land B\in\Gamma\), a premise \(\Gamma,A,B\Rightarrow C\) gives \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\). If \(A\lor B\in\Gamma\), use both premises \(\Gamma,A\Rightarrow C\) and \(\Gamma,B\Rightarrow C\). The implication and native-negation rules are

\[\frac{\Gamma\Rightarrow A\quad\Gamma,B\Rightarrow C}{\Gamma\Rightarrow C}\ (A\to B\in\Gamma),\qquad \frac{\Gamma\Rightarrow A}{\Gamma\Rightarrow C}\ (\neg A\in\Gamma).\]

The latter rule obtains falsum from the principal negation and its antecedent, then permits any conclusion. No classical contradiction rule is present. In particular, a proof under \(\neg A\) ending in falsum does not by itself license conclusion \(A\).

Use a supplied term or a protected fresh parameter

Let \(B\) be an equality-free one-slot body, \(t\) a term over \(X\), and \(B(t)\) its capture-free instance. The term rules are

\[\frac{\Gamma,B(t)\Rightarrow C}{\Gamma\Rightarrow C}\ (\forall xB\in\Gamma),\qquad \frac{\Gamma\Rightarrow B(t)}{\Gamma\Rightarrow\exists xB}.\]

For the other rules use \(X\sqcup\{e\}\). An upward arrow renames old free names into the left component. The opened body \(\operatorname{open}(B)\) uses the right-tagged parameter \(e\) for its available slot. The protected rules are

\[\frac{\Gamma^\uparrow\Rightarrow\operatorname{open}(B)}{\Gamma\Rightarrow\forall xB},\qquad \frac{\Gamma^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(B)\Rightarrow C^\uparrow}{\Gamma\Rightarrow C}\ (\exists xB\in\Gamma).\]

Thus \(e\) occurs in neither the lifted ambient assumptions nor the lifted conclusion. Existential-left retains its principal existential in the lifted context; the branch also receives its opened witness assumption. Each rule has only one finite premise tree, not a family indexed by all domain objects.

Every displayed formula remains equality-free

Every formula in every context and conclusion of a raw certificate of a pure sequent is equality-free. Its height is zero at an atomic or falsum leaf, and one plus the maximum of the premise heights at an inference. A one-premise inference therefore adds one.

Inspect the root rule and continue into its children. Propositional premises use components of their principal or concluding formulas; those components are equality-free. A quantifier premise uses an opened or instantiated body. The fragment laws preserve equality-freeness under each of these scope maps. Lifted contexts merely rename their members. Hence every premise is again a pure sequent, and finite tree induction proves the assertion throughout the certificate. This also explains why restricting the endsequent is enough for the cut-free grammar. The displayed height recurrence measures this actual finite tree.

Enlarge a context without inserting a new inference

For pure \(\Gamma\subseteq\Delta\), a supplied raw certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) gives one of \(\Delta\Rightarrow A\), with the same height.

Recurse on the tree with \(\Delta\) arbitrary. Identity and falsum leaves remain licensed by inclusion. A retained left principal remains available. For conjunction-left insert its two factors in the target context; for disjunction-left insert the appropriate factor in each branch. Implication-left weakens its antecedent premise and its continuation with the same consequent inserted. Negation-left weakens its antecedent premise. Right introductions reapply after weakening their children, inserting the same temporary assumption for implication or negation.

Universal-left retains its principal and its specified term, and weakens the premise to \(\Delta,B(t)\). Existential-right keeps its witness. For universal-right use \(\Gamma^\uparrow\subseteq\Delta^\uparrow\). For existential-left use that lifted inclusion with the same opened body inserted. The protected name is still absent from all lifted side formulas. Every original inference is replaced by one of the same arity; the height recurrence gives exact equality. This is the constructive counterpart of quantified sequent weakening.

Substitute across all four quantified rules

For every free substitution \(\sigma:X\to\operatorname{Term}(Y)\), a supplied raw certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) gives an actual raw certificate of \(\Gamma[\sigma]\Rightarrow A[\sigma]\), with the same height. Arbitrary free renaming, including noninjective renaming, is a special case.

Induct on the supplied tree, with target names and substitution arbitrary. Atomic identity substitutes the common term tuple; membership passes to the image context. Falsum and all propositional constructors commute with substitution. At a retained left rule its principal membership becomes the corresponding image membership; insertion of temporary formulas commutes with taking an image.

For universal-left and existential-right replace the witness \(t\) by \(t[\sigma]\). The identity \((B(t))[\sigma]=(B[\sigma])(t[\sigma])\) identifies the transformed premise with the required instance. For universal-right and existential-left extend \(\sigma\) by its protected extension: old replacement terms stay in the left component and the new right-tagged name maps to the new right tag. Opening and lifting contexts commute with this extension. In existential-left, it also takes the lifted \(C\) to the lift of \(C[\sigma]\). Thus the actual freshness restriction on both context and conclusion is preserved.

Reapply the same rule after every recursive call. The tree is still finite and has the same leaf, successor and maximum heights. Equality-freeness of every substituted formula follows from the shared syntax laws. No injectivity hypothesis on \(\sigma\), new substitution constructor, or semantic completeness theorem is used.

Expand identity at every pure formula

For every equality-free \(A\in\Gamma\), there is a specified raw cut-free certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\).

Use strong induction on logical depth, allowing the free-name set and context to vary. A relation atom uses identity and falsum uses falsum-left. For conjunction apply conjunction-left and introduce its two factors from their smaller identities. For disjunction apply disjunction-left, use each factor identity, and choose the corresponding right introduction. For implication introduce its antecedent, use the retained implication-left rule, and supply identities of its antecedent and consequent. For negation introduce its child, then use negation-left with that child's identity.

For \(\forall xB\), apply universal-right. The lifted context contains the lifted principal universal. Apply universal-left at the fresh term \(e\); its instance is exactly \(\operatorname{open}(B)\). The smaller opened-body identity concludes this premise. For \(\exists xB\), apply existential-left. In its fresh branch the opened-body identity, followed by existential-right at \(e\), gives the lifted target existential. Opening preserves logical depth, so both body calls are strictly smaller than the quantified parent. The tag conditions hold because all side formulas are lifted copies. These constructions use only the primitive cut-free rules.

Change a falsum conclusion to any pure conclusion

A supplied raw certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow\bot\) gives a cut-free certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\) for every equality-free \(C\).

Recurse on the supplied tree. Its last rule cannot be atomic identity or a right introduction, because those have a different outer conclusion. Falsum-left already permits \(C\). Negation-left also permits \(C\) with its unchanged antecedent premise. At conjunction-left and universal-left recurse on the unique continuation; at disjunction-left recurse on both branches. At implication-left keep its antecedent proof and recurse on its continuation. At existential-left recurse in the lifted branch, replacing its lifted falsum conclusion by \(C^\uparrow\); then reapply that rule. These are all remaining cases. The result contains no cut step.

Make composition an explicit additional constructor

A raw certificate with cut has the same primitive rules and also permits, for an equality-free cut formula \(B\),

\[\frac{\Gamma\Rightarrow B\qquad\Delta,B\Rightarrow A}{\Gamma\cup\Delta\Rightarrow A}.\]

It is a separate finite-tree collection. A cut-free certificate gives one with cut by copying all its constructors. Define its height by the same recurrence, counting a cut as a two-premise inference. Weakening and free substitution transform these trees too, preserving their heights.

Copying follows direct recursion. The structural constructions are the same rule-by-rule recursions as before, with one extra case: for a larger ending context \(\Theta\), weaken the producer to \(\Theta\) and its consumer to \(\Theta,B\), then reapply cut with ending context \(\Theta\cup\Theta=\Theta\). For substitution transform both premises by the same map and reapply cut. Union and insertion commute with images. The cut formula remains equality-free after substitution. If the ending sequent is pure, the purity proof also extends: its union context supplies pure members on both sides, and the explicitly pure cut formula supplies the only formula not already controlled by the endsequent. Thus every displayed formula remains pure, even across cuts.

The quantified cut-elimination construction removes every such composition step while retaining this exact pure endsequent and its parameter supply.

Read either sequent tree as native intuitionistic deduction

A supplied raw cut-free certificate, or a raw certificate with cut, of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) gives an actual native intuitionistic raw derivation of the same sequent with equality disabled.

Induct on the tree. Leaves become native assumption or falsum elimination. Right introductions copy their native introductions. At conjunction-left, native projections of the assumed principal supply its two temporary factors to the recursively translated continuation by proof replacement. Disjunction-left is native case elimination. At implication-left, apply the assumed principal implication to the translated antecedent premise, then replace the continuation's temporary consequent by that proof. Negation-left obtains falsum from the translated antecedent and the assumed negation, and then eliminates falsum.

Universal-left replaces its temporary body instance by universal elimination of the assumed principal at its specified term. Universal-right copies the protected native universal introduction. Existential-left is native existential elimination, with its assumed principal as the major premise and the translated protected branch. Existential-right copies native existential introduction. The full-syntax opening and instance identities are literal, so the resulting derivations have exactly the same endpoints. Finally a cut is the already proved native composition operation, giving the stated union context. No equality or classical contradiction rule is introduced.

Translate a pure native deduction into a tree with cuts

A supplied equality-disabled intuitionistic raw derivation in which every displayed formula is equality-free gives an actual raw sequent certificate with cut of the same endsequent.

Recurse on the native tree. An assumption uses compound sequent identity. Conjunction, disjunction, implication and negation introductions copy their right rules; native disjunction elimination uses a sequent disjunction-left consumer under an added principal and then cuts with its translated major premise.

For a conjunction projection, the consumer under the additional conjunction uses conjunction-left and the smaller selected-factor identity. Cut the translated conjunction against this consumer. For implication elimination, the consumer under its additional implication uses implication-left, the translated antecedent weakened to that context, and consequent identity. Cut the supplied implication against it. For negation elimination use negation-left under the added negative principal, with the translated positive premise, and cut with the translated negation. Falsum elimination cuts its translated falsum against a falsum-left consumer of the desired conclusion.

Universal elimination uses a consumer with its principal universal inserted. Universal-left at the specified term and body-instance identity produce the required instance; cut with the translated universal. Native universal introduction and existential introduction copy the corresponding sequent right rules. For native existential elimination, translate the witness branch, weaken its lifted context to retain the additional principal existential, and use sequent existential-left as a consumer. Cut with the translated existential major premise. Its concluding formula is still a left-renamed original formula, so the witness tag cannot escape.

Every cut formula is a displayed pure native formula, and therefore satisfies the cut rule's equality-free requirement. All recursive calls concern original strict subtrees. There is no classical-rule case and no equality-rule case. The transformation produces finite cutful trees without assuming that their cuts can already be eliminated.

Keep public certificates over the same auxiliary reserve

A public cut-free certificate of a pure sequent \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) is a raw certificate over \(X\sqcup U\), where \(U=\{u_0,u_1,\ldots\}\), whose endsequent is the left-renamed copy of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\). A public certificate with cut uses a raw cutful tree with the same convention. Derivability in either collection means existence of its specified certificate.

Weakening, free substitution, free renaming, identity and the translations above extend to public certificates. In particular, public cutful sequent derivability agrees exactly with native equality-disabled intuitionistic derivability for every pure finite context and conclusion.

For substitution extend the given map across the reserve, leaving each auxiliary name as the corresponding auxiliary variable; then apply raw substitution. The full-syntax scope identities identify the end formulas with precisely the reserved copies of the intended original formulas. Weakening uses the renamed context inclusion, and identity uses membership in its image. Both sequent-to-native translations act directly on the underlying raw tree. In the converse direction, the renamed end formulas are pure, so raw purification and translation produce the required reserved cutful tree. No original ground term or coherent choice of fresh names is required.

Move an existential witness through an assumed universal implication

For equality-free one-slot bodies \(P,Q\), there is a raw cut-free certificate of

\[\forall x(P(x)\to Q(x)),\ \exists xP(x)\ \Rightarrow\ \exists xQ(x).\]

Apply existential-left to the second assumption, with protected \(e\). The lifted context still contains the universal implication, and the branch also assumes \(P(e)\). Apply universal-left to that implication at \(e\), inserting \(P(e)\to Q(e)\). Use implication-left: its antecedent is the compound identity of \(P(e)\); its continuation assumes \(Q(e)\) and introduces the lifted \(\exists xQ(x)\) at \(e\), using compound identity for \(Q(e)\). Reapply universal-left and existential-left. The conclusion omits \(e\), so the existential-left freshness condition is satisfied. No cut is needed in this displayed construction, although a general native elimination translated above may introduce one.

Do equality-free endpoints forbid equality inside a native proof?

Suppose a native derivation has equality disabled and pure endpoints. Must its original tree already contain only equality-free formulas?

Show answerHide answer

No. Let \(A=\bot\to\bot\), allow assumption \(A\), and choose a free parameter term \(t\). From the assumption, left disjunction introduction gives \(A\lor(t=t)\), without applying an equality rule. Eliminate that disjunction: in each branch use the still available ambient assumption \(A\), concluding \(A\). The endpoints are \(A\Rightarrow A\), but the displayed intermediate disjunction contains an equality atom.

Purification replaces equality atoms by falsum throughout this equality-disabled tree, fixes its pure endpoints and returns the pure native input needed by the sequent translation. Restricting only the endpoint formulas is therefore not a justification for skipping that transformation. It also supplies no conservativity statement about inputs using logical equality rules.

Does a public existential certificate supply an original ground term?

In the empty language and with no displayed free names, can the pure cut-free calculus prove \(\exists x(\bot\to\bot)\)? Does that give a raw certificate without any auxiliary names?

Show answerHide answer

There is a public cut-free certificate. In its reserve, use implication-right over the assumption \(\bot\), closed by falsum-left, to obtain \(\bot\to\bot\). Existential-right at the reserved variable \(u_0\) gives the specified formula. The available slot is unused in its body, so this instance is exactly that implication.

There is no raw certificate over the empty parameter set, even if cuts are allowed. Translating such a certificate into native raw deduction would contradict the proved empty-domain obstruction. Thus the public proof uses its auxiliary term internally and produces no ground term of the original empty language. The reserve boundary persists independently of the later question of eliminating cuts.
