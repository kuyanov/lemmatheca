Keep infinitely many parameter names unused

Fix a first-order language \(L\) with countably many function and relation symbols in each arity, and take the parameter names to be \(\mathbb N\). All formulas below are the native full formulas, with their primitive constructive connectives. A parameter universe is a nonempty subset \(U\subseteq\mathbb N\) whose complement is infinite. A formula or term is supported on \(U\) when every free parameter occurring in it belongs to \(U\). Quantified placeholders do not count as free parameters.

Write \(\mathcal E(U)\) for the equality-free complete formulas supported on \(U\), and \(\operatorname{Term}(U)\) for the terms supported there. A supported premise set is a subset of \(\mathcal E(U)\). We use \(\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\) for the finitely supported certificate consequence with equality rules disabled. Thus an infinite allowed premise set still supplies only finitely many assumptions to any one proof. Proofs may use the separate auxiliary reserve of the certificate calculus; that reserve does not add parameters to their end formulas.

A world needs prime choices and present witnesses

A prime Henkin theory over \(U\) is a set \(T\subseteq\mathcal E(U)\) satisfying these conditions. It is proper: \(\bot\notin T\). It is deductively closed over \(U\): if \(A\in\mathcal E(U)\) and \(T\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\), then \(A\in T\). It is prime: \(A\lor C\in T\) implies \(A\in T\) or \(C\in T\). Finally, it has parameter witnesses: if \(\exists xD\in T\), some \(c\in U\) satisfies \(D(c)\in T\).

Here \(D\) is a one-placeholder body, and \(D(c)\) is its capture-free instance at the variable term named \(c\). Parameter witnesses are in particular term witnesses. Primeness extends the propositional notion; the additional witness condition is needed because first-order worlds have objects as well as propositions. Properness and deductive closure imply \(T\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}\bot\), and conversely consistency implies properness by the assumption rule.

Finite proof supports survive unions and discharged assumptions

For arbitrary premise sets, consequence is monotone, admits substitution of supplied proofs for assumptions, and satisfies

\[\Gamma\cup\{A\}\vdash_{\mathrm I}C\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\to C.\]

If \(\Gamma_0\subseteq\Gamma_1\subseteq\cdots\), every deduction from \(\bigcup_n\Gamma_n\) is already a deduction from some \(\Gamma_N\).

For deduction, take the finite supporting context of a proof from \(\Gamma\cup\{A\}\). Remove its possible occurrence of \(A\), keep the remaining assumptions in \(\Gamma\), and apply native implication introduction. Conversely, combine a finite proof of the implication with the assumption \(A\) by implication elimination. Monotonicity and assumption replacement are the established weakening and replacement transformations, taking finite unions of their supporting contexts.

For the increasing union, choose a stage containing each member of the finite proof support and take the maximum of these finitely many stages; the empty support can use stage zero. The same finite certificate is now allowed at that stage. This is a statement about proof supports, with no infinitary inference rule.

A fresh name can be treated as an arbitrary parameter

Let \(D\) be an equality-free one-slot body and \(c\) a name occurring in neither \(\Gamma\) nor the free support of \(D\). Then \(\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}D(c)\) implies \(\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}\forall xD\). If \(c\) also does not occur in \(B\), then

\[\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}\exists xD,\quad\Gamma\cup\{D(c)\}\vdash_{\mathrm I}B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}B.\]

Only finitely many assumptions occur in the supplied certificates. Rename \(c\) to the new right-tagged eigenparameter and every other original name to its left-tagged copy. On the old assumptions, on \(D\), and on \(B\) where applicable, this renaming agrees with left inclusion, because \(c\) is absent from their free supports. The opening and substitution laws identify the renamed \(D(c)\) with the opened one-slot body. Certificate renaming consequently supplies exactly the premises of the native eigenparameter rules.

For universal introduction, apply that rule to the renamed proof and discharge the protected parameter. For existential elimination, first combine the finite supports of the existential proof and the branch proof; weaken to their union, rename the branch as described, and apply the native elimination rule. The concluding formula and ambient assumptions keep only their original names. This justifies using an actually fresh numerical name as the protected parameter; it does not replace the eigenparameter condition by a semantic assertion.

Adding a fresh witness preserves avoidance

Say that \(\Gamma\) avoids \(B\) if \(\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}B\). Suppose \(\Gamma\cup\{\exists xD\}\) avoids \(B\), and \(c\) occurs in none of \(\Gamma,D,B\). Then

\[\Gamma\cup\{\exists xD,D(c)\}\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}B.\]

If the enlarged set proved \(B\), apply fresh existential elimination with ambient premises \(\Gamma\cup\{\exists xD\}\). That ambient set proves the existential by its assumption leaf and has no occurrence of \(c\). The result would be a proof of \(B\) from the allegedly avoiding ambient set, a contradiction. The avoided conclusion can be any formula; it need not be falsum.

Choose a larger universe before constructing the theory

Let \(U\) be a parameter universe, \(\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal E(U)\), and \(B\in\mathcal E(U)\). Partition \(\mathbb N\setminus U\) into two disjoint infinite sets \(R,S\), and put \(V=U\cup R\). This is a reserved enlargement: \(R\) supplies new witnesses, while \(S\) remains unused. For a definite choice, enumerate the complement increasingly and assign its even and odd indexed members to \(R,S\), respectively.

The candidate enumeration is a surjection \(n\mapsto F_n\) from \(\mathbb N\) onto \(\mathcal E(V)\). Such an enumeration exists: a term is a finite tree of countable symbol choices and numerical parameter names; a full formula is another finite tree of these terms and finitely many constructor choices. Finite products and countable unions of countable sets are countable. Restrict to the equality-free supported trees and enumerate them, repeating \(\bot\) where needed. This argument counts the actual full syntax and all finite arities, rather than only the smaller classical formulas. No effective decision procedure for the arbitrary set \(U\) is asserted.

Inspect each formula and immediately supply its witness

Define the avoiding stages \(\Gamma_n=\Gamma\cup\Delta_n\), with \(\Delta_0=\varnothing\) finite, by the following rule. If \(\Gamma_n\cup\{F_n\}\vdash_{\mathrm I}B\), let \(\Delta_{n+1}=\Delta_n\). Otherwise add \(F_n\). If this added formula has outer constructor \(\exists xD\), also add \(D(c_n)\), where \(c_n\) is the least member of

\[R\setminus\left(\operatorname{FV}(B)\cup\operatorname{FV}(F_n)\cup\bigcup_{A\in\Delta_n}\operatorname{FV}(A)\right).\]

In the safe case with another outer constructor, add only \(F_n\). “Added” includes the case where \(F_n\) was already present: an existential encountered by the enumeration is processed even then. The displayed least member exists because \(R\) is infinite and the excluded union is finite. A member of \(R\) never occurs in the original \(\Gamma\), which is supported on \(U\).

The avoiding extension is the specified union \(\mathsf H(U,\Gamma,B)=\bigcup_n\Gamma_n\), with the reserved enlargement and enumeration just chosen. Its construction uses classical case distinctions about whether a finite certificate exists; it is not an algorithm for deciding intuitionistic provability.

Every stage remains supported and avoids the conclusion

If \(\Gamma\) avoids \(B\), then every \(\Delta_n\) is finite, every member of \(\Gamma_n\) belongs to \(\mathcal E(V)\), the stages increase, and \(\Gamma_n\) avoids \(B\). The union contains \(\Gamma\) and avoids \(B\). Moreover, for every \(F\in\mathcal E(V)\),

\[F\notin\mathsf H(U,\Gamma,B)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathsf H(U,\Gamma,B)\cup\{F\}\vdash_{\mathrm I}B.\]

Induct on stages. Every update adds at most two formulas, preserving finiteness. A candidate is supported on \(V\); its chosen witness lies in \(R\subseteq V\), so the instance remains supported and equality-free by substitution. The old finite set is retained, so inclusion increases. In the rejected case avoidance is unchanged. In the safe case without a new witness it is the case hypothesis. In the existential case the chosen name is absent from \(B,F_n,\Delta_n\), and from \(\Gamma\) by the disjoint reserve; safe adjunction therefore preserves avoidance.

A proof of \(B\) from the union would already occur at one stage by finite support, contradicting its avoidance. To prove the last assertion, choose \(n\) with \(F_n=F\). Had the safe branch been taken, \(F\) would belong to the union. Thus the rejected branch gave a proof of \(B\) from \(\Gamma_n\cup\{F\}\); weakening puts that proof in the displayed larger set. This is maximal avoidance of \(B\), not a claim that every formula or its negation was selected.

Every supported nonconsequence has a prime Henkin extension

If \(U\) is a parameter universe, \(\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal E(U)\), \(B\in\mathcal E(U)\), and \(\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}B\), the specified construction gives a parameter universe \(V\supseteq U\) and a prime Henkin theory \(T=\mathsf H(U,\Gamma,B)\) over \(V\), with

\[\Gamma\subseteq T,\qquad T\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}B,\qquad B\notin T.\]

The stage invariants give support, containment and avoidance. If a supported equality-free \(A\) were deducible from \(T\) but absent from it, maximal avoidance would give \(T\cup\{A\}\vdash_{\mathrm I}B\). Replacing that assumption by its supplied proof from \(T\) would yield \(T\vdash_{\mathrm I}B\). This contradiction proves deductive closure. If \(\bot\in T\), explosion would also prove \(B\), so the theory is proper. The assumption rule similarly excludes \(B\in T\).

Suppose \(A\lor C\in T\), but neither disjunct belongs. Both disjuncts are supported on \(V\) and equality-free. Maximal avoidance gives proofs of \(B\) from each of \(T\cup\{A\}\) and \(T\cup\{C\}\). Combine their finite supports, use the disjunction assumption, and apply native disjunction elimination. This would prove \(B\) from \(T\), again impossible.

Finally, let \(F=\exists xD\in T\), and choose a stage inspecting \(F\). That stage cannot reject it: a proof of \(B\) from \(\Gamma_n\cup\{F\}\subseteq T\) would contradict union avoidance. The safe branch therefore adds a fresh instance \(D(c_n)\), with \(c_n\in R\subseteq V\). That instance remains in \(T\). This proves the parameter-witness condition. The complement of \(V\) still contains the infinite set \(S\), so the resulting universe retains room for further extensions.

Present conjunctions and existentials have present evidence

Let \(T\) be prime Henkin over \(U\), and let all displayed complete formulas and one-slot bodies be equality-free and supported on \(U\). Then

\[A\land C\in T\iff A\in T\text{ and }C\in T,\qquad A\lor C\in T\iff A\in T\text{ or }C\in T.\]
\[\exists xD\in T\iff D(t)\in T\text{ for some }t\in\operatorname{Term}(U).\]

If \(\forall xD\in T\), then \(D(t)\in T\) for every \(t\in\operatorname{Term}(U)\). Also \(\neg A\in T\iff(A\to\bot)\in T\).

Use native conjunction introduction and elimination followed by deductive closure. For disjunction the forward implication is primeness and the reverse implications are native introductions. A present existential has a parameter witness by definition; conversely a supported term instance supplies the existential by its native witness rule. Universal elimination gives each supported instance. All concluding formulas retain support and equality-freeness under instantiation.

For primitive negation, derive \(A\to\bot\) from \(\neg A\) by assuming \(A\), applying negation elimination and discharging that assumption. In the reverse direction assume \(A\), apply the supplied implication to get falsum, and use negation introduction. Closure converts these two native derivations into the membership equivalence. This comparison does not identify the two formula trees.

An omitted implication fails in a larger prime theory

Suppose \(T\) is prime Henkin over \(U\), with \(A,C\in\mathcal E(U)\). If \(A\to C\notin T\), there are a parameter universe \(V\supseteq U\) and a prime Henkin theory \(S\supseteq T\) over \(V\) such that \(A\in S\) and \(C\notin S\).

If \(T\cup\{A\}\vdash_{\mathrm I}C\), deduction gives \(T\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\to C\), and closure contradicts the hypothesis. Thus this enlarged premise set avoids \(C\). Apply prime Henkin extension to \((U,T\cup\{A\},C)\). Its output has exactly the required containment and omission, while preserving an infinite unused complement. With \(C=\bot\), the primitive-negation membership law gives the corresponding assertion when \(\neg A\) is omitted: some proper larger theory contains \(A\).

An omitted universal formula has a fresh future counterinstance

Suppose \(T\) is prime Henkin over \(U\), \(D\) is equality-free and supported on \(U\), and \(\forall xD\notin T\). For every \(c\notin U\), there are a parameter universe \(V\supseteq U\cup\{c\}\) and a prime Henkin theory \(S\supseteq T\) over \(V\) with \(D(c)\notin S\).

If \(T\vdash_{\mathrm I}D(c)\), the fresh universal rule would give \(T\vdash_{\mathrm I}\forall xD\), and closure would put that formula in \(T\). Thus \(T\) avoids \(D(c)\). The seed universe \(U\cup\{c\}\) is nonempty, and deleting one element from an infinite complement leaves it infinite. Both \(T\) and the avoided instance are supported on this seed. Apply the extension theorem; its larger universe includes \(c\), so the counterinstance uses an object name genuinely available there.

Implication and universality test all larger theories

The extension order is \((U,T)\le(V,S)\) when \(U\subseteq V\) and \(T\subseteq S\), with both pairs prime Henkin. For supported equality-free formulas and bodies,

\[A\to C\in T\iff\forall(V,S)\ge(U,T)\ [A\in S\Longrightarrow C\in S],\]
\[\forall xD\in T\iff\forall(V,S)\ge(U,T)\ \forall t\in\operatorname{Term}(V)\ [D(t)\in S].\]

Similarly \(\neg A\in T\) if and only if no larger prime Henkin theory contains \(A\).

For each forward implication, inclusion retains the asserted formula in \(S\). Native implication or universal elimination and deductive closure there give the stated conclusion; negation would yield falsum if \(A\) were also present, contradicting properness. For the reverse implications, an omitted implication has the larger counterexample in . An omitted universal has a fresh name in the complement of \(U\), and supplies a larger counterinstance at that variable term. The negation case uses its missing-negation specialization. In particular, testing universal instances only among the present terms is not the condition proved here: the larger universe and its new terms matter. These future tests supply the implication, negation and universal cases of the canonical first-order truth lemma.

Does finite proof support make a fresh reserve automatic?

Let \(P\) be a unary relation symbol and \(\Gamma=\{P(c):c\in\mathbb N\}\). Every deduction from \(\Gamma\) uses only finitely many of its members. Does that alone allow \(\Gamma\) to be supported on a parameter universe with infinite unused complement?

Show answerHide answer

No. The support of \(P(c)\) is exactly \(\{c\}\). A set \(U\) supporting all of \(\Gamma\) must therefore contain every natural number, leaving no unused parameter. This is a condition on the whole allowed premise set, distinct from the finite support of one proof. The premise set is consistent: the one-element classical structure making \(P\) true satisfies every member under its unique assignment, and ordinary soundness excludes a certificate of falsum.

An infinite fresh reserve is a separate condition on the whole allowed premise set. Merely inspecting one finite proof cannot reserve names uniformly for all later stages. Within the construction above, the stronger invariant \(\Gamma_n=\Gamma\cup\Delta_n\), with \(\Delta_n\) finite, is what keeps the selected half-reserve fresh.
