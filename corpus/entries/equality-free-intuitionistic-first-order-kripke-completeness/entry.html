A world records its available names and accepted formulas

Fix a first-order language \(L\) with countably many symbols in each finite arity. We use the native full formula syntax and the intuitionistic certificate consequence \(\vdash_{\mathrm I}\), with equality rules disabled. A canonical world is a pair \(w=(U,T)\), where \(U\subseteq\mathbb N\) is a nonempty parameter universe with infinite unused complement, and \(T\) is a prime Henkin theory over \(U\). Thus its formulas are equality-free and supported on \(U\).

The canonical extension order is \((U,T)\le(V,S)\) when \(U\subseteq V\) and \(T\subseteq S\). It is reflexive and transitive. Worlds can gain both accepted formulas and available parameters. The collection of worlds is a set: the universes are subsets of \(\mathbb N\), and the theories are subsets of the fixed set of full formulas on numerical names.

Use actual terms as the objects available at a world

At \(w=(U,T)\), define the canonical domain \(D_w=L\text{-}\operatorname{Term}(U)\): these are actual terms whose variable labels are elements of \(U\). The erasure map \(e_U:D_w\to L\text{-}\operatorname{Term}(\mathbb N)\) relabels each variable \(c\in U\) by its numerical name \(c\). A variable term at any member of \(U\) shows that \(D_w\) is nonempty, even if the language has no constants.

The term structure at \(w\) interprets a function symbol by term formation, \(f^{w}(t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1})=f(t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1})\). It interprets a relation symbol by

\[R^{w}(t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R(e_Ut_0,\ldots,e_Ut_{n-1})\in T.\]

The right side is the ordinary complete atomic formula with those terms. For \(w\le z=(V,S)\), the transition map \(j_{wz}:D_w\to D_z\) relabels variable labels through the inclusion \(U\hookrightarrow V\). These are actual term maps; different labels are not identified by a quotient.

The term structures form a nonempty Kripke diagram

The structures and transition maps above form a varying-domain Kripke model \(\mathcal C\). Its transition maps satisfy \(j_{ww}=\mathrm{id}\) and \(j_{zt}j_{wz}=j_{wt}\), preserve functions and preserve true relations.

Term relabeling commutes with every function constructor. Erasing a transitioned term leaves the same numerical term: \(e_V(j_{wz}t)=e_Ut\). Hence an atomic formula accepted by \(T\) is the very same numerical formula in \(S\), by \(T\subseteq S\). This proves preservation of relations. Identity and composite inclusions give the two coherence identities by induction on terms. Each domain is nonempty by the variable term already identified.

There is at least one world. Take \(U=\{0\}\), the empty premise set and the avoided formula \(\bot\). The empty set does not derive falsum: any one-element ordinary structure and ordinary soundness exclude such a certificate. The specified prime Henkin extension therefore supplies a world. This establishes nonemptiness without using the completeness theorem being constructed.

Substitute the assigned terms into every currently available slot

Let \(A\) be a scoped formula with free-name set \(\alpha\) and \(n\) available quantified placeholders. At a world \(w=(U,T)\), let \(a:\alpha\sqcup\operatorname{Fin}(n)\to D_w\) assign a term to every available variable. Its ground instance is the complete numerical-name formula

\[\langle A,a\rangle_U=A^{\mathrm{open}}[x\mapsto e_U(a(x))].\]

Here \(A^{\mathrm{open}}\) is the opening of all available slots into free names, and the brackets mean the established capture-free term substitution. Quantifiers still occurring inside \(A\) retain their protected slots. For a complete formula and \(s:\alpha\to D_w\), use the same notation \(\langle A,s\rangle_U\) with its empty available-slot assignment.

For a body \(B\) with \(n+1\) available slots, its partially grounded body \(B\langle a\rangle\) substitutes \(e_Ua\) into the old free names and the first \(n\) slots, leaving the newest slot protected. More precisely, map each old variable to its assigned numerical term, with its names in the left summand, and map the newest placeholder to the variable in the sole right-hand slot. This is a native one-slot body over \(\mathbb N\).

Grounding respects term values, transitions and the newest slot

For every actual term \(t\), its value in the term structure is its simultaneous term substitution by the assigned terms. Erasure commutes with that substitution. Every ground instance is supported on \(U\), and is equality-free if \(A\) is. Grounding commutes with each connective and satisfies

\[\langle A,j_{wz}\circ a\rangle_V=\langle A,a\rangle_U,\]
\[\langle\forall xB,a\rangle_U=\forall x\,B\langle a\rangle,\qquad \langle\exists xB,a\rangle_U=\exists x\,B\langle a\rangle,\]
\[\langle B,a\frown t\rangle_U=(B\langle a\rangle)(e_Ut).\]

In the last line, \(a\frown t\) appends \(t\) as the newest slot, and the right side is the native instance of a one-slot body.

Induct on terms for the value assertion: variables read the assignment and function constructors form the corresponding substituted term. Relabeling the result by the underlying numerical labels commutes with each constructor, giving erasure after substitution. Each substituted term uses only names in \(U\), so the free-support substitution law gives support of the formula. The same native substitution preserves equality-freeness.

Erasure of \(j_{wz}t\) is literally the old erasure, proving the first identity. The connective identities follow from the recursive definition of substitution. At a quantifier, its lifted substitution maps every old variable to the relabeled old term and maps the fresh last slot to itself. That is exactly the displayed definition of partial grounding. Substituting the erased witness into that remaining slot gives the appended assignment on every old and new variable. The opening and instantiation substitution identities therefore give the last line. These identities retain all earlier slots and the actual supplied assignment.

Forcing in the canonical model is membership in its theory

For every equality-free scoped formula \(A\), every canonical world \(w=(U,T)\), and every assignment \(a\) of its free names and available slots into \(D_w\),

\[w\Vdash_{\mathcal C}A[a]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\langle A,a\rangle_U\in T.\]

In particular, for complete formulas the right side is \(\langle A,s\rangle_U\in T\).

Induct on the same native full formula, with its world and all assignments arbitrary. Falsum is never forced and is absent from every proper theory. At a relation atom, the term-value identity identifies the erased term values with the terms of its ground instance. The assertion is then the definition of the relation at that world. A logical equality atom cannot occur under the equality-free hypothesis.

For conjunction and disjunction, apply the induction hypotheses and the prime-theory membership laws. Both child instances are supported and equality-free by the grounding lemma, so those laws apply.

For implication, its forcing clause tests every larger world with the transported assignment. Grounding that assignment gives the unchanged two numerical formulas, by . The induction hypotheses turn the test into: every larger prime theory containing the antecedent instance contains the consequent instance. This is exactly future implication membership. The same argument for primitive negation says that no larger prime theory contains its child instance; the established future-negation law gives its membership. Negation is treated as its actual constructor, without replacing its formula tree by a classical abbreviation.

For \(\exists xB\), a forced witness is a present term \(t\in D_w\). The induction hypothesis and the newest-slot identity put \((B\langle a\rangle)(e_Ut)\) in \(T\); native existential introduction and closure give the grounded existential. Conversely, a present grounded existential has a parameter witness \(c\in U\). Its variable term belongs to \(D_w\), and its witness-instance membership, the same identity and the induction hypothesis show that this term is a forced witness.

For \(\forall xB\), membership of its ground instance persists to every larger theory. Native universal elimination there supplies the instance at the erasure of every term in the future domain. The newest-slot identity and induction make each required body forced, proving universality. Conversely suppose the grounded universal is absent. Choose \(c\notin U\). The missing-universal extension supplies a larger world with \(c\) available and its \(c\)-instance absent. Its variable term is an object of that future domain. Grounding and induction show that the body fails there, contradicting the forcing clause for the universal. This proves the remaining direction and completes the scoped induction.

Read selected numerical names by their variable terms

At \(w=(U,T)\), choose the least \(c_U\in U\). The numerical-name assignment \(\nu_w:\mathbb N\to D_w\) sends \(c\in U\) to its variable term and sends every \(c\notin U\) to the variable term at \(c_U\). Thus \(e_U(\nu_w(c))\) is the original variable term whenever \(c\in U\).

If a complete formula \(A\) is supported on \(U\), then \(\langle A,\nu_w\rangle_U=A\): substitution agrees with the variable substitution at every name in its finite free support. Consequently the truth lemma gives \(w\Vdash A[\nu_w]\iff A\in T\) for every supported equality-free \(A\). The arbitrary assignment outside \(U\) has no effect on this assertion.

A supported failed consequence chooses a canonical counterworld

Suppose \(\Gamma\) is supported and equality-free over a parameter universe \(U\), \(A\) has the same properties, and \(\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\). Use the specified extension to put

\[V=\operatorname{enlarge}(U),\qquad T=\mathsf H(U,\Gamma,A),\qquad w_{\Gamma,A}=(V,T).\]

Then \((\mathcal C,w_{\Gamma,A},\nu_{w_{\Gamma,A}})\) is a pointed countermodel: it forces every member of \(\Gamma\) and fails to force \(A\).

Prime Henkin extension makes this pair a world, includes \(\Gamma\) in \(T\), and omits \(A\). The old support lies in \(V\). The numerical-name assignment and truth lemma therefore make each premise forced and the conclusion unforced. The same world and assignment handle all premises, even when the premise set is infinite.

Embed the original names while leaving a new reserve

Now let the original free-name set \(\alpha\) be any countable set, possibly empty. Choose an injection \(k:\alpha\hookrightarrow\mathbb N\) and define the reserved embedding \(\iota(a)=2k(a)\). Its seed universe is \(U_\alpha=\{0\}\cup\iota[\alpha]\). This is nonempty, and all odd natural numbers remain outside it. Its complement is therefore infinite. Renaming all end formulas by \(\iota\) makes them supported on this universe.

The reserve recovery map \(r:\mathbb N\sqcup\mathbb N\to\alpha\sqcup\mathbb N\) sends a left-hand name \(\iota(a)\) to the left-hand original name \(a\), every other left-hand number to the right-hand reserve name \(0\), and every right-hand reserve number \(n\) to the right-hand reserve name \(n\). Injectivity of \(\iota\) makes the first case unique. This map exists even when \(\alpha=\varnothing\), since the reserve summand remains inhabited.

A certificate using the embedded names reflects to the original syntax

For any injective renaming \(\iota:\alpha\to\mathbb N\), any premise set \(\Gamma\), and complete formula \(A\),

\[\iota[\Gamma]\vdash_{\mathrm I}A^\iota\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}A,\qquad \iota[\Gamma]=\{B^\iota:B\in\Gamma\}.\]

Here \(B^\iota\) denotes native free-name renaming, and the conclusion is the original certificate consequence.

A supplied certificate of the renamed consequence has a finite context \(\Delta'\subseteq\iota[\Gamma]\). For each member choose an original \(B\in\Gamma\) whose renaming is that member. These finitely many originals form a finite context \(\Delta\subseteq\Gamma\).

The same recovery construction works for this arbitrary injective renaming. The raw tree of the supplied certificate uses names from \(\mathbb N\sqcup\mathbb N\), where the second summand is its auxiliary reserve. Apply native raw-tree renaming by the reserve recovery map. On every embedded original name, the composite is the original left inclusion. Thus the renaming composition identities identify the new end context with the left-renamed \(\Delta\), and the new conclusion with the left-renamed \(A\). This is exactly an original public certificate. Protected eigenparameter rules lift the renaming past their fresh slot, so no auxiliary name is captured.

The finitely many preimages remain allowed premises in \(\Gamma\), giving the asserted theory consequence. An empty original name set needs no retraction from \(\mathbb N\) to the empty set: the actual recovery map lands in original names together with the certificate reserve.

Every failed equality-free consequence has a pointed countermodel

Let \(L\) have countably many symbols in each finite arity, let \(\alpha\) be a countable original free-name set, and let \(\Gamma\) be any set of equality-free complete formulas on \(\alpha\), with \(A\) also equality-free. If \(\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\), the canonical model has a world and an assignment forcing all of \(\Gamma\) without forcing \(A\).

Use the reserved embedding and seed universe from . Equality-freeness survives renaming. If the renamed premise set derived \(A^\iota\), would contradict the original nonconsequence. Apply to this supported renamed input, obtaining its specified canonical world \(w\) and numerical-name assignment \(\nu_w\).

Give each original name \(a\) the term \(\nu_w(\iota(a))\). The existing Kripke renaming identity says that forcing \(B^\iota\) under \(\nu_w\) is precisely forcing the actual original \(B\) under this composite assignment. It therefore transfers every forced premise and the unforced conclusion back to the original syntax. The frame and term domains just constructed are actual sets and belong to the range of models quantified over in Kripke consequence.

Kripke consequence agrees with native intuitionistic deduction

Under the countability and equality-free hypotheses of ,

\[\Gamma\models_{\mathrm K}A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}A.\]

The premise set may be infinite. Semantic consequence tests all pointed varying-domain models and their assignments.

A certificate is sound in every such model by Kripke soundness. Conversely, if no certificate exists, gives an actual pointed model and assignment forcing the whole premise set while failing the conclusion. This contradicts semantic consequence. Thus that consequence implies a native certificate, proving the equivalence.

A countermodel can have a least world

Given any pointed varying-domain model \((K,w)\), restrict its worlds to \(K_{\ge w}=\{z:w\le z\}\), retaining their domains, structures and transition maps. This is a model with least world \(w\). For every full scoped formula, its forcing at any retained world and assignment is unchanged. Hence the countermodels above may be chosen rooted.

The identity and composition conditions are inherited, and every retained world still has its nonempty domain. If \(z\ge w\) and \(t\ge z\) in the original model, transitivity gives \(t\ge w\). Thus all the original futures of a retained world remain present. Induct on the formula: terms and atoms are identical, current-world connectives and witnesses use induction, and negation, implication and universal quantification test precisely the same futures with precisely the same transported assignments. The original pointed failure and all forced premises therefore survive at the least world.

A semantic consequence uses only finitely many premises

Under the same countability and equality-free hypotheses, if \(\Gamma\models_{\mathrm K}A\), some finite \(\Delta\subseteq\Gamma\) satisfies \(\Delta\models_{\mathrm K}A\). Also \(\Gamma\) is satisfiable at a world and assignment exactly when \(\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}\bot\).

Completeness supplies a certificate, whose finite context \(\Delta\) is contained in \(\Gamma\). That certificate gives a deduction from \(\Delta\), and soundness makes it a semantic consequence. If \(\Gamma\) is not contradictory, apply the pointed countermodel theorem with conclusion \(\bot\); its world forces every premise. Conversely a model of \(\Gamma\) excludes a proof of falsum by soundness. These arguments use finite proof support, without bounding the model or its domains.

Do finite inputs guarantee finite worlds and finite domains?

Let the language have one binary relation \(R\). Consider the finite equality-free premise set

\[\forall x\,\neg R(x,x),\qquad\forall x\,\forall y\,\forall z\,((R(x,y)\land R(y,z))\to R(x,z)),\qquad\forall x\,\exists y\,R(x,y).\]

Could a pointed Kripke model forcing these three premises have a finite present domain? Does completeness provide a finite bound on both the worlds and domains for this input with conclusion \(\bot\)?

Show answerHide answer

No finite present domain is possible. Universal forcing tests the current world itself. There the relation is irreflexive, its present instances are transitive, and every object has a present \(R\)-successor. Starting at any object, repeatedly choose successors. A finite nonempty domain would repeat an object; transitivity along the nonempty cycle would give \(R(a,a)\), contradicting irreflexivity.

The singleton-world model with domain \(\mathbb N\) and relation \(<\) forces all three premises. Thus they do not derive falsum, while every countermodel to that conclusion has an infinite present domain. In particular no bound making both worlds and domains finite follows from the finite input. This example already has just one world; it makes no claim that this premise set needs infinitely many worlds. The propositional finite countermodel bound is a different result.
