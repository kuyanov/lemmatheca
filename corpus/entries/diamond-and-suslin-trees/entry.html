Diamond guesses every subset on stationarily many initial segments

Work in ZFC. A diamond sequence is a sequence \((D_\alpha)_{\alpha<\omega_1}\) with \(D_\alpha\subseteq\alpha\) such that, for every \(X\subseteq\omega_1\),

\[\{\alpha<\omega_1:D_\alpha=X\cap\alpha\}\text{ is stationary}.\]

The principle \(\diamondsuit_{\omega_1}\), abbreviated \(\diamondsuit\), asserts that a diamond sequence exists. Stationarity is essential: the correct guesses must meet any club required by a construction. We assume diamond where stated; we do not assert that ZFC proves it.

Diamond implies the continuum hypothesis

\(\diamondsuit\implies 2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1\).

Proof

Fix a diamond sequence. Regard each \(X\subseteq\omega\) as a subset of \(\omega_1\). Its stationary set of correct guesses meets the club tail \(\{\alpha:\omega<\alpha<\omega_1\}\), so for some such \(\alpha\), \(D_\alpha=X\cap\alpha=X\). Choose the least such \(\alpha\). Different subsets of \(\omega\) receive different indices, giving \(|\mathcal P(\omega)|\le\aleph_1\). Cantor's theorem gives \(\aleph_1\le2^{\aleph_0}\), so equality holds.

□

A countable-level tree can split without having an uncountable branch

A Suslin tree is an order tree of height \(\omega_1\) in which every chain and every antichain is countable. Each level is an antichain, so its levels are countable. An Aronszajn tree excludes uncountable branches; the Suslin requirement also excludes uncountable antichains.

For the construction, nodes at level \(\alpha\) are binary functions \(t:\alpha\to\{0,1\}\), ordered by extension. We maintain:

Every level \(T_\alpha\) is nonempty and countable; restrictions of a node to lower levels belong to those levels; each node has an extension on every higher constructed level; and \(T_{\alpha+1}=\{t^\frown i:t\in T_\alpha,\ i\in\{0,1\}\}\). Thus every node has two immediate successors. We call the extension property normality here, specifying it rather than relying on varying conventions.

Restriction closure makes the strict predecessors of \(t\in T_\alpha\) exactly \(t\restriction\beta\), \(\beta<\alpha\); they have order type \(\alpha\). Consequently the assigned level agrees with its tree height.

At a countable limit, choose branches covering every old node

Suppose levels below a nonzero countable limit \(\delta\) satisfy these conditions. If \(A\) is a maximal antichain in \(T_{<\delta}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\delta}T_\alpha\), one can choose a countable nonempty level \(T_\delta\) extending every old node, with every new node extending a member of \(A\). Without a prescribed antichain, the same extension is possible.

Proof

The old tree is countable, being a countable union of countable levels. Enumerate its nodes, and choose an increasing cofinal sequence \((\delta_n)\) in \(\delta\). For each old node \(s\), maximality gives an \(a\in A\) comparable with \(s\). Take the larger of \(a,s\). By normality extend it successively to levels cofinal in \(\delta\), choosing each new level above the current height. The union is a binary function of length \(\delta\), extends \(s\) and \(a\), and has all its restrictions in the old tree. In the case without \(A\), begin at \(s\) directly.

Choose one such function for each old node, and let \(T_\delta\) be their set. Repetitions are harmless. This level is countable and extends every old node. Its restrictions are already in the old levels, preserving the tree conditions.

□

A club aligns ordinal codes with height cuts

During the recursion, injectively label the nodes on level \(\alpha\) by ordinals in \([\omega\cdot\alpha,\omega\cdot\alpha+\omega)\). This is possible since the level is countable; the disjoint intervals make the complete label map \(q:T\to\omega_1\) injective. For every nonzero limit \(\delta\) with \(\omega\cdot\delta=\delta\),

\[q[T_{<\delta}]=q[T]\cap\delta.\]

These \(\delta\)'s form a club.

Proof

If \(\alpha<\delta\), then \(q(t)<\omega\cdot(\alpha+1)<\omega\cdot\delta=\delta\) for \(t\in T_\alpha\). If \(\alpha\ge\delta\), then \(q(t)\ge\omega\cdot\alpha\ge\delta\). This proves the equality.

Ordinal left multiplication \(\alpha\mapsto\omega\cdot\alpha\) is a normal function, and its fixed points below \(\omega_1\) form a club: start above any countable ordinal, iterate this function countably many times, and take the supremum; continuity makes that supremum fixed. Increasing limits of fixed points are fixed. Intersect with the club of nonzero limit ordinals.

□

Explicit closure bounds reflect a maximal antichain

Let \(T\) be a normal countable-level tree of height \(\omega_1\), and \(A\) a maximal antichain in it. For each node \(t\), choose \(a(t)\in A\) comparable with \(t\), and define

\[f(\beta)=\sup\{\operatorname{ht}(a(t))+1:t\in T_{<\beta+1}\}.\]

Then \(f(\beta)<\omega_1\), and at every nonzero limit \(\delta\) closed under \(f\), the set \(A\cap T_{<\delta}\) is a maximal antichain in \(T_{<\delta}\). Such \(\delta\)'s contain a club.

Proof

The nodes below \(\beta+1\) are countable, so the supremum is countable. If \(t\) has height \(\gamma<\delta\), then \(\operatorname{ht}(a(t))+1\le f(\gamma)<\delta\). Thus its chosen comparable witness belongs to the restricted antichain. Every old node has such a witness, which proves maximality there. The closure-point theorem gives a club of suitable limits.

□

Diamond constructs a Suslin tree

Assuming \(\diamondsuit\), there exists a Suslin tree.

Proof

Fix a diamond sequence \((D_\alpha)\). Construct the levels and labels simultaneously by transfinite recursion. Start with the empty function as the sole root. At successor stages take both binary successors of every node. At a nonzero limit \(\delta\), the old tree and its labels have already been constructed. Form

\[A_\delta=\{t\in T_{<\delta}:q(t)\in D_\delta\}.\]

If \(A_\delta\) is a maximal antichain in the old tree, apply to it; otherwise apply that lemma without a prescribed antichain. After forming the new level, label it in the interval specified in . Each operation uses only earlier levels. The lemma preserves countability, restriction closure and normality; successors preserve them as well. Thus \(T=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_1}T_\alpha\) has height \(\omega_1\) and countable levels.

Let \(A\) be any maximal antichain in the completed tree, and put \(X=q[A]\subseteq\omega_1\). By diamond, the set of \(\delta\)'s with \(D_\delta=X\cap\delta\) is stationary. Intersect it with the coding club of and the closure club of . Choose a \(\delta\) in the intersection. At that stage, injectivity and the coding equality give \(A_\delta=A\cap T_{<\delta}\), which is maximal in the old tree. Every node at level \(\delta\) was therefore made to extend a member of this antichain.

No node of \(A\) can have height at least \(\delta\). Such a node restricts to level \(\delta\), and hence extends some member of \(A\cap T_{<\delta}\), contradicting that \(A\) is an antichain. Thus \(A\subseteq T_{<\delta}\) and is countable. Every antichain extends to a maximal antichain by Zorn's lemma, so every antichain is countable.

Finally suppose a chain were uncountable. Its heights are unbounded in \(\omega_1\), since a bounded collection of countable levels is countable. Its downward closure is therefore a branch \(B\) with one node \(b_\alpha\) at every level. For each \(\alpha\), choose the successor \(s_\alpha\) of \(b_\alpha\) different from \(b_{\alpha+1}\). If \(\alpha<\beta\), then \(s_\beta\) extends \(b_{\alpha+1}\), which is incompatible with \(s_\alpha\). Hence \(\{s_\alpha:\alpha<\omega_1\}\) is an uncountable antichain, a contradiction. Both chains and antichains are countable, as required.

□

Does diamond list every subset of omega one?

Does \((D_\alpha)\) enumerate all of \(\mathcal P(\omega_1)\)?

Show answerHide answer

No. Cantor's theorem gives \(2^{\aleph_1}>\aleph_1\), so a sequence of length \(\omega_1\) cannot list all those subsets. It guesses their restrictions: for each \(X\), stationarily many coordinates contain \(X\cap\alpha\), potentially the same restriction for many different \(X\)'s. For subsets of \(\omega\), restrictions above \(\omega\) equal the whole subset, which is why CH follows.

What does this construction assume?

Does the construction establish diamond, or derive a Suslin tree from CH alone?

Show answerHide answer

Neither conclusion follows from this argument. It assumes a diamond sequence and uses its stationary guessing property to meet the coding and reflection clubs. CH supplies only a bound on the number of subsets of \(\omega\); it supplies no such guesses for maximal antichains. This entry proves the two conditional consequences of diamond, without claims about its derivability, consistency or independence.
