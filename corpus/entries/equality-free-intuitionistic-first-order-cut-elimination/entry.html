Compose pure sequents by replacing an assumption

Fix a language \(L\) and free-name set \(X\). Use the pure single-conclusion sequents: every formula is equality-free, and \(\Gamma\) is a finite set of assumptions. Write \(\mathcal P_X(\Gamma;C)\) for the actual raw cut-free trees of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\), generated by the fifteen primitive rules already specified. Semicolons separate the antecedent from its single conclusion; commas in the antecedent mean insertion.

A cut operation takes supplied trees of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) and \(\Delta,A\Rightarrow C\) and returns a cut-free tree of \(\Gamma\cup\Delta\Rightarrow C\). The separate trees with cut instead permit composition as a primitive inference. We construct the operation first, then use it to remove every such inference.

For a one-slot body \(B\), write \(B(t)\) for its supplied-term instance and \(B_e\) for its opening over \(X\sqcup\{e\}\). A superscript \(\uparrow\) left-renames old names into that disjoint union. The four quantified rules retain exactly the protected contexts of the preceding calculus.

Keep all assumptions in the replacement invariant

The replacement operation \(K_A\) is required uniformly for every free-name set and pure sequent:

\[p\in\mathcal P_X(\Gamma;A),\quad q\in\mathcal P_X(\Delta;C),\quad \Delta\subseteq\Gamma\cup\{A\}\quad\longmapsto\quad K_A(p,q)\in\mathcal P_X(\Gamma;C).\]

Thus a consumer may use \(A\) more than once, or have \(A\) already present in a retained context. No distinguished occurrence is deleted from a set. With an additional temporary \(D\), weaken the producer to \(\Gamma,D\) and use \(\Delta,D\subseteq\Gamma,D,A\).

Use the logical depth of the actual formula as the outer induction measure: atoms and falsum have depth zero, a unary constructor adds one, and a binary constructor adds one to the maximum child depth. Scope substitution, opening and free renaming preserve depth. Consequently \(B(t)\) and \(B_e\) have smaller depth than \(\forall B\) or \(\exists B\). At a fixed outer depth, auxiliary recursions descend to strict consumer or producer premise trees. The assertion is uniform in formulas and name sets, so a left-renamed cut formula is covered at the same depth.

Invert right conclusions, including a supplied universal instance

Actual cut-free trees admit these operations:

  • From \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B\land D\), obtain both \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B\) and \(\Gamma\Rightarrow D\).
  • From \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B\to D\), obtain \(\Gamma,B\Rightarrow D\); from \(\Gamma\Rightarrow\neg B\), obtain \(\Gamma,B\Rightarrow\bot\).
  • From \(\Gamma\Rightarrow\forall xB\) and a specified term \(t\) over \(X\), obtain \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B(t)\).

Recurse on the supplied tree. A principal right introduction gives its premise, or its two conjunction premises. For universal-right, substitute \(t\) for its protected \(e\), fixing all old names. The actual substitution operation changes \(\Gamma^\uparrow\Rightarrow B_e\) into exactly \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B(t)\).

Falsum-left already permits the new conclusion; negation-left does too, with its original antecedent proof. At conjunction-left recurse on the continuation. At disjunction-left recurse on both continuations. At implication-left keep its antecedent proof and recurse on its continuation. If inversion adds an assumption \(B\), weaken every unchanged antecedent proof to allow \(B\), as in the propositional inversions.

At universal-left retain its specified term and recurse on its continuation. At existential-left recurse in the protected branch and reapply existential-left: an added assumption is lifted with the context, and an instance requested at \(t\) becomes the instance at \(t^\uparrow\) there. The scope laws identify the new branch conclusion with the lift of the intended conclusion. These are all possible left rules. Atomic identity and a different right introduction cannot have the specified outer conclusion. Every recursive call concerns a strict child; no existential inversion is used.

Rebuild the consumer after removing retained copies

Suppose the principal reductions for a fixed depth are available over every name set. Then the replacement invariant is preserved through every propositional consumer rule.

Recurse on the consumer tree with \(\Gamma,p\) arbitrary. At a relation-identity leaf its concluding atom either equals \(A\), when \(p\) is the answer, or belongs to \(\Gamma\), when the same leaf applies. At falsum-left, either falsum belongs to \(\Gamma\), or the removed formula is falsum; in the latter case apply syntactic explosion to \(p\).

Rebuild a right introduction after transforming its strict children. For implication-right and negation-right first weaken \(p\) by the discharged assumption. At a left rule, transform each strict child with its additional component assumptions retained in the target. If its principal formula is already in \(\Gamma\), reapply the same left rule. Otherwise its membership in \(\Delta\subseteq\Gamma,A\) forces that principal to equal \(A\); use its principal reduction.

For implication-left the transformed children are \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B\) and \(\Gamma,D\Rightarrow C\). For disjunction-left they are \(\Gamma,B\Rightarrow C\) and \(\Gamma,D\Rightarrow C\). The retained cut formula has already been removed from these children by the recursion. This is the set-context consumer construction, now applied to full first-order formulas; quantified children will be treated explicitly below.

Keep the actual terms in the two term rules

The consumer recursion also preserves its invariant at existential-right, and at a nonprincipal universal-left rule.

If the consumer ends by introducing \(\exists B\) at \(t\), its strict child concludes \(B(t)\). Transform that child with the same producer and inclusion, then reapply existential-right at that same \(t\). The conclusion and all free names are unchanged.

If it ends with universal-left on \(\forall B\in\Delta\), its strict child has context \(\Delta,B(t)\). Weaken the producer to \(\Gamma,B(t)\), and recurse using \(\Delta,B(t)\subseteq\Gamma,B(t),A\). The result is \(\Gamma,B(t)\Rightarrow C\). When \(\forall B\in\Gamma\), reapply universal-left. Otherwise the principal is the cut formula; its separate reduction is . Every recursive call uses the original strict child and the original witness term, rather than choosing a new term.

Lift the producer into each protected consumer branch

The replacement invariant is preserved by universal-right and by a nonprincipal existential-left consumer step.

For universal-right, the child is \(\Delta^\uparrow\Rightarrow B_e\). Left-rename the producer to \(\Gamma^\uparrow\Rightarrow A^\uparrow\). Lifting preserves the inclusion \(\Delta^\uparrow\subseteq\Gamma^\uparrow,A^\uparrow\). Recurse on the strict child and reapply universal-right to the result \(\Gamma^\uparrow\Rightarrow B_e\). Its tag is absent from the lifted target context.

For existential-left on \(\exists B\in\Delta\), the child is \(\Delta^\uparrow,B_e\Rightarrow C^\uparrow\). Weaken the renamed producer by \(B_e\). Recursing gives \(\Gamma^\uparrow,B_e\Rightarrow C^\uparrow\), with every retained copy of \(A^\uparrow\) removed. If \(\exists B\in\Gamma\), reapply existential-left. Otherwise \(A=\exists B\), and this transformed branch is precisely the continuation used by .

Each child has smaller height. The renaming and weakening operations preserve the producer's height, and renaming preserves the cut formula's depth. Thus changing the name set does not change the induction level. The new tag can occur in its opened body, but never in the lifted ambient context or lifted conclusion.

Use smaller replacements at the four native connectives

Assume replacement has been constructed for every smaller-depth formula, over every name set. The principal conjunction, implication and native-negation cases reduce as follows; the disjunction case uses the producer recursion below.

For \(A=B\land D\), right inversion of the producer gives the two trees \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B\) and \(\Gamma\Rightarrow D\). The transformed consumer proves \(C\) from \(\Gamma,B,D\). Replace \(B\), weakening its producer by \(D\), then replace \(D\). The result is \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\).

For \(A=B\to D\), the transformed consumer supplies \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B\) and \(\Gamma,D\Rightarrow C\). Invert the producer to \(\Gamma,B\Rightarrow D\); replace its \(B\) using the first tree, then replace the temporary \(D\) in the continuation. For \(A=\neg B\), invert its producer to \(\Gamma,B\Rightarrow\bot\), replace \(B\) by the transformed antecedent tree, then apply syntactic explosion. These are the established asymmetric principal reductions; each replacement formula is a strict component. No output-height bound is needed at the smaller outer depth.

Carry both disjunction continuations through quantifiers

Assume replacement for every formula of depth below that of \(B\lor D\), over every name set. Given a producer \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B\lor D\), and continuations \(\Gamma,B\Rightarrow C\), \(\Gamma,D\Rightarrow C\), there is a cut-free tree of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\).

Recurse on the producer. A left or right disjunction introduction selects its corresponding continuation and the smaller replacement \(K_B\) or \(K_D\). Falsum-left permits \(C\); negation-left keeps its antecedent proof and also permits \(C\). At conjunction-left recurse in its enlarged continuation context; at disjunction-left do so in both branches. At implication-left keep its antecedent proof and recurse on its continuation. Weaken both supplied continuations by each newly retained assumption. These are exactly the propositional producer cases.

At universal-left retain its witness \(t\), weaken both continuations by the instance of its principal body at \(t\), recurse, and reapply universal-left. At existential-left, left-rename both continuations into the producer's fresh branch and weaken them by its opened assumption. They then prove \(C^\uparrow\) from that branch context plus \(B^\uparrow\) or \(D^\uparrow\). Recurse on the smaller producer child and reapply existential-left, concluding \(C\). Neither quantifier right rule, nor any other right rule, can conclude the disjunction. All cases therefore descend through strict producer children or use an already constructed smaller replacement.

Cut a universal proof at the consumer's specified term

Assume smaller-depth replacement operations. Given \(p:\Gamma\Rightarrow\forall xB\) and the transformed continuation \(r:\Gamma,B(t)\Rightarrow C\), construct \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\).

Apply to \(p\) at the actual term \(t\), obtaining \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B(t)\). Apply the already constructed \(K_{B(t)}\) to this tree and \(r\). Instantiation preserves body depth, which is strictly smaller than the depth of \(\forall B\).

In the consumer construction, \(r\) is obtained by first recursively transforming its strict universal-left child. Thus it contains the body instance but no retained copy of the removed universal beyond \(\Gamma\). If the producer ends in universal-right, its inversion is literally replacement of the fresh tag \(e\) by \(t\). This is the principal quantified reduction; the global inversion accounts for producers ending in other left rules.

Use an existential proof with a protected continuation

Assume replacement for every smaller-depth formula. From

\[p:\Gamma\Rightarrow\exists xB,\qquad r:\Gamma^\uparrow,B_e\Rightarrow C^\uparrow,\]

one can construct a cut-free tree \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\). The continuation has exactly the displayed freshness form.

Recurse on the producer \(p\), carrying \(r\). If it ends with existential-right at \(t\), its child proves \(B(t)\) from \(\Gamma\). Substitute \(t\) for the continuation's \(e\), fixing every old name. Its context becomes \(\Gamma,B(t)\), and its conclusion becomes \(C\). The smaller replacement \(K_{B(t)}\) combines those two trees.

At falsum-left use the same falsum assumption. At negation-left retain its antecedent proof and choose conclusion \(C\). At conjunction-left or disjunction-left weaken the continuation by the lifted new component assumptions, recurse on each strict child with existential conclusion, and reapply the left rule. At implication-left keep its antecedent proof and recurse on its continuation. At universal-left weaken \(r\) by the lifted principal body instance, recurse with the producer's supplied term unchanged, and reapply universal-left.

The remaining possible producer rule is existential-left. Its two protected tags are treated in . Atomic identity and all other right introductions have a different outer conclusion. Thus this is a recursion on strict producer children, ending either in the actual existential introduction or in a rule already permitting the desired conclusion.

When this operation is invoked for a principal existential-left consumer, the continuation \(r\) comes from the strict consumer-child transformation in . That transformation removes retained copies of \(\exists B\) before this producer recursion starts. No same-rank call is made on the unchanged original pair of trees.

Keep two existential parameters in different tags

In the producer recursion of , suppose the producer's last rule is existential-left on \(\exists D\in\Gamma\). Its child has names \(X\sqcup\{f\}\) and proves \((\exists B)^\uparrow\) from \(\Gamma^\uparrow,D_f\). The continuation can be transported to the further extension \((X\sqcup\{f\})\sqcup\{e\}\) so that the recursion and existential-left remain valid.

Rename the original continuation along the explicitly specified map

\[j:X\sqcup\{e\}\longrightarrow(X\sqcup\{f\})\sqcup\{e\},\qquad j(x)=\operatorname{inl}(\operatorname{inl}x),\quad j(e)=\operatorname{inr}e.\]

The map retains the continuation's tag as the newest tag; it does not identify it with \(f\). The opening and renaming identities identify its opened \(B\) with the opening of the left-renamed body. Its side context and conclusion are the twice-lifted \(\Gamma\) and \(C\). Weaken by \(D_f^\uparrow\). The resulting tree has context \((\Gamma^\uparrow,D_f)^\uparrow,(B^\uparrow)_e\) and conclusion \((C^\uparrow)^\uparrow\).

It is therefore a legitimate continuation for the strict producer child over \(X\sqcup\{f\}\). The recursive result is \(\Gamma^\uparrow,D_f\Rightarrow C^\uparrow\); reapply the producer's existential-left rule at \(f\), obtaining \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\). Both context and conclusion satisfy the original freshness condition. The protected scope composition laws preserve these equations below any further binders. Renaming and weakening do not alter the producer child's smaller height, so this commuting case preserves termination as well as freshness.

Compose supplied trees in the primitive constructive rules

For every equality-free formula \(A\), the replacement operation \(K_A\) exists. In particular, supplied pure raw cut-free trees can be composed by

\[\mathcal P_X(\Gamma;A)\times\mathcal P_X(\Delta,A;C)\longrightarrow\mathcal P_X(\Gamma\cup\Delta;C).\]

Use strong induction on logical depth, uniformly over formulas, contexts and free-name sets. At a relation atom the consumer identity case supplies replacement; no compound left rule has that principal. At falsum use syntactic explosion. Equality atoms do not occur. For each binary connective and native negation, the smaller replacements supply and . For each quantifier they supply or , since all instances and opened bodies have strictly smaller depth.

Feed these principal operations to the consumer recursion. Its propositional, term and protected-branch cases cover all fifteen primitive rules. Each same-depth consumer call has smaller consumer height; each auxiliary disjunction or existential producer call has smaller producer height. Calls using a smaller replacement may increase height, but those operations are already defined for all finite input trees. These nested well-founded constructions give the required actual finite tree.

For split contexts, weaken the producer to \(\Gamma\cup\Delta\) and use \(\Delta,A\subseteq\Gamma\cup\Delta,A\) for the consumer inclusion. The output has exactly the displayed sequent and uses neither cut nor a new admissibility constructor.

Remove every cut at the same endsequent

Every pure raw certificate with cut has a cut-free raw certificate with the identical antecedent and conclusion. The same actual transformation applies to public certificates over their fixed auxiliary reserve. Thus adding cut changes neither raw nor public derivability.

Recurse on the supplied cutful tree. At a relation or falsum leaf keep that leaf. At each of the thirteen other primitive rules recursively transform its children, then reapply the same rule. A quantified branch keeps its actual extended name set and its original opening or term. At a cut, recursively transform both children and use on the resulting cut-free trees.

The outer recursion always inspects strict subtrees of the original cutful input; its newly constructed replacement tree is not recursively processed again. For public certificates perform the raw transformation over \(X\sqcup U\) with the same countable reserve. Conversely, copying each primitive constructor embeds any cut-free tree into the cutful grammar. No reserve name is removed. This proves both existence equivalences without a proof-size bound or a claim about every possible reduction sequence.

Recover exactly equality-disabled intuitionistic deduction

For every pure finite \(\Gamma\) and pure conclusion \(C\), native intuitionistic raw derivability with equality disabled agrees with raw cut-free sequent derivability. Their public derivability relations also agree. A supplied native tree gives a supplied cut-free tree, and conversely.

The established native-to-cutful construction first purifies the input tree's equality-containing intermediates, fixing its pure endpoints, then translates every native rule into a cutful sequent tree. Apply . Conversely the existing sequent-to-native operation directly reads each primitive cut-free rule as native deduction. Both directions preserve the exact raw endpoints.

For public inputs perform the same operations on their actual reserved raw trees. The opening and renaming laws give precisely the required left-renamed endpoints. Every native input here has its equality rules disabled before purification; this result does not remove logical equality rules from an equality-enabled proof. It also introduces no classical contradiction step.

Track actual scoped subformulas and the falsum of negation

A scoped subformula occurrence of a complete full formula \(A\) is obtained by choosing child constructors repeatedly, including the empty path. Below each quantifier its newest slot remains available; a selected occurrence \(B\) may therefore have several available slots. The term tuples and variable occurrences in \(B\) are retained.

For a root sequent \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\) over \(X\), a complete formula \(D\) over any name set \(Y\) is an analytic instance if either \(D=\bot\), or there are a root formula \(A\in\Gamma\cup\{C\}\), a scoped subformula occurrence \(B\) of \(A\), and an actual scope substitution \(\sigma\) sending all free names and available slots of \(B\) to terms over \(Y\), such that \(D=B\langle\sigma\rangle\).

The extra falsum is necessary: native negation-right has a falsum conclusion in its premise even though \(\bot\) is not a child of the primitive tree \(\neg A\). This is the quantified version of the native analytic closure. Actual substitutions retain more information than connective shapes and allow the different name sets in protected branches.

Keep every displayed formula in the analytic instances

Every formula in every antecedent and conclusion of a pure cut-free raw tree of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\) is an analytic instance of that endsequent, at the node's actual name set. Every pure cutful proof, or native equality-disabled intuitionistic proof with pure endpoints, consequently has a cut-free result with this property. The same assertion holds over the public reserve.

Start at the root, where each formula represents itself under the identity scope substitution. Propagate the representation from the conclusion of a primitive rule to each premise. Retained assumptions keep their representations. A propositional component chooses a child occurrence of the representing formula; the outer constructor is preserved by scope substitution. Implication-left uses the antecedent component as a premise conclusion and the consequent in its continuation. Implication-right adds its antecedent and concludes its consequent. Native negation uses its child and, at negation-right, the explicitly allowed falsum.

At universal-left and existential-right, choose the principal body's occurrence and extend its representing substitution by the actual supplied witness term for the newest slot. At universal-right and existential-left, lift all previous term images into the old-name component and assign that slot the protected tag. Compose every retained side formula's substitution with the same left renaming. These are exactly the four primitive quantifier premises.

Child selection is transitive along a scoped occurrence, and composition of scope substitutions preserves its literal formula equation, including all term arguments and nested slots. Induction down the finite tree proves the assertion at every node. Cut elimination and supply the stated resulting trees. There may be infinitely many analytic instances as witness terms vary; no finite first-order search space or decision procedure follows.

For an empty antecedent, disjunct extraction and witness extraction read the final constructive rule of the resulting tree and return its actual selected proof data. The existential term stays over that tree's parameter set.

Falsum-left and all left rules require a principal member of the empty antecedent. Thus no primitive last rule is possible. Read one quantified cut as actual instantiation

For a supplied pure \(p:\Gamma\Rightarrow\forall xB\) and term \(t\), composing \(p\) with the universal-left use \(\forall xB\Rightarrow B(t)\) can be replaced by an actual cut-free tree of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B(t)\).

Construct the consumer by universal-left at \(t\), whose premise \(\forall B,B(t)\Rightarrow B(t)\) is compound identity. The principal universal replacement obtains \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B(t)\) by the specified-term inversion of \(p\); its continuation is the identity of that instance. If \(p\) itself is universal-right on a tree \(\Gamma^\uparrow\Rightarrow B_e\), the entire composition can therefore be replaced directly by substituting \(t\) for \(e\) in that premise. If \(t\) contains old free names, they remain old names, and substitutions below internal binders use the protected scope extension.

Why transform the existential consumer branch first?

An existential-left consumer retains its principal existential in its premise context. Why cannot its producer's witness immediately be substituted into that original branch?

Show answerHide answer

The original branch can still use \((\exists B)^\uparrow\). Substituting a witness for its protected tag changes \(B_e\) to \(B(t)\), but leaves that retained existential in the old side context. A smaller cut on \(B(t)\) would therefore leave the formula one meant to remove.

The strict consumer-child recursion first replaces every permitted use of that retained existential by the lifted producer, keeping \(B_e\) as an additional target assumption. The resulting continuation is exactly \(\Gamma^\uparrow,B_e\Rightarrow C^\uparrow\). Only this continuation is passed to the producer recursion and its smaller instance replacement. If the existential already belongs to \(\Gamma\), it is a legitimate retained target assumption and the original left rule may simply be reapplied.

Can an open existential proof supply a prescribed instance?

Does a cut-free proof of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow\exists xP(x)\) always give a proof of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow P(t)\) for a supplied term \(t\), as universal inversion does?

Show answerHide answer

No. Take the language with one unary relation symbol \(P\) and no function symbols. Use one free name \(v\), \(\Gamma=\{\exists xP(x)\}\), and \(t=v\). Compound identity gives the existential sequent. In the two-element structure \(\{0,1\}\), interpret \(P\) as \(\{0\}\) and assign \(v\) the value \(1\). The assumption is true, while \(P(v)\) is false.

A sequent certificate for the latter would translate into native deduction and contradict its ordinary soundness. Thus no such certificate exists, even if cuts are allowed. The principal existential construction instead uses the entire protected continuation and follows the supplied producer tree; it assumes no inversion of an arbitrary open existential proof.
