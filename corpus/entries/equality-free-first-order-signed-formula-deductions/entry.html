Compare formulas through their actual signed readings

Fix a language \(L\), free parameter set \(X\), and the full scoped formulas. For every equality-free full formula \(F\), define its positive reading and negative reading by

\[F^+=r(N^+(F)),\qquad F^-=r(N^-(F)).\]

Here \(N^\pm\) are the specified signed normalizations, and \(r\) is their full reading. Thus both displayed outputs are full formula trees at precisely the scope of \(F\). Their negative relation literals are read as primitive negations; signed truth is read as \(\neg\bot\).

Write \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) for an actual raw classical full deduction with finite allowed context \(\Gamma\). Choose either equality mode and retain it throughout. Only the formula being normalized must be equality-free; the allowed context may contain other full formulas. A conversion of conclusions takes a supplied tree and returns a tree with the stated new conclusion and exactly the same context.

For complete \(F\), a normalization package supplies, for every finite context, the four conversion arrows \(F\to F^+\), \(F^+\to F\), \(\neg F\to F^-\), and \(F^-\to\neg F\). Here each arrow denotes a conversion of proof conclusions, not a new formula connective. Its existence will be proved by depth induction.

Keep substitutions and opened bodies literal

Positive and negative readings commute with every compatible scope substitution. Consequently, for either sign,

\[(F[\sigma])^\pm=F^\pm[\sigma],\qquad \operatorname{open}(B)^\pm=\operatorname{open}(B^\pm),\qquad (B(t))^\pm=B^\pm(t).\]

Free renaming is included. The same identities hold for a signed tree's reading and duality.

First apply the normalization scope identity, then the reading scope identity. Both traversals use the same terms and protect the same newest slot under an internal binder. Opening, free substitution and term instantiation are the specified special scope maps, so their equalities follow without choosing fresh spellings. Equality-freeness is retained by all these maps, making both sides defined. We will use these identities to identify the actual conclusions of quantified proof rules.

Introduce and remove a double negation by deductions

For every full formula \(A\), a supplied proof of \(A\) gives one of \(\neg\neg A\). In classical mode a supplied proof of \(\neg\neg A\) gives one of \(A\), with either equality mode. Classical deduction also supplies \(A\lor\neg A\) in every finite context.

For double-negation introduction, temporarily assume \(\neg A\), weaken the supplied proof of \(A\), obtain falsum, and discharge the assumption by negation introduction. For elimination, temporarily assume \(\neg A\); the supplied \(\neg\neg A\) gives falsum, so classical contradiction concludes \(A\). The stated excluded-middle deduction gives the last assertion. These are primitive-rule constructions, not a conversion of classical truth into a proof. Every temporary context is removed by its specified introduction or contradiction rule.

Transport both factors or the selected disjunct

Suppose conversions between \(A,A'\) and between \(B,B'\) are available in every finite ambient context. Then there are conversions in both directions between \(A\land B\) and \(A'\land B'\), and between \(A\lor B\) and \(A'\lor B'\).

Project a supplied conjunction, convert its factors in the same context, and introduce the new conjunction. Reverse the factor conversions for the converse. For a supplied disjunction, use case elimination. In its \(A\)-branch convert the assumed \(A\) and introduce the left target disjunct; in its \(B\)-branch do the corresponding right introduction. Both branches conclude the same target disjunction, so case elimination discharges them. Reverse the child conversions to go back. Uniformity in the ambient context licenses the conversions under these temporary assumptions.

Derive both directions of the negative conjunction clause

Classical raw deductions convert \(\neg(A\land B)\) and \(\neg A\lor\neg B\) in both directions, in every context and either equality mode.

Given \(\neg(A\land B)\), split on \(A\lor\neg A\). In the negative branch introduce the left disjunct \(\neg A\). In the positive branch assume \(B\), form \(A\land B\), and contradict the supplied negation. Discharge \(B\), obtaining \(\neg B\), then introduce the right disjunct. The split concludes \(\neg A\lor\neg B\).

Conversely, assume \(A\land B\) and split the supplied negative disjunction. Its left branch contradicts the projected \(A\), and its right branch contradicts the projected \(B\). Both branches give falsum. Discharge the conjunction assumption to obtain its primitive negation. Only the forward direction uses the classical split.

Derive both directions of the negative disjunction clause

Raw deductions convert \(\neg(A\lor B)\) and \(\neg A\land\neg B\) in both directions. These particular conversions need no classical rule and work in either equality mode.

Given the negative disjunction, assume \(A\), introduce \(A\lor B\), and contradict the given proof. Discharge \(A\) to obtain \(\neg A\). Repeating this argument with \(B\) and right disjunction introduction gives \(\neg B\); conjoin the results.

For the converse, assume \(A\lor B\) and project the supplied two negations. Its left branch contradicts \(A,\neg A\), and its right branch contradicts \(B,\neg B\). Case elimination yields falsum. Discharge the disjunction assumption. This explicitly constructs the reverse negation proof.

Replace implication in either polarity

Classical deduction provides conversions in both directions for each pair

\[A\to B\quad\text{and}\quad\neg A\lor B,\qquad \neg(A\to B)\quad\text{and}\quad A\land\neg B.\]

For a supplied implication, split on \(A\lor\neg A\). The positive branch applies the implication and introduces \(B\) on the right; the negative branch introduces \(\neg A\) on the left. Conversely, assume \(A\) and split a supplied \(\neg A\lor B\). The negative branch gives falsum and hence \(B\); the other branch already gives \(B\). Discharge \(A\).

Given \(\neg(A\to B)\), obtain \(A\) by classical contradiction: under \(\neg A\), assume \(A\), derive falsum and hence \(B\); discharge \(A\) to form the contradicted implication. Also obtain \(\neg B\): under \(B\), implication introduction gives \(A\to B\) by ignoring its temporary antecedent, contradicting the supplied negation. Discharge \(B\), then conjoin these two results.

Conversely, from \(A\land\neg B\), assume \(A\to B\), project \(A\) and obtain \(B\). The projected \(\neg B\) gives falsum. Discharge the implication. All ambient supplied proofs are weakened into their temporary contexts; neither equality rule is used in these deductions.

Transport a body conversion through a quantifier

Let \(B,C\) be full one-slot bodies. Suppose conversions between their opened formulas are supplied in every finite context over the extended parameter set. Then deductions convert \(\forall xB\) and \(\forall xC\), and convert \(\exists xB\) and \(\exists xC\), in both directions.

For a universal proof, rename it and its context into \(X\sqcup\{e\}\). Universal elimination at the term \(e\) gives \(\operatorname{open}(B)\). Convert this proof into \(\operatorname{open}(C)\), and apply universal introduction. Its side context is exactly the left-renamed original context, so \(e\) is absent from it. Reversing the supplied conversion proves the converse.

For an existential proof, open its witness branch with \(e\) and assume \(\operatorname{open}(B)\). Convert that assumption to \(\operatorname{open}(C)\), then introduce the lifted \(\exists xC\) at \(e\). The conclusion contains no free \(e\), by its syntax, so existential elimination discharges the branch. Reverse the body conversion for the converse. The instance identities identify the opened formula with the instance of the lifted body; no original ground term is required.

Turn a negated universal into a negative witness

Classical deductions convert \(\neg\forall xB\) and \(\exists x\neg B\) in both directions, for every full one-slot body and either equality mode.

For the forward direction, apply classical contradiction with temporary \(\neg\exists x\neg B\). We construct \(\forall xB\) in this enlarged context. Lift all its assumptions into the left component of \(X\sqcup\{e\}\). To derive \(\operatorname{open}(B)\), use classical contradiction with additional \(\neg\operatorname{open}(B)\). Existential introduction at \(e\) gives the lifted \(\exists x\neg B\), contradicting the lifted temporary negative existential. Classical contradiction therefore gives the opened body. Universal introduction is legal because the lifted ambient assumptions omit \(e\). Its result contradicts the original \(\neg\forall xB\). The outer contradiction rule now concludes \(\exists x\neg B\).

Conversely, assume \(\forall xB\) and eliminate the supplied existential with protected \(e\). The branch assumes \(\neg\operatorname{open}(B)\). The lifted universal instantiated at \(e\) gives \(\operatorname{open}(B)\), hence falsum. Falsum contains no witness parameter, so existential elimination is valid. Discharge the universal assumption to obtain \(\neg\forall xB\). This direction needs no classical contradiction. The forward construction names witnesses only in protected branches.

Turn a negated existential into all negative instances

Raw deductions convert \(\neg\exists xB\) and \(\forall x\neg B\) in both directions, with either equality mode and without a classical rule.

For the forward direction lift the context to fresh \(e\). Assume \(\operatorname{open}(B)\), introduce the lifted existential at \(e\), and contradict the lifted supplied negative existential. Discharge the opened body, obtaining \(\neg\operatorname{open}(B)\). It is the opening of \(\neg B\). Universal introduction gives \(\forall x\neg B\), since \(e\) is absent from the ambient assumptions.

Conversely assume \(\exists xB\), and use its witness branch. The lifted supplied universal, instantiated at \(e\), gives the negation of the assumed opened body. Obtain falsum, eliminate the existential branch and discharge the existential assumption. The two quantifier rules therefore use exactly their stated freshness restrictions, including the requirement on the branch conclusion.

Verify every atomic and propositional conversion clause

For an equality-free complete formula at an atomic or propositional constructor, suppose normalization packages are supplied for its strict children in every finite ambient context. Then a normalization package for that formula is constructed by the following clauses.

At a relation atom \(R(\bar t)\), its positive reading is that atom and its negative reading is its primitive negation; all four operations retain the supplied proof. At falsum the positive reading is falsum and the negative reading is \(\neg\bot\), again giving identity operations. A logical equality atom cannot occur by the equality-free hypothesis.

At \(\neg A\), the positive reading is \(A^-\). Thus its two positive conversions are exactly the child's negative conversions. Its negative reading is \(A^+\). From \(\neg\neg A\), use double-negation elimination and then the child's positive conversion; in reverse, convert \(A^+\) back to \(A\) and introduce a double negation.

At conjunction or disjunction, use on the positive child conversions. For a negative conjunction, first use , then convert each negative child inside its disjunction branch. Reverse those operations for the converse. For a negative disjunction, use , convert both projected negative children, and conjoin; reverse those conversions and that De Morgan deduction to go back.

At implication, the positive reading is \(A^-\lor B^+\). Apply the first conversion pair in , then the child's negative and positive conversions in the two disjunction branches. Reverse the component conversions and the implication deduction for the converse. The negative reading is \(A^+\land B^-\); use the second implication pair and the corresponding child conversions on both factors. This specifies both directions of every propositional constructor.

Verify both quantified conversion clauses with fresh parameters

Let a quantified complete formula have one-slot body \(B\). Suppose a normalization package for \(\operatorname{open}(B)\) is supplied in every context over its extended parameter set. Then a normalization package for either quantified formula can be constructed using the protected quantifier rules.

For \(\forall xB\), its positive reading is \(\forall xB^+\). Apply with the two positive conversions for \(\operatorname{open}(B)\). The scope identity identifies its positive reading with \(\operatorname{open}(B^+)\), so the final universal has exactly the required full tree.

Its negative reading is \(\exists xB^-\). First convert \(\neg\forall xB\) to \(\exists x\neg B\) by . In a protected existential branch, apply the child's negative conversion from \(\neg\operatorname{open}(B)\) to \(\operatorname{open}(B^-)\), then introduce the target existential at that same protected parameter. The branch conclusion is the lifted target existential and omits that parameter. To reverse, convert the negative body back inside its witness branch, then apply the reverse quantified-negation deduction.

For \(\exists xB\), positive conversion uses existential congruence and the positive opened-body conversions. Its negative reading is \(\forall xB^-\). Convert \(\neg\exists xB\) to \(\forall x\neg B\) by , then use universal congruence with the negative opened-body conversions. Reverse both operations for the converse. All these child calls have the body's smaller logical depth; all quantifier introductions and witness eliminations retain the same lifted side context. These clauses construct the required parent package from the supplied opened-body package.

Construct all four normalization conversions

For every complete equality-free full formula \(F\), every finite context \(\Gamma\), and either equality mode, there are specified conversions of actual classical raw trees

\[\Gamma\Rightarrow F\ \longleftrightarrow\ \Gamma\Rightarrow F^+,\qquad \Gamma\Rightarrow\neg F\ \longleftrightarrow\ \Gamma\Rightarrow F^-.\]

Each arrow accepts its supplied tree and returns a tree in the same primitive calculus and the identical context.

Construct the four arrows together by strong induction on the logical depth of \(F\), allowing every free-parameter set and finite context. Use the atomic and propositional clauses or the quantified clauses to obtain the package at each constructor. Every recursive call concerns a strict child, possibly with its available slot opened as a fresh name. Opening is a scope map and preserves depth, so those calls still have smaller depth. This well-founded recursion defines actual finite proof operations; it does not select a proof from semantic validity.

Read signed duality as a provable primitive negation

For every complete signed formula \(A\), actual classical raw proofs convert between \(\neg r(A)\) and \(r(A^\perp)\), in every finite context and either equality mode.

The reading \(r(A)\) is equality-free. Apply its two negative conversions. The established literal identities give \(N^-(r(A))=N^+(r(A))^\perp=A^\perp\). Thus its negative reading is exactly \(r(A^\perp)\), not merely a semantically equivalent expression. Substitute this identity in the output and input types of the two operations. The resulting deductions connect the actual native negation constructor with the actual signed-dual constructor tree.

These actual deduction conversions supply the reverse direction of the native/sequent correspondence, with equality disabled on both sides.

Preserve the same reserve and the same finite consequences

All four conversions and the signed-dual conversions act on public classical certificates as well. In particular, with either equality mode,

\[\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm C}F\iff\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm C}F^+,\qquad \Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm C}\neg F\iff\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm C}F^-.\]

A public certificate is a raw tree over \(X\sqcup U\), whose context and conclusion are the left-renamed original formulas. Apply the relevant raw conversion to its renamed formula. By , normalizing this renamed formula gives exactly the renamed normalization of the original formula. Hence the output has precisely the public certificate's required end context and conclusion. The same identity holds for signed duality and reading.

Existence of the input certificate therefore gives existence of the output certificate in either direction, proving the two displayed equivalences. The operation retains every available auxiliary name; it does not remove a reserved witness or add a new constant to the language. With equality disabled, its output still has equality disabled. No conclusion about removing equality rules from an equality-enabled input is asserted.

Convert a negated universal implication with a nested existential

Let \(P\) be a unary relation symbol and \(R\) a binary relation symbol. Put

\[F=\neg\forall z\bigl(P(z)\to\exists w\,R(z,w)\bigr).\]

Its positive reading is exactly \(\exists z\bigl(P(z)\land\forall w\neg R(z,w)\bigr)\). The two formulas have actual classical raw proof conversions, with identical contexts and either equality mode; the same holds for public certificates.

The initial negation selects the negative normalization of the universal, producing an existential. Negative normalization of its implication produces a conjunction: its first factor is the positive \(P(z)\), and its second is the negative normalization of \(\exists wR(z,w)\). That second factor is \(\forall w\overline{R(z,w)}\). Reading the literal sign as primitive negation gives the displayed full tree. The older slot for \(z\) is retained when the newest slot for \(w\) is introduced; no free or bound occurrence changes its role. Apply the positive conversions for this exact \(F\), and use its literal reading calculation to identify their endpoints. The public result follows from .

Does a signed dual become the literal native negation tree?

Do the signed-dual proof conversions imply \(r(A^\perp)=\neg r(A)\) as full formula trees?

No. Take the signed formula \(A=\bot\land\bot\). Its dual is \(\top\lor\top\), whose reading has a disjunction at its root. The full formula \(\neg r(A)=\neg(\bot\land\bot)\) instead has primitive negation at its root. The trees are unequal. The theorem supplies deductions between them; it does not change these constructor identities or identify the resulting proof trees.

Can a formula conversion discard a temporary witness name?

In the empty language with no displayed free parameters, let \(F=\exists x(\bot\to\bot)\). Its positive reading is \(F^+=\exists x(\neg\bot\lor\bot)\). Does the existence of the transformed public certificate imply that \(F^+\) has a raw proof with no reserve?

Show answerHide answer

No. The supplied public proof of \(F\) uses a reserved variable as its witness. Copy it into classical mode and apply positive conversion to obtain a public certificate of the specified \(F^+\). Its proof is still over the reserve.

If a raw proof of \(F^+\) existed over the empty parameter set, the reverse positive conversion would give a raw classical proof of \(F\) in that same empty context and parameter set. This contradicts the established empty-domain raw obstruction, valid with either equality mode. Formula conversion therefore preserves the raw/public boundary; it supplies no ground term missing from the original language.
