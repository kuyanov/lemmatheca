Count formulas together with their finite parameter lists

Begin in ZFC. For a set \(M\), use the actual membership structure \((M,\in)\) and the definable-subset operation \(\operatorname{Def}(M)\). A definition code over \(M\) consists of a natural number \(n\), a finite membership-language formula with free variables among \(x,y_0,\ldots,y_{n-1}\), and a parameter tuple \(\bar a\in M^n\). Its output is the set

\[\{x\in M:(M,\in)\models\varphi(x,\bar a)\}.\]

Write \(C(M)\) for this set of codes and put \(\mu_M=\max(\aleph_0,|M|)\). Different codes may have the same output. The map from codes onto \(\operatorname{Def}(M)\) counts definable subsets without claiming that definitions are unique or that arbitrary subsets have codes.

Finite parameter lists give one uniform upper bound

For every set \(M\),

\[|C(M)|\le\mu_M,\qquad |\operatorname{Def}(M)|\le\mu_M.\]

For each fixed \(n\), the formulas in question form a countable set: they are finite strings over a countable alphabet, with a decidable grammatical restriction. The parameter lists have size \(|M|^n\le\mu_M^n\). Since \(\mu_M\) is infinite, finite products of an infinite cardinal give \(\mu_M^n\le\mu_M\), including \(n=0\). Thus the codes of each length have size at most \(\aleph_0\cdot\mu_M=\mu_M\). Their countable union still has size at most \(\aleph_0\cdot\mu_M=\mu_M\). The output map is onto \(\operatorname{Def}(M)\), so its image satisfies the same bound.

The maximum with \(\aleph_0\) keeps the argument valid for finite and empty \(M\), where counting formulas still produces infinitely many codes. This generalizes the earlier countable-domain argument.

An infinite set and its definable subsets have equal size

If \(M\) is infinite, then \(|\operatorname{Def}(M)|=|M|\).

For every \(a\in M\), the formula \(x=a\), with parameter \(a\), defines the singleton \(\{a\}\) as a subset of \(M\). The assignment \(a\mapsto\{a\}\) is injective, so \(|M|\le|\operatorname{Def}(M)|\). Since \(M\) is infinite, \(\mu_M=|M|\), and gives the reverse bound. Apply Cantor–Schröder–Bernstein.

Count every constructible level by induction

For every ordinal \(\alpha\), including finite ordinals,

\[|L_\alpha|\le\max(\aleph_0,|\alpha|).\]

Use transfinite induction along the actual constructible recursion. At zero, \(L_0=\varnothing\). At a successor, \(L_{\beta+1}=\operatorname{Def}(L_\beta)\), so and the induction hypothesis give

\[\begin{aligned}|L_{\beta+1}|&\le\max(\aleph_0,|L_\beta|)\\&\le\max(\aleph_0,|\beta|)\\&\le\max(\aleph_0,|\beta+1|).\end{aligned}\]

At a nonzero limit \(\lambda\), let \(\mu=\max(\aleph_0,|\lambda|)\). Every \(\beta<\lambda\) has \(|L_\beta|\le\max(\aleph_0,|\beta|)\le\mu\). Since \(L_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}L_\beta\), the indexed union bound gives \(|L_\lambda|\le|\lambda|\cdot\mu\le\mu\cdot\mu=\mu\). This finishes all three clauses of the induction.

The proof gives an upper bound at finite stages, not the false equation \(|L_n|=n\). For example, the finite levels are \(V_n\), whose sizes grow much faster.

At infinite indices the bound is exact

For every ordinal \(\alpha\ge\omega\),

\[|L_\alpha|=|\alpha|.\]

The ordinal part of \(L_\alpha\) is exactly \(\alpha\). Thus \(\beta\mapsto\beta\), for \(\beta<\alpha\), is an injection from \(\alpha\) into \(L_\alpha\). This gives \(|\alpha|\le|L_\alpha|\). The previous upper bound gives \(|L_\alpha|\le\max(\aleph_0,|\alpha|)=|\alpha|\). The two bounds prove equality.

Under V equals L, choose a level to which condensation applies

Now assume \(V=L\). Fix the finite construction fragment \(S\) used by condensation. Given an ordinal \(\kappa\) and a set \(x\), there is a \(\theta>\kappa\) such that \(x\in L_\theta\) and

\[(L_\theta,\in)\models S+V=L.\]

The finite-family reflection theorem for \(L\) applies to the finitely many sentences in \(S\) and the single first-order sentence \(V=L\). Under our assumption, \(L=V\) satisfies all of them. Reflect above a stage containing \(x\) and above \(\kappa\). The resulting \(L_\theta\) has the stated properties.

This is the strength required by the elementary-level condensation theorem. Transitivity alone, or being an arbitrary low constructible level, would not supply its model-theoretic hypotheses.

Every subset of an infinite cardinal appears before its successor

Assume \(V=L\). If \(\kappa\) is an infinite cardinal and \(x\subseteq\kappa\), then for some ordinal \(\lambda<\kappa^+\),

\[x\in L_\lambda.\]

Choose \(L_\theta\) as in , containing \(x\) and every ordinal below \(\kappa\). Apply the small-hull construction with seed \(\kappa\cup\{x\}\). It gives \(X\preccurlyeq(L_\theta,\in)\) containing each \(\xi<\kappa\) and \(x\), with \(|X|=\kappa\).

By condensation, the specified Mostowski collapse \(\pi_X\) maps \(X\) bijectively onto \(L_\lambda\) for a nonzero limit ordinal \(\lambda\). Every \(\xi<\kappa\) is fixed, by . Consequently each such \(\xi\) belongs to \(L_\lambda\). Its ordinal part is \(\lambda\), so \(\kappa\le\lambda\); in particular \(\omega\le\lambda\).

The bijection and now give \(|\lambda|=|L_\lambda|=|X|=\kappa\). Since \(\kappa^+\) is the least ordinal of cardinality greater than \(\kappa\), this implies \(\lambda<\kappa^+\). Finally the subset-fixing theorem gives \(\pi_X(x)=x\), because every member of \(x\) is an ordinal below \(\kappa\). Hence \(x\in L_\lambda\), as required.

The seed contains \(\kappa\) pointwise. It need not contain \(\kappa\) as an additional object: no claim that \(\pi_X(\kappa)=\kappa\) occurs in this proof.

A supplied stage bound has a separate counting consequence

Let \(\kappa\) be an infinite cardinal. If every subset of \(\kappa\) belongs to \(L_{\kappa^+}\), then \(2^\kappa=\kappa^+\).

The hypothesis means \(\mathcal P(\kappa)\subseteq L_{\kappa^+}\), so the identity on subsets is an injection into that level. Its size is \(\kappa^+\) by . Thus \(2^\kappa\le\kappa^+\). Conversely, Cantor's theorem gives \(\kappa<2^\kappa\), hence \(\kappa^+\le2^\kappa\). The bounds agree.

This implication isolates the cardinal arithmetic. It assumes the stage bound; the preceding hull and collapse argument is what establishes that bound from \(V=L\).

The axiom of constructibility implies GCH

ZF together with \(V=L\) proves the generalized continuum hypothesis:

\[2^\kappa=\kappa^+\qquad(\kappa\text{ any infinite cardinal}).\]

The constructible well-order argument first supplies Choice, so the ZFC cardinal counts and small-hull argument above apply. For each \(x\subseteq\kappa\), supplies \(\lambda<\kappa^+\) with \(x\in L_\lambda\). Monotonicity of the hierarchy puts \(x\) in \(L_{\kappa^+}\). Therefore \(\mathcal P(\kappa)\subseteq L_{\kappa^+}\), and gives the conclusion.

The containment concerns each subset individually. The proof does not assert that the whole object \(\mathcal P(\kappa)\) is an element of \(L_{\kappa^+}\).

Read power sets, bijections and successor cardinals inside L

Return to ambient ZF, without assuming \(V=L\). For an ordinal \(\kappa\), define the constructible power set

\[\mathcal P^L(\kappa)=\{x\subseteq\kappa:x\in L\}.\]

This is a set in \(L\), by the internal power-set theorem. A cardinal of \(L\) is an ordinal \(\kappa\) for which there is no bijection in \(L\) between \(\kappa\) and a smaller ordinal. Its successor \((\kappa^+)^L\) is the least larger cardinal as computed by \(L\). The superscript concerns which bijections are available, not a different collection of ordinals: \(L\) contains every ordinal.

Thus \(L\models 2^\kappa=(\kappa^+)^L\) means that a bijection in \(L\) pairs \(\mathcal P^L(\kappa)\) with \((\kappa^+)^L\). It does not identify either the internal power set or the internal successor with its ambient counterpart.

The constructible universe satisfies GCH internally

In ZF, the constructible universe satisfies GCH. More explicitly, if \(\kappa\) is an infinite cardinal of \(L\) and \(\lambda=(\kappa^+)^L\), there is a bijection belonging to \(L\) between \(\mathcal P^L(\kappa)\) and \(\lambda\).

The inner-model theorem and constructible Choice theorem give \(L\models\mathrm{ZFC}\). Also \(L\models V=L\). The preceding proof is a proof in ZF from that additional axiom, so it can be carried out inside \(L\). In that proof, subsets are the members of \(\mathcal P^L(\kappa)\), elementary hulls and collapse maps are constructed in \(L\), and all size comparisons use bijections available in \(L\).

In particular its subset-stage conclusion is: for every \(x\in\mathcal P^L(\kappa)\), some \(\alpha<\lambda\) satisfies \(x\in L_\alpha\). The internally computed levels agree with the actual ones by hierarchy absoluteness. The internal counting and Cantor bounds then give the required internal bijection. This is the stated mathematical meaning of \(L\models\mathrm{GCH}\).

As in the inner-model entry, class satisfaction here is the formula-by-formula relativization to \(L\). No set-sized structure with domain all of \(L\), or global truth predicate for arbitrary classes, is being assumed.

Can the subset-stage theorem include the empty cardinal?

At \(\kappa=0\), does every \(x\subseteq\kappa\) belong to some \(L_\alpha\) with \(\alpha<\kappa^+\)?

No. Here \(\kappa^+=1\) and the only subset is \(x=\varnothing\). The only ordinal below \(1\) is \(0\), but \(L_0=\varnothing\) does not contain \(\varnothing\). The subset first appears as an element of \(L_1=\{\varnothing\}\). This already disproves a statement for all cardinals. In the proof for infinite \(\kappa\), infinitude also makes the seed size exactly \(\kappa\) and ensures the collapse index is an infinite ordinal, where \(|L_\lambda|=|\lambda|\) applies.

What happens if the internal successor has smaller ambient size?

For this question assume ambient Choice. Suppose \(\kappa\) is an ambient infinite cardinal and \(\lambda=(\kappa^+)^L<\kappa^+\), where the last successor is ambient. What is the ambient size of \(\mathcal P^L(\kappa)\)?

Show answerHide answer

It is \(\kappa\). First \(\kappa\) is also a cardinal of \(L\): a constructible bijection with a smaller ordinal would be an ambient bijection, contradicting the ambient cardinal property. Thus supplies a bijection \(f\in L\) from \(\mathcal P^L(\kappa)\) to \(\lambda\). It remains a bijection in the ambient universe, because its domain, range and ordered pairs are the same actual sets.

Now \(\kappa<\lambda<\kappa^+\). By the definition of the ambient successor cardinal, \(|\lambda|=\kappa\). The bijection therefore gives \(|\mathcal P^L(\kappa)|=\kappa\) in the ambient universe, although \(L\) computes its size as its own next cardinal. This is a conditional calculation; no existence of such an ambient universe is needed here. It explains why internal GCH cannot be replaced by the ambient equation \(|\mathcal P^L(\kappa)|=\kappa^+\).
