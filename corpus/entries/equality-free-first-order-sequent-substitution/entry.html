Take images of the actual finite sequents

Fix a language \(L\), parameter sets \(X,Y\), the signed formulas \(\mathcal N_X(n)\), and the seven-rule raw certificates \(\mathcal C_X(\Gamma)\). Contexts are finite sets of complete signed formulas; their members are alternative conclusions. Every certificate considered here is cut-free.

For a free substitution \(\sigma:X\to\operatorname{Term}(Y)\), the substituted sequent is its image \(\Gamma[\sigma]=\{A[\sigma]:A\in\Gamma\}\), using the existing capture-free signed substitution. A renamed sequent \(\Gamma[f]\) is the special case \(\sigma(x)=f(x)\), as a variable term. Equal images occur just once, since the sequent is a set. Neither operation adds a logical inference to the calculus.

Keep the freshly opened parameter outside every replacement term

For an eigenparameter premise, names belong to \(X\sqcup\{e\}\). The protected extension \(\sigma^e:X\sqcup\{e\}\to\operatorname{Term}(Y\sqcup\{e'\})\) sends each original \(x\) to \(\sigma(x)\) with all its names placed in the left component, and sends the new right-tagged name \(e\) to the new right-tagged name \(e'\).

The names in every old image stay in \(Y\), so none becomes \(e'\). This is a free-name substitution on an already opened premise. Inside a quantified formula, by contrast, the scope lift protects the newly bound slot. These two operations serve the same freshness purpose at different levels: one acts on a proof branch's free names, the other acts under a formula binder.

Opening and lifting contexts commute with protected substitution

Let \(B\in\mathcal N_X(1)\), let \(\Gamma\) be a sequent over \(X\), and write \(\Gamma^\uparrow\) for its image under the left inclusion. Then

\[\operatorname{open}(B)[\sigma^e]=\operatorname{open}(B[\sigma]),\qquad \Gamma^\uparrow[\sigma^e]=(\Gamma[\sigma])^\uparrow.\]

The first equality is the opening identity, transported through the signed reading. Check the two kinds of input: an original free name gives the left-tagged term \(\sigma(x)\) on both sides; the opened slot gives \(e'\) on both sides. Composition of scope substitutions then proves equality of the entire formula, including all internal binders.

For each side formula \(A\), substituting \(\sigma^e\) in its left-renamed copy gives the left-renamed copy of \(A[\sigma]\). There is no occurrence of the new right-tagged name in this copy. Taking images of the finite set gives the context equality; coincident images cause no problem. In particular, the transformed side context still omits the transformed eigenparameter.

Transform witness terms without changing logical rank

For every one-slot signed body \(B\), term \(t\) over \(X\), and free substitution \(\sigma\),

\[(B(t))[\sigma]=(B[\sigma])(t[\sigma]).\]

Moreover, substitution commutes with signed duality and preserves logical rank and the quantifier-free test. The same statements hold for free renaming.

The instance equality follows by comparing the composed scope maps. An original free name has image \(\sigma(x)\) in both orders. The available slot first becomes \(t\), then \(t[\sigma]\), which is exactly its image in the other order. The signed scope composition law proves the equality. Duality changes only signs and logical constructors, so it commutes with the same term traversal. Rank and the quantifier-free test inspect that retained logical tree, and hence are unchanged even if a replacement term is much larger than the name it replaces.

Record the finitely many names in an endsequent

The free support of a sequent is \(\operatorname{FV}(\Gamma)=\bigcup_{A\in\Gamma}\operatorname{FV}(A)\), a finite set of free parameter names. Quantified slots do not contribute to it. Its substituted support is

\[\operatorname{FV}(\Gamma[\sigma])= \bigcup_{x\in\operatorname{FV}(\Gamma)}\operatorname{FV}(\sigma(x)).\]

The signed free support is the support of its full reading. Reading commutes with substitution, so the full-formula support identity gives the corresponding identity for each signed formula. Taking the finite union over \(\Gamma\), and exchanging the two finite unions, gives the displayed formula. The support records an endsequent, not every auxiliary name in its proof: a temporary eigenparameter or reserve witness may occur only inside the tree.

Enlarge a sequent by rebuilding its primitive proof tree

For every supplied \(p\in\mathcal C_X(\Gamma)\) and inclusion \(\Gamma\subseteq\Delta\), there is a specified raw certificate \(W(p,\Delta)\in\mathcal C_X(\Delta)\), with exactly the same height as \(p\).

Define \(W\) recursively on the given tree, with the larger target sequent arbitrary. An atomic identity leaf remains an identity, because its two signed literals are still present. A truth leaf remains a truth leaf for the same reason. These cases retain height zero.

Suppose the last inference introduces \(A\land B\), with side sequent \(\Lambda\) and premises \(\Lambda,A\) and \(\Lambda,B\). The original conclusion is \(\Lambda,A\land B\subseteq\Delta\). Therefore \(\Lambda\subseteq\Delta\) and the principal conjunction already belongs to \(\Delta\). Recursively enlarge the two premises to \(\Delta,A\) and \(\Delta,B\), then reapply conjunction introduction. Its conclusion is \(\Delta,A\land B=\Delta\). Thus the new root is a genuine primitive rule, not an extra weakening step.

For disjunction introduction enlarge its premise to \(\Delta,A,B\), then introduce \(A\lor B\), which already belongs to \(\Delta\). For bottom introduction enlarge its child to \(\Delta\), then reapply the same bottom introduction; its inserted bottom is already a member of \(\Delta\). These are exactly the same set equalities as in the conjunction case.

For universal introduction with body \(B\), enlarge the premise from \(\Lambda^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(B)\) to \(\Delta^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(B)\). Context lifting preserves inclusion, and the new eigenparameter remains absent from the lifted target side context. Reapply universal introduction and use \(\forall B\in\Delta\). For existential introduction at \(t\), enlarge its premise to \(\Delta,B(t)\), retain the same witness term, and reapply the rule using \(\exists B\in\Delta\). These cases exhaust the seven rules.

Every recursive child has its old height, by induction. Each one-child inference adds one in both trees, and each conjunction inference takes the maximum of the same two heights and adds one. The height recurrence therefore gives exact equality, not merely an upper bound. Extra unused side formulas never add nodes.

Substitute through an entire raw certificate

For every free substitution \(\sigma:X\to\operatorname{Term}(Y)\) and supplied \(p\in\mathcal C_X(\Gamma)\), a specified tree transformation returns \(S_\sigma(p)\in\mathcal C_Y(\Gamma[\sigma])\), with exactly the same height.

Recurse on \(p\), allowing the target parameter set and substitution to vary. An identity leaf \(R(\bar t),\overline{R(\bar t)}\) becomes the identity leaf at the common substituted tuple \(\bar t[\sigma]\). Both image members belong to \(\Gamma[\sigma]\). A truth leaf is still a truth leaf, because truth is unchanged.

At conjunction introduction transform both children using \(\sigma\), then reapply that rule to the substituted component formulas. Image commutes with insertion, so its two premises and conclusion are exactly the required images. Disjunction and bottom introduction use the same fact with their one child. This argument permits two different original formulas to acquire the same image; no rule requires those formulas to remain distinct.

At existential introduction with body \(B\) and witness \(t\), the recursively transformed premise has the inserted formula \((B(t))[\sigma]\). By , this is \((B[\sigma])(t[\sigma])\). The genuine existential rule with body \(B[\sigma]\) and witness \(t[\sigma]\) therefore has exactly the substituted endsequent.

At universal introduction, apply the recursion to the eigenparameter premise using \(\sigma^e\), not an unrestricted assignment to its right-tagged name. By , the resulting premise is \((\Lambda[\sigma])^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(B[\sigma])\). It has the exact freshness form required for universal introduction. Reapply that rule. The original branch's protected name may change its tag's spelling, but cannot become a name inside an old replacement term.

Every case replaces one original rule by one rule of the same arity, with recursive calls only on original strict children. The operation therefore terminates on a finite tree. Its height is unchanged by the same zero, successor and maximum recurrence used for weakening. Terms can grow while this logical proof height stays fixed. Neither cut, semantic completeness nor an additional substitution constructor has been used.

In the principal quantified cut reduction, this operation replaces a protected universal parameter by the existential consumer’s actual witness term.

Rename freely, and refresh a presentation by a bijection

Every map \(f:X\to Y\), including a noninjective map, transforms a supplied raw certificate of \(\Gamma\) into one of \(\Gamma[f]\) with the same height. If \(f\) is a bijection, raw certificates exist for \(\Gamma\) exactly when they exist for \(\Gamma[f]\).

Use with variable images. For the converse under a bijection, rename a supplied image certificate along \(f^{-1}\). The formula identity and composition laws give \((\Gamma[f])[f^{-1}]=\Gamma\), so the resulting certificate has precisely the original endsequent. This claims an exact correspondence of endsequents and existence, without asserting equality of differently reconstructed proof trees.

In particular, a presentation over \(X\sqcup\{e\}\) can be refreshed to \(X\sqcup\{e'\}\) by the bijection that fixes the old names and changes the right tag. There is no need to select a name outside an unspecified set: both tags are disjoint from the old names by construction. Inside subsequent eigenparameter rules, the transformation protects their newly introduced tags in turn.

Carry the transformations across the auxiliary reserve

Weakening, arbitrary free substitution and arbitrary free renaming also transform the public certificates, with exactly the same raw-tree height. Thus \(\Gamma\subseteq\Delta\) gives a transformation from a certificate of \(\Gamma\) to one of \(\Delta\), and \(\sigma:X\to\operatorname{Term}(Y)\) gives one from a certificate of \(\Gamma\) to one of \(\Gamma[\sigma]\).

A certificate is a raw tree over \(X\sqcup U\), whose end formulas are the left-renamed copies of \(\Gamma\). For weakening, take the left-renamed inclusion and apply \(W\). For substitution, extend \(\sigma\) to \(X\sqcup U\): send \(x\) to its replacement term included into \(Y\sqcup U\), and leave each auxiliary \(u_i\) as the corresponding auxiliary name. Apply \(S\) to this extended substitution.

On the end formulas, composing left renaming with this extension is exactly first applying \(\sigma\), then left renaming. Consequently the transformed raw endsequent is the reserve copy of \(\Gamma[\sigma]\), as required. The raw operation's exact height equality gives the certificate equality as well. Set variable images for renaming. In particular, these operations do not require a ground term in the original language: existing reserved witness names remain available, while an end formula still contains no reserve name.

Replace a free name beside a bound slot

Let \(R\) be a binary relation symbol, \(f\) a unary function symbol, and \(x,y\) parameter names. Put \(B(z)=R(x,z)\) and \(\Gamma=\{\forall z R(x,z),\exists z\overline{R(x,z)}\}\). There is a raw certificate of \(\Gamma\); substituting \(x\mapsto f(y)\) gives one of

\[\{\forall z R(f(y),z),\ \exists z\overline{R(f(y),z)}\}.\]

Both constructed certificates have height two.

Apply universal introduction to the positive universal formula, using protected parameter \(e\). Its premise has \(R(x,e)\) and the left-renamed negative existential. Introduce that existential using witness \(e\). The remaining premise is the atomic identity containing \(R(x,e)\) and its negative literal, of height zero. Existential introduction raises the height to one, and universal introduction raises it to two.

Apply \(S_\sigma\) with the stipulated replacement. The transformed identity uses \(R(f(y),e')\) and its negative. Its existential witness is still \(e'\), and its universal rule still binds only the body's slot. Thus the two root quantified formulas have exactly the displayed terms, and preserves height two. This example separates growth of an atomic term from the unchanged logical tree.

Can replacing a free name turn it into the bound variable?

Start with \(\forall z\,R(x,z)\), and replace the free name \(x\) by another free name \(y\). Is the result \(\forall y\,R(y,y)\)?

Show answerHide answer

No. The correct result is \(\forall z\,R(y,z)\). The inserted name belongs to the free-parameter component; the quantified placeholder belongs to a disjoint bound-slot component. The two formulas can have different truth values, so identifying these occurrences changes more than their printed names.

In the two-element domain \(\{0,1\}\), interpret \(R(a,b)\) as \(a=b\), and assign the free \(y\) the value zero. The correct formula is false, since its instance at one is false. The incorrectly captured formula is true, since every element equals itself. This gives a concrete semantic distinction between the actual free substitution and the proposed captured expression.

Must a substitution keep different free names distinct?

Suppose \(X\) has two names, and \(\sigma\) replaces both by the same free name \(y\). Does this violate the freshness condition in the proof-substitution theorem?

Show answerHide answer

No. The theorem accepts any free substitution. Its protected extension sends both old names to the left-tagged \(y\), while the newly opened eigenparameter goes to the right-tagged \(e'\). These are distinct because their tags differ, independently of the behavior on the two old names. Thus an arbitrary supplied raw or public certificate still has its corresponding transformed certificate and unchanged height. What must stay separate is a fresh parameter from the substituted side formulas; distinctness of all original free names is unnecessary.
