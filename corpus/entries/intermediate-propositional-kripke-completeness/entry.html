Fix the axiom set before choosing premises

Let \(\mathcal F\) be the native formulas with numbered atoms. Write \(\vdash_{\mathrm I}\) for the original intuitionistic deduction relation and use the earlier finite-extra-assumption relation \(\Gamma\vdash_S A\). Its union characterization is

\[\Gamma\vdash_S A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Gamma\cup S\vdash_{\mathrm I}A.\]

The two fixed axiom sets considered here are

\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal W&=\{\neg B\lor\neg\neg B:B\in\mathcal F\},\\\mathcal L&=\{(B\to C)\lor(C\to B):B,C\in\mathcal F\}.\end{aligned}\]

These give KC consequence \(\vdash_{\mathcal W}\) and LC consequence \(\vdash_{\mathcal L}\). Every compound-formula instance is included. For a class \(\mathscr K\) of nonempty preordered frames, consequence over \(\mathscr K\), written \(\Gamma\models_{\mathscr K}A\), means that every persistent valuation on every frame in \(\mathscr K\), at every world forcing all of \(\Gamma\), forces \(A\). Let \(\mathscr D\) consist of frames with directed future cones and \(\mathscr C\) of frames with linearly ordered future cones. No finiteness restriction is imposed.

Both axiom sets are consistent

Neither \(\mathcal W\) nor \(\mathcal L\) derives \(\bot\) in the original intuitionistic calculus.

Take the one-world frame \(\{*\}\), and, for definiteness, make every atom false there. Its sole future cone is directed and linearly ordered. The earlier weak excluded middle and linearity characterizations show that this world forces every member of each respective axiom set. If either set derived \(\bot\), intuitionistic soundness would make this world force falsum. This is impossible.

The argument concerns all instances at once. It supplies the consistency needed to obtain a nonempty canonical model independently of any later premise set or conclusion.

Keep exactly the prime theories containing the axioms

Fix any \(S\subseteq\mathcal F\) with \(S\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}\bot\). Let \(W^c\) be the earlier set of intuitionistic prime theories. The canonical worlds over \(S\) are

\[W_S=\{P\in W^c:S\subseteq P\}.\]

Here prime theory means deductively closed, consistent and prime for disjunction. Order \(W_S\) by literal set inclusion. The restricted canonical model \(\mathcal C_S\) has that order and valuation

\[P\Vdash_{\mathcal C_S}p_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_i\in P.\]

Inclusion is a partial order, and the valuation persists along inclusion. The specified prime extension \(\mathsf E(S,\bot)\) contains \(S\), so is a world and proves nonemptiness. Thus \(\mathcal C_S\) is a model. It is fixed once \(S\) is fixed: neither its worlds nor its valuation depends on \(\Gamma\) or \(A\).

The restriction retains every future of each retained world

The literal inclusion \(j:W_S\to W^c\), \(j(P)=P\), into all intuitionistic prime theories is a bounded morphism from \(\mathcal C_S\) to the earlier canonical model \(\mathcal C\).

It preserves inclusion and verifies exactly the same atoms. For the back condition, suppose \(j(P)\subseteq Q\), where \(Q\) is any prime theory. Since \(S\subseteq P\subseteq Q\), the same set \(Q\) belongs to \(W_S\). Use that very world as the lift: it extends \(P\) and maps to \(Q\) exactly.

Thus restricting has removed no future of a retained world. This is why implication and negation can keep their earlier meanings; an arbitrary subset of canonical worlds would not suffice.

Forcing remains exactly membership

For every consistent \(S\), every \(P\in W_S\) and every \(A\in\mathcal F\),

\[P\Vdash_{\mathcal C_S}A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\in P.\]

Bounded-morphism invariance for the specified inclusion gives \(P\Vdash_{\mathcal C_S}A\) if and only if \(j(P)\Vdash_{\mathcal C}A\). The original truth lemma identifies the latter with \(A\in P\). It covers every native constructor, including primitive negation.

Every failed extended deduction chooses a world in the fixed model

Let \(S\) be consistent. If \(\Gamma\not\vdash_S A\), the specified world

\[P_{\Gamma,A}=\mathsf E(\Gamma\cup S,A)\in W_S\]

forces all of \(\Gamma\) in \(\mathcal C_S\) and does not force \(A\).

The union characterization says \(\Gamma\cup S\not\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\). The earlier prime-extension construction therefore makes \(P_{\Gamma,A}\) prime, containing \(\Gamma\cup S\) and omitting \(A\). Containment of \(S\) puts it in the already fixed \(W_S\). The restricted truth lemma gives each claimed forcing assertion.

This generic counterworld construction has not yet proved completeness for a class of frames. We must still prove that the particular canonical frame has the required geometry.

Package a finite list of assumptions into one formula

For a finite list \(L\) of formulas, its conjunction package is defined recursively by

\[c([])=\top:=(\bot\to\bot),\qquad c(B::L)=B\land c(L).\]

Write \(|L|_{\mathrm{set}}\) for the set of formulas occurring in \(L\); repetitions are permitted. The empty package is a theorem, rather than an extra assumption that needs a premise.

The package can replace exactly its finitely many assumptions

For every \(U\subseteq\mathcal F\), formula \(A\) and finite list \(L\),

\[U\cup|L|_{\mathrm{set}}\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad U\cup\{c(L)\}\vdash_{\mathrm I}A.\]

If all entries of \(L\) belong to a deductively closed theory \(R\), then \(c(L)\in R\).

Induct on \(L\). Under the assumption \(c(B::L)\), the two conjunction eliminations give \(B\) and \(c(L)\). Continuing recursively gives every list entry. Conversely, assumptions for the list entries construct \(c(L)\): for the empty list, introduce \(\bot\to\bot\) by assuming and returning \(\bot\); at a cons step, combine the head assumption with the recursively constructed tail by conjunction introduction.

These deductions and the unchanged assumptions from \(U\) give both displayed implications by replacing assumption leaves. In the second assertion, the same introduction construction is a deduction of \(c(L)\) from \(R\), and deductive closure puts its conclusion in \(R\). This includes the empty list because every closed theory contains each intuitionistic theorem.

An inconsistent union separates the two theories by one formula

Let \(Q,R\) be deductively closed theories. If \(Q\cup R\vdash_{\mathrm I}\bot\), there is a finite list \(L\) of members of \(R\) such that, with \(B=c(L)\),

\[B\in R\qquad\text{and}\qquad\neg B\in Q.\]

Finite support supplies a finite \(D\subseteq Q\cup R\) deriving \(\bot\). List the finite set \(D\cap R\) as \(L\). Every member of \(D\) lies in \(Q\) or in this list, so weakening gives \(Q\cup|L|_{\mathrm{set}}\vdash_{\mathrm I}\bot\). The package bridge gives \(Q\cup\{B\}\vdash_{\mathrm I}\bot\). Native negation introduction discharges \(B\), giving \(Q\vdash_{\mathrm I}\neg B\), hence \(\neg B\in Q\) by closure. Every list entry belongs to \(R\), so the bridge also gives \(B\in R\).

If \(D\cap R\) is empty, this argument uses the closed proof of \(\top\) and yields \(\neg\top\in Q\); it does not require the finite set to be nonempty.

The KC canonical frame has directed future cones

If \(P,Q,R\in W_{\mathcal W}\), \(P\subseteq Q\) and \(P\subseteq R\), then \(Q\cup R\) is consistent and the specified prime theory

\[U=\mathsf E(Q\cup R,\bot)\in W_{\mathcal W}\]

contains both \(Q\) and \(R\). Consequently every future cone of \(W_{\mathcal W}\) is directed.

Suppose instead that the union derives \(\bot\). The preceding bridge gives a formula \(B\) with \(B\in R\) and \(\neg B\in Q\). Since \(P\) contains every weak excluded middle instance, \(\neg B\lor\neg\neg B\in P\). Primeness of \(P\) gives one of its disjuncts.

If \(\neg B\in P\), then \(\neg B\in R\), contradicting \(B\in R\) and consistency of \(R\). If \(\neg\neg B\in P\), then \(\neg\neg B\in Q\), contradicting \(\neg B\in Q\) and consistency of \(Q\). Both contradictions use the native negation-elimination rule. Thus the union is consistent.

The specified prime extension of that union contains both theories. It contains \(\mathcal W\) already because \(Q\) does, so it is a world of the same canonical frame. It is the required joint future. The formula \(B\) is generally compound; restricting the assumed schema to atomic instances would not justify this proof.

The LC canonical frame has linearly ordered future cones

If \(P,Q,R\in W_{\mathcal L}\), \(P\subseteq Q\) and \(P\subseteq R\), then \(Q\subseteq R\) or \(R\subseteq Q\).

If neither inclusion holds, choose \(B\in Q\setminus R\) and \(C\in R\setminus Q\). The common predecessor contains the instance \((B\to C)\lor(C\to B)\). Primeness of \(P\) puts one of these implications in \(P\).

If \(B\to C\in P\), it belongs to \(Q\). From it and \(B\in Q\), implication elimination and closure give \(C\in Q\), contrary to the choice of \(C\). If \(C\to B\in P\), the same reasoning in \(R\) gives \(B\in R\), contrary to the choice of \(B\). Hence the theories are comparable. This proves local comparability; theories without a common predecessor are not being compared.

The two frame classes validate the added deductions

For all \(\Gamma,A\),

\[\begin{aligned}\Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal W}A&\Longrightarrow\Gamma\models_{\mathscr D}A,\\\Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal L}A&\Longrightarrow\Gamma\models_{\mathscr C}A.\end{aligned}\]

The respective directedness and linearity characterizations make every member of the chosen axiom set valid on every frame in its class, for all persistent valuations. The already established soundness with added assumptions therefore applies to the original deduction and proves the assertion.

Both extensions are strongly complete for their frame conditions

For every premise set \(\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal F\) and every formula \(A\),

\[\begin{aligned}\Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal W}A&\Longleftrightarrow\Gamma\models_{\mathscr D}A,\\\Gamma\vdash_{\mathcal L}A&\Longleftrightarrow\Gamma\models_{\mathscr C}A.\end{aligned}\]

For each extension one fixed canonical model, chosen independently of \(\Gamma,A\), supplies a counterworld to every failed deduction.

Soundness gives the forward implications. For the converse in KC, if \(\Gamma\not\vdash_{\mathcal W}A\), the specified construction supplies the world \(\mathsf E(\Gamma\cup\mathcal W,A)\) in the fixed \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal W}\), forcing \(\Gamma\) and failing \(A\). The directedness theorem proves that this model's frame belongs to \(\mathscr D\), so the world refutes \(\Gamma\models_{\mathscr D}A\). The same argument for LC uses \(\mathcal C_{\mathcal L}\) and local linearity.

Consistency of the two axiom sets made both fixed models nonempty before any conclusion was chosen. The proof works without enumerating the premises: only the numbered language and the earlier prime-extension construction are countable.

Each semantic consequence uses only finitely many premises

For \((S,\mathscr K)=(\mathcal W,\mathscr D)\) or \((\mathcal L,\mathscr C)\),

\[\Gamma\models_{\mathscr K}A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\exists D\subseteq\Gamma\;\bigl(D\text{ finite and }D\models_{\mathscr K}A\bigr).\]

Completeness gives \(\Gamma\cup S\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\). Finite support supplies finite \(F\subseteq\Gamma\cup S\) deriving \(A\). Put \(D=F\cap\Gamma\). Every member of \(F\) belongs to \(D\cup S\), so weakening and the union characterization give \(D\vdash_S A\). Soundness yields the finite semantic consequence. Conversely, if a world forces \(\Gamma\), it forces \(D\subseteq\Gamma\); apply the assumed consequence for \(D\).

This bounds the premises used by one consequence, not the number of worlds needed for a counterexample.

Finite satisfiability gives a world for all premises

For either pair \((S,\mathscr K)\) above, \(\Gamma\) is satisfiable at a world of a model on a frame in \(\mathscr K\) if and only if \(\Gamma\not\vdash_S\bot\). Moreover, this satisfiability holds if and only if every finite subset of \(\Gamma\) is satisfiable in that class.

A world forcing \(\Gamma\) rules out \(\Gamma\vdash_S\bot\) by soundness and impossibility of forcing falsum. Conversely, if this deduction fails, the specified counterworld for conclusion \(\bot\) forces all premises in the appropriate canonical model. Its frame belongs to \(\mathscr K\).

Satisfiability passes to subsets. For the other compactness direction, suppose all finite subsets are satisfiable. If \(\Gamma\vdash_S\bot\), the finite-support argument of gives finite \(D\subseteq\Gamma\) with \(D\vdash_S\bot\). Its assumed satisfying world would force falsum by soundness, a contradiction. Thus the deduction fails and the characterization gives a world satisfying all of \(\Gamma\).

Countermodels may have a least world

A failed KC deduction has a countermodel with a least world and with every pair of worlds admitting a common upper bound. A failed LC deduction has a countermodel with a least world and a linearly ordered frame. These assertions allow infinite premise sets and infinite frames.

Take the specified canonical counterworld \(P\), and restrict its model to the generated future cone \(\mathord\uparrow P\). Its least world is \(P\) itself, and generated-submodel truth preservation retains all premises and failure of the conclusion there.

In KC, any \(Q,R\) in the cone have the common predecessor \(P\). Canonical directedness supplies \(U\supseteq Q,R\); since \(P\subseteq Q\subseteq U\), that same \(U\) still lies in the restricted cone. Thus every pair joins there. In LC, local comparability above \(P\) compares any two worlds in the cone, so its inclusion order is linear. Antisymmetry is inherited from inclusion. No finite bound on either cone has been proved.

Why prove the geometry after the truth lemma?

The restricted truth lemma makes every added axiom true at every world of its canonical model. Would that fact alone establish that the frame validates the schema under every valuation?

Show answerHide answer

No. The earlier matching fork model forces every weak excluded middle instance at all its worlds. Its bounded morphism to a two-world chain transfers all these formulas for the specified matching valuation. Nevertheless the two fork leaves have no common upper bound, so the frame is not in \(\mathscr D\). A valuation verifying one atom on just one leaf refutes that atom's weak excluded middle at the root.

Thus truth of all instances in one valuation does not imply validity of the frame. The canonical directedness and comparability proofs above establish properties of the order itself; the earlier frame correspondences then apply to every persistent valuation. This additional argument is what puts the canonical countermodel in the required semantic class.

Do the intuitionistic finite countermodel methods finish the argument?

Can we infer finite countermodels for KC or LC just by applying the earlier intuitionistic finite-model or finite-tree construction?

Show answerHide answer

Those conclusions require an additional preservation argument. For example, the four-world diamond with a greatest world validates every weak excluded middle instance. Its six-path strict unraveling has distinct terminal paths \([r,u,t]\) and \([r,v,t]\). Both extend the root path \([r]\), but neither has an extension and they have no common upper bound. The unraveled frame is therefore not directed above its root.

The specified last-world bounded morphism still preserves all formulas in the pulled-back valuation. It does not make every other valuation on the path frame validate weak excluded middle. In particular, verifying \(p_0\) exactly above one of those terminal paths gives the earlier nonjoining-futures countervaluation.

Likewise ordinary intuitionistic finite-model existence guarantees neither local directedness nor local linearity of the resulting frame. To obtain a finite-model theorem or decision procedure for either extension one must prove the relevant condition survives a suitable construction. Strong completeness and the two compactness results here make no such claim.
