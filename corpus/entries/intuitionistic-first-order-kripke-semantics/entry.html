Transport objects as information grows

Fix a language \(L\). A first-order Kripke model consists of a nonempty preordered set \(W\), a nonempty \(L\)-structure \(M_w\) with domain \(D_w\) for each \(w\in W\), and a map \(\tau_{wv}:D_w\to D_v\) whenever \(w\leq v\). The transition maps satisfy

\[\tau_{ww}=\mathrm{id}_{D_w},\qquad \tau_{vu}\circ\tau_{wv}=\tau_{wu}\quad(w\leq v\leq u).\]

Each transition is a homomorphism: it commutes with every interpreted function, including constants, and preserves every true relation instance. For a function symbol \(f\) and relation symbol \(R\), this means

\[\tau_{wv}(f^{M_w}(\bar d))=f^{M_v}(\tau_{wv}\bar d),\qquad R^{M_w}(\bar d)\Longrightarrow R^{M_v}(\tau_{wv}\bar d).\]

The relation need not be reflected, and the map need not be injective. Logical equality is interpreted as actual equality of values: every function preserves a true equality, even when it identifies previously distinct values. Forcing a negated equality requires the equality to be false at every future world; present inequality is insufficient.

Terms and assignments commute with transition

Use the full scoped syntax. For a formula with \(n\) available slots, an assignment at \(w\) is a function \(a:X\sqcup\operatorname{Fin}(n)\to D_w\). Its transported assignment is \(a^{wv}=\tau_{wv}\circ a\). Write \(a[d]\) for extension by a value \(d\) for the newest last slot. Term values satisfy

\[\tau_{wv}(\llbracket t\rrbracket_{w,a})=\llbracket t\rrbracket_{v,a^{wv}},\qquad (a[d])^{wv}=(a^{wv})[\tau_{wv}(d)].\]

Moreover, for an all-variable term substitution \(\sigma\), evaluating \(t[\sigma]\) at \(a\) equals evaluating \(t\) at the assignment \(x\mapsto\llbracket\sigma(x)\rrbracket_{w,a}\).

For a variable, the first equality is the definition of transported assignment. For \(f(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\), commute the transition through \(f^{M_w}\), then apply the induction hypotheses to the argument values. This includes a constant, whose tuple has length zero. The substitution equality is the term substitution law in the structure at \(w\).

For the assignment equality check an old free name, an old slot, and the new last slot. The first two use \(\tau_{wv}\circ a\); the last uses \(\tau_{wv}(d)\). Transition coherence also gives \((a^{wv})^{vu}=a^{wu}\) and \(a^{ww}=a\). These identities ensure that an object's later value is independent of how the route to that world is divided.

Retain the constructive connective clauses

For a full scoped formula \(A\), the relation forcing at an assignment, written \(w\Vdash A[a]\), is defined recursively. Falsum is never forced. Equality \(s=t\) is forced exactly when its two term values at \(w,a\) agree; a relation atom is forced exactly when its tuple of term values belongs to \(R^{M_w}\). Conjunction and disjunction use their respective conjunction and disjunction of forcing statements at the current world.

The remaining propositional clauses use every future world:

\[w\Vdash(A\to B)[a]\iff\forall v\geq w\;(v\Vdash A[a^{wv}]\Longrightarrow v\Vdash B[a^{wv}]).\]
\[w\Vdash\neg A[a]\iff\forall v\geq w\;\neg(v\Vdash A[a^{wv}]).\]

These are the constructive propositional clauses with assignments transported along the frame. In particular, failure to force an atom now does not mean that its negation is forced. Native negation is retained as its own formula constructor.

Existence is current, while universality includes future objects

For a scoped body \(B\) with one more available slot, the quantifier forcing clauses are

\[w\Vdash(\exists xB)[a]\iff\exists d\in D_w\;w\Vdash B[a[d]].\]
\[w\Vdash(\forall xB)[a]\iff\forall v\geq w\;\forall d\in D_v\;v\Vdash B[(a^{wv})[d]].\]

The existential witness belongs to the present domain. The universal clause tests every future domain, using the transported old values and then the newly chosen object. It is not enough to inspect all objects presently available. For a complete formula, an assignment \(a:X\to D_w\) supplies its free names; its bound-slot component is empty.

The constructive quantifier laws and their countermodels show which familiar classical rearrangements these future-world clauses permit.

Every forced formula survives transition

For every full scoped formula \(A\), every \(w\leq v\), and every assignment \(a\) at \(w\), forcing \(w\Vdash A[a]\) implies \(v\Vdash A[a^{wv}]\).

Induct on \(A\), with the world and assignment arbitrary. Equality persists because applying a function to equal values preserves equality, and identifies the transported term values. Relation atoms use the homomorphism condition and the same identity. Falsum has no forced instance; conjunction and disjunction use their component induction hypotheses.

For implication, a future \(u\geq v\) is also above \(w\). Apply the original implication there; coherence identifies the twice-transported assignment with \(a^{wu}\). Negation uses the same argument to exclude its subformula at every such \(u\).

For an existential, transport its present witness \(d\) to \(\tau_{wv}(d)\). The body induction hypothesis and the extension identity give the required body forcing at \(v\). For a universal, take any \(u\geq v\) and any \(d\in D_u\). The original universal clause already supplies this body instance, since \(w\leq u\); coherence again identifies its old assignment. These cases prove persistence without requiring injective or surjective transition maps.

Evaluate a syntactic substitution in the same model

Let \(A\) have free names \(X\) and \(n\) available slots, and let \(\sigma:X\sqcup\operatorname{Fin}(n)\to\operatorname{Term}(Y\sqcup\operatorname{Fin}(m))\) be a scope substitution. At every world and target assignment \(a\),

\[w\Vdash A\langle\sigma\rangle[a]\iff w\Vdash A[x\mapsto\llbracket\sigma(x)\rrbracket_{w,a}].\]

Induct on the actual full tree, with worlds and assignments arbitrary. Atomic clauses use term substitution; the current conjunction and disjunction clauses use their component equivalences. For implication or negation, take a future world \(v\). The term transport identity says that the values of \(\sigma(x)\) evaluated at \(a^{wv}\) are exactly the transported values evaluated at \(a\). Thus the component induction hypotheses identify the two future forcing conditions.

Under either binder, syntax uses the protected scope lift. Evaluating that lift under an assignment extended by \(d\) gives the evaluated old substitution extended by the same \(d\): an old variable uses its replacement term with its slots included in the larger scope, and the new last slot remains the variable assigned \(d\). For existence, apply this equality at the current world and the same witness. For universality, apply it at each future world and each object in its domain, using term transport first. The witness sets and future worlds agree on both sides, so these are equivalences in both directions.

Free substitution and free renaming are special cases. The theorem interprets the specified syntactic operation on the shared syntax; it does not replace the model or introduce a different formula language.

Read an opened parameter and an instance at their actual values

Let \(B\) be a one-slot full body, \(a:X\to D_w\), \(d\in D_w\), and \(t\) a term over \(X\). Opening and instantiation satisfy

\[w\Vdash\operatorname{open}(B)[a\sqcup d]\iff w\Vdash B[a[d]].\]
\[w\Vdash B(t)[a]\iff w\Vdash B[a[\llbracket t\rrbracket_{w,a}]].\]

For every complete \(C\), its left-renamed copy satisfies \(w\Vdash C^\uparrow[a\sqcup d]\iff w\Vdash C[a]\).

Apply to the scope maps defining opening and instantiation. In the first map, the opened slot is the right-tagged free parameter, whose assigned value is \(d\). In the second it is the included term \(t\), with exactly its displayed value. All old names keep their values. Left renaming never uses the new right tag, which proves the final equivalence. The same reasoning works at every future world because the term and assignment transport identities hold there.

Test a premise set at each pointed model and assignment

A world and assignment force a premise set \(T\) of complete formulas when \(w\Vdash B[a]\) for every \(B\in T\). A pointed first-order countermodel to \(T\Rightarrow A\) is a model, a world and an assignment forcing all of \(T\) and failing to force \(A\).

The Kripke consequence \(T\models_{\mathrm K}A\) requires preservation of premises at every such model, world and assignment. A formula is Kripke valid when this holds with the empty premise set. These definitions quantify over the varying-domain models above, with equality interpreted by values.

Every intuitionistic raw rule preserves forcing

For every supplied raw intuitionistic derivation of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\), every model, world and assignment forcing \(\Gamma\) forces \(A\). This holds with logical equality enabled or disabled.

Induct on the supplied finite tree, with the model's world and its assignment arbitrary. Assumptions use the premise hypothesis. Conjunction rules, disjunction introductions and disjunction elimination use the current forcing clauses; in an elimination branch insert the chosen forced disjunct among the assumptions.

For implication introduction, consider an arbitrary future \(v\geq w\) forcing the antecedent at the transported assignment. Persistence supplies all ambient assumptions there, so the branch induction hypothesis supplies the consequent. Implication elimination uses its future condition at \(w\) itself. Negation introduction has the same future argument with an impossible falsum conclusion. Negation elimination also tests the current world, and falsum elimination has no forced premise. Classical contradiction is absent from the intuitionistic rule list.

Universal elimination takes the universal clause at the present world and the value of its specified term, then uses . Existential introduction uses that same value as its present witness. For universal introduction, take arbitrary \(v\geq w\) and \(d\in D_v\). Persistence transports each ambient assumption to \(v\). Assign \(d\) to the protected fresh name; the opening and left-renaming identities turn the branch induction hypothesis into the required body instance. The fresh name occurs in no ambient assumption.

For existential elimination choose the present witness \(d\) provided by its first premise. Extend the current assignment by \(d\). The opened body is forced by the opening identity, and every lifted ambient assumption is forced by the renaming identity. Apply the branch induction hypothesis. Its conclusion is a left-renamed copy of the original conclusion, so the same identity removes the fresh parameter. This is where the rule's restriction on the concluding formula is used.

Equality reflexivity is equality of a value with itself. In replacement, the equality premise identifies the two specified term values. The instantiation identities therefore give the same forcing condition for the two body instances, even when that body contains quantifiers or future implications. The second premise supplies the required conclusion. No reflection of equality along transitions has been used.

Certificates and arbitrary-premise proofs are sound

Every intuitionistic certificate preserves forcing from its finite context, and every arbitrary-premise consequence satisfies \(T\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\Longrightarrow T\models_{\mathrm K}A\), in either equality mode. Consequently a pointed countermodel rules out the corresponding deduction.

At the chosen world \(w\), take one element of its nonempty domain and assign it to all auxiliary reserve names. Keep the original assignment on the other summand. The certificate is an actual raw tree, so applies. Left-renaming removes all reserve names from its end formulas. No coherent choice of a new element at every world is required: future assignments in the raw argument are transports of this chosen assignment.

An arbitrary-premise certificate records a finite allowed context contained in \(T\). Its members are forced by the premise-set hypothesis, so finite-context soundness applies. If a conclusion failed in a specified pointed model, either soundness statement would contradict that failure. These arguments establish soundness and its countermodel consequence; they do not assert a completeness theorem.

A single world recovers ordinary first-order truth

For every nonempty classical \(L\)-structure \(M\), let its singleton Kripke model have one world, domain \(M\), its given interpretations and identity transition. At its sole world, forcing any full scoped formula is equivalent to its ordinary classical interpretation under the same assignment.

The identity maps obey coherence and the homomorphism conditions. Induct on the full formula. Term values and atoms agree literally; conjunction, disjunction and falsum have the same clauses. For implication or negation, the only future is the current world, so the clause reduces to the corresponding ordinary condition. Universal forcing ranges over exactly the same domain elements as ordinary universal truth; existence does likewise. The body induction hypotheses identify these quantified conditions. Thus Kripke validity implies validity in every nonempty classical structure.

Connect nullary relations to the shared propositional language

The nullary propositional reduct of a first-order model uses the same frame, takes the nullary relation symbols as atoms, and makes \(R\) true at \(w\) exactly when its unique empty tuple is in \(R^{M_w}\). The homomorphism condition makes this atom valuation persistent.

Its formula inclusion replaces each native propositional atom \(R\) by the full relation formula \(R()\), retaining falsum, primitive negation and every propositional connective. It introduces no quantifier, equality or variable. Conversely, a propositional Kripke model supplies a first-order model in the language with just its nullary atoms and no function symbols: use a one-element domain at every world, identity transitions, and its atom valuations as the relation interpretations.

The propositional inclusion preserves the exact forcing clauses

For every model, world and assignment, forcing the included native propositional formula is equivalent to propositional forcing in the specified reduct. The model lifted from a propositional model likewise forces each included formula exactly when the original model does.

Induct on the native propositional formula. A nullary atom has the same empty-tuple interpretation in the reduct and the first-order model, independently of the assignment. Falsum, conjunction and disjunction use identical clauses. For implication and native negation, the future worlds are literally the same, and the component induction hypotheses apply at each future world and its transported assignment. This proves the first equivalence. In the specified lifted model the reduct's valuation is the original valuation, so the same induction proves the second. This is a bridge for actual included formulas and the specified models.

Let a later world contain one additional object

Define a two-world growing-domain model with \(0<1\), \(D_0=\{0\}\), \(D_1=\{0,1\}\), and inclusion transition. Its language has two unary relation symbols \(P,Q\) and no functions. At either world put \(P(d)\) exactly when \(d=0\), and \(Q(d)\) exactly when \(d=1\).

Both domains are nonempty. Inclusion fixes each represented integer, so it preserves the two relations and obeys identity and composition. Thus these data satisfy every model condition. Write \(E=\exists xQ(x)\), a complete formula with no free names.

Can either quantifier be checked at the wrong worlds?

In the specified growing-domain model, does the root force \(\forall xP(x)\), since every root object has property \(P\)? Does it force \(E\), since a future world has a \(Q\)-witness?

Neither conclusion is forced. The universal clause at the root includes world \(1\) and its object \(1\), where \(P(1)\) fails. The root's own domain contains only zero, where \(Q(0)\) fails, so the existential clause supplies no current witness. At world \(1\), however, \(E\) is forced by witness one.

The root does force \(\exists xP(x)\), with witness zero, and every present object satisfies \(P\). The failure of its universal formula comes from an additional future object, while the failure of \(E\) comes from the absence of a present witness. These are the two different time directions in the quantifier clauses.

The growing model rejects classical rules for a quantified formula

For the specified \(E\), the root forces \(\neg\neg E\), but forces neither \(E\) nor \(\neg E\). Therefore it fails to force both \(E\lor\neg E\) and \(\neg\neg E\to E\). Neither displayed formula is a closed theorem of intuitionistic first-order deduction, with equality enabled or disabled.

The preceding calculation gives failure of \(E\) at the root and truth of \(E\) at world one. That future witness excludes root forcing of \(\neg E\). Every future of the root is either zero or one, and each has world one as a future forcing \(E\). Hence neither future can force \(\neg E\): its own negation clause would exclude that verified future. This is exactly root forcing of \(\neg\neg E\).

Both disjuncts of the excluded-middle formula fail at the root. The root itself also refutes the implication from double negation to \(E\). Certificate soundness would force the conclusion of any closed intuitionistic proof there, contradicting these failures. Each formula is classically valid by the ordinary laws for an arbitrary proposition, so the example isolates the constructive rule restriction even when the tested proposition contains a genuine existential quantifier.
