Distinguish free names from available bound slots

Fix a first-order language \(L\). A scoped parameter is a full formula \(R\in\mathrm{BF}_L(X,n)\), where \(X\) is the free-name set and \(n\) is the number of available bound slots. We use the native full syntax: equality and relation atoms, falsum, primitive negation, conjunction, disjunction, implication and both quantifiers. In particular, no constructive connective is replaced by its classical abbreviation. Complete formulas have \(n=0\).

A binder adds a newest slot while retaining all the old ones. Let \(\iota_n:X\sqcup\mathrm{Fin}(n)\to X\sqcup\mathrm{Fin}(n+1)\) include every free name unchanged and every old slot as the same old slot. The protected lift of a scoped parameter is

\[R^+=R\langle\operatorname{var}\circ\iota_n\rangle.\]

Here \(\langle\sigma\rangle\) is the established capture-free scope map. Thus \(R^+\) cannot refer to the newly added slot. This is distinct from the free-name lift \(C^\uparrow=C[\mathrm{inl}]\), which takes a complete formula over \(X\) to one over \(X\sqcup\{e\}\). The former adds a bound slot; the latter renames free names into an extended name set.

Translate with a parameter protected at each binder

For \(R,A\in\mathrm{BF}_L(X,n)\), the first-order Friedman translation \(F_R(A)\) extends the propositional translation by the recursion

\[\begin{aligned}F_R(\bot)&=R,\\F_R(s=t)&=(s=t)\lor R,\\F_R(P(\bar t))&=P(\bar t)\lor R,\\F_R(\neg A)&=F_R(A)\to R.\end{aligned}\]
\[\begin{aligned}F_R(A\land B)&=F_R(A)\land F_R(B),\\F_R(A\lor B)&=F_R(A)\lor F_R(B),\\F_R(A\to B)&=F_R(A)\to F_R(B),\\F_R(\forall xB)&=\forall x\,F_{R^+}(B),\\F_R(\exists xB)&=\exists x\,F_{R^+}(B).\end{aligned}\]

Every atomic term tree is retained. The parameter is inserted whole, without recursively translating it. The recursive calls concern strict children of \(A\), with the parameter lifted at either binder, so the definition terminates for every full formula. Primitive negation becomes implication into the parameter; it does not retain its negation constructor. Below, \(\widehat B_R\) abbreviates the one-slot body \(F_{R^+}(B)\) when \(R\) is complete and \(B\) has one available slot.

A free parameter must remain free under a quantifier

Let \(P\) be unary and let \(y\) be a free name. With parameter \(R=P(y)\), the translation of \(\forall xP(x)\) is

\[F_{P(y)}(\forall xP(x))=\forall x\,(P(x)\lor P(y)),\]

where the last \(y\) remains free. Capturing that occurrence would change the formula: the displayed formula and \(\forall y(P(y)\lor P(y))\) have different truth values in a two-element structure.

In the one-slot body, \(P(x)\) uses the new bound slot, while \(R^+\) uses the left-tagged original name \(y\). The atomic and universal clauses give the displayed equality. Equivalently, if the source binder is printed with the same letter as the parameter, first print its bound variable as a fresh \(z\); the result is \(\forall z(P(z)\lor P(y))\), not a formula that binds the inserted \(y\).

For the separation, take domain \(\{0,1\}\), let \(P\) hold exactly at \(0\), and assign \(y=0\). The protected formula is true: at either value of its bound variable, its right disjunct holds. The captured formula is false at the bound value \(1\), where both disjuncts fail. Thus parameter protection expresses a mathematical condition, rather than a choice of typography.

Apply every scope map to the parameter as well

Let \(R,A\in\mathrm{BF}_L(X,n)\), and let \(\sigma:X\sqcup\mathrm{Fin}(n)\to\operatorname{Term}_L(Y\sqcup\mathrm{Fin}(m))\) be any scope substitution. Then there is literal formula equality

\[F_R(A)\langle\sigma\rangle=F_{R\langle\sigma\rangle}(A\langle\sigma\rangle).\]

In particular, free term substitution, free-name renaming, opening all slots and simultaneous slot instantiation satisfy this identity, with the same operation applied to \(R\). The claim concerns substitution of terms into variable positions, not arbitrary replacement of relation atoms by formulas.

First, protection commutes with the protected extension \(\sigma^+\) beneath a binder:

\[(R^+)\langle\sigma^+\rangle=(R\langle\sigma\rangle)^+.\]

Indeed, an old variable first included into the larger scope is sent by \(\sigma^+\) to its old \(\sigma\)-term with every variable included into the larger target scope. This is exactly the other composite. The scope-composition law then gives the formula equality, including inside any binders of \(R\).

Now induct on \(A\), keeping \(R\), both scopes and \(\sigma\) arbitrary. At falsum both sides are \(R\langle\sigma\rangle\). At an equality or relation atom, both sides substitute into the same original terms and append that same parameter as the right disjunct. Conjunction, disjunction and implication use the two child identities. At native negation they use the child identity as antecedent and \(R\langle\sigma\rangle\) as consequent.

For a universal or existential, scope mapping uses \(\sigma^+\) in the body. Apply the body induction hypothesis with parameter \(R^+\), then the protection identity above. Both sides have the same outer quantifier and identical bodies. These are all constructors. Specializing \(\sigma\) to each stated scope map gives the consequences.

Unlike term substitution, arbitrary propositional formula substitution can replace an atom by a compound formula whose translation is different. That counterexample does not contradict this identity, whose term substitutions retain the outer equality or relation constructor.

Open or instantiate the translated body without changing its parameter

Let \(R\) be complete over \(X\), let \(B\in\mathrm{BF}_L(X,1)\), and let \(t\) be a term over \(X\). For the body \(\widehat B_R=F_{R^+}(B)\),

\[\operatorname{open}(\widehat B_R)=F_{R^\uparrow}(\operatorname{open}(B)),\qquad \widehat B_R(t)=F_R(B(t)).\]

For arbitrary scoped \(R,A\), opening and simultaneous instantiation more generally satisfy

\[\operatorname{open}(F_R(A))=F_{\operatorname{open}(R)}(\operatorname{open}(A)),\]
\[(F_R(A))(\bar t)=F_{R(\bar t)}(A(\bar t)).\]

Apply to the opening or instance map. In the special one-slot case, \(R^+\) contains no new-slot occurrence. Opening that slot therefore gives precisely \(R^\uparrow\), while replacing it by \(t\) gives precisely \(R\). The old free names retain their tags in either calculation. These parameter equalities identify the two specialized formulas. The same argument allows terms containing original free names: none of their occurrences is captured beneath a binder.

Translate all allowed assumptions by their finite image

For a complete parameter \(R\), define the translated context and translated theory by

\[\Gamma^{[R]}=\{F_R(C):C\in\Gamma\},\qquad T^{[R]}=\{F_R(C):C\in T\},\]

where \(\Gamma\) is finite and \(T\) may be arbitrary. They satisfy

\[(\Gamma,C)^{[R]}=\Gamma^{[R]},F_R(C),\qquad (\Gamma\cup\Delta)^{[R]}=\Gamma^{[R]}\cup\Delta^{[R]}.\]

For every free renaming \(f:X\to Y\),

\[(\Gamma[f])^{[R[f]]}=(\Gamma^{[R]})[f].\]

Thus \((\Gamma^\uparrow)^{[R^\uparrow]}=(\Gamma^{[R]})^\uparrow\). Repeated image formulas are identified as members of a finite set.

The image of an insertion is the old image with the image of the inserted formula; the image of a union is the union of the images. For renaming, a member on the left is \(F_{R[f]}(C[f])\) for some \(C\in\Gamma\). By , this is exactly \(F_R(C)[f]\), a member of the right. The same reasoning backwards gives equality. Taking \(f=\mathrm{inl}\) gives the protected-context identity.

An available term lets the parameter prove every translated formula

Fix either logical-equality mode. Given a complete parameter \(R\), a complete formula \(A\), and a supplied term \(r\in\operatorname{Term}_L(X)\), there is a specified raw intuitionistic derivation

\[K_{R,r}(A):\{R\}\Rightarrow F_R(A).\]

Consequently a supplied raw proof of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow R\) gives one of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow F_R(A)\), by actual assumption replacement.

Use strong induction on the logical depth of the original \(A\), with name set, parameter and term arbitrary. At falsum use the assumption \(R\). At either kind of atom introduce the right disjunct of \(\text{atom}\lor R\). For conjunction combine the two child proofs; for disjunction take the left child proof and introduce that disjunct. For implication, weaken the consequent child's proof to allow the translated antecedent and discharge it. For primitive negation, assume its translated child, retain \(R\), and discharge to obtain the required implication into \(R\).

If \(A=\forall xB\), pass to names \(X\sqcup\{e\}\). Apply induction to \(\operatorname{open}(B)\), parameter \(R^\uparrow\), and the renamed term \(r^\uparrow\). It produces the proof from \(\{R^\uparrow\}\) of \(F_{R^\uparrow}(\operatorname{open}(B))=\operatorname{open}(\widehat B_R)\). Native universal introduction concludes \(\forall x\widehat B_R\). Its fresh name is absent from the lifted parameter assumption.

If \(A=\exists xB\), apply induction to \(B(r)\), with the same \(R,r\). Its conclusion is \(F_R(B(r))=\widehat B_R(r)\). Existential introduction uses the supplied term \(r\) and concludes \(\exists x\widehat B_R\). Opening and instantiation preserve logical depth, so both quantified recursive calls have smaller original depth. These clauses determine a finite raw tree for every \(A\); they use no equality or classical rule. The existential clause explains why an actual available term is required.

Translate every propositional rule of the native tree

With a supplied term \(r\), all propositional constructors of a raw intuitionistic native derivation have specified translated constructions. The context is always \(\Gamma^{[R]}\) and the conclusion is always translated by \(F_R\).

An assumption \(C\in\Gamma\) becomes its image assumption \(F_R(C)\in\Gamma^{[R]}\). Conjunction introduction combines the two translated children; either projection projects the translated conjunction. Either disjunction introduction keeps its chosen disjunct. Disjunction elimination retains its case split: its major premise becomes \(F_R(B)\lor F_R(C)\), and its two translated branches derive \(F_R(D)\) from the corresponding inserted translated assumptions. The insertion identity supplies exactly the native branch contexts.

Implication introduction discharges the translated antecedent, and implication elimination applies the translated function to the translated argument. Primitive negation introduction is different: its translated child proves \(R\) under the extra assumption \(F_R(B)\), so implication introduction gives \(F_R(B)\to R\). Primitive negation elimination combines the translated \(B\) with that implication by implication elimination; its conclusion \(R\) is the translation of falsum.

Finally, a falsum-elimination premise becomes a proof of \(R\). Feed that very proof to for the requested translated conclusion. This replaces the old explosion step without treating \(R\) as actual falsum. Thus every native propositional rule has been covered, including the two primitive negation rules.

Translate witness rules and both protected branches

For complete \(R\) and available term \(r\), all four native quantifier constructors transform with the same witness terms and the same protected eigenparameter branches.

For universal elimination at its supplied term \(t\), apply native elimination to the translated major \(\forall x\widehat B_R\). The result \(\widehat B_R(t)=F_R(B(t))\) is exactly the translated original conclusion. For existential introduction, the translated child has this same instance formula; introduce \(\exists x\widehat B_R\) at that very \(t\). Neither clause chooses another original witness.

For universal introduction, recursively translate its actual premise over \(X\sqcup\{e\}\), using parameter \(R^\uparrow\) and available term \(r^\uparrow\). Its context is \((\Gamma^\uparrow)^{[R^\uparrow]}=(\Gamma^{[R]})^\uparrow\), and its conclusion is \(F_{R^\uparrow}(\operatorname{open}(B))=\operatorname{open}(\widehat B_R)\). Native universal introduction now gives the translated universal. The parameter contributes only original names, so it has not introduced \(e\) into a side assumption.

For existential elimination, the translated major is \(\exists x\widehat B_R\). Translate its supplied branch with that same lifted parameter and term. Its context and conclusion are precisely

\[(\Gamma^{[R]})^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(\widehat B_R)\Rightarrow F_R(C)^\uparrow.\]

Here the context identities translate both the ambient assumptions and the temporary witness assumption; the renaming identity translates the branch conclusion. Native existential elimination therefore concludes \(F_R(C)\). The fresh name occurs neither in the lifted final conclusion nor in the lifted ambient context, so the witness has been discharged. The formulas used in all four clauses are identified by and .

Split translated equality before using replacement

If logical equality is enabled, reflexivity and replacement also have specified translated constructions. If it is disabled, no equality rule is introduced by any construction in this entry.

A reflexivity leaf at \(t\) becomes the native reflexivity proof of \(t=t\), followed by left disjunction introduction, giving \((t=t)\lor R\).

Suppose replacement originally takes \(s=t\) and \(B(s)\) to \(B(t)\). The translated children give

\[(s=t)\lor R,\qquad F_R(B(s))=\widehat B_R(s).\]

Perform disjunction elimination on the first proof. Under its left assumption \(s=t\), weaken the second proof and apply native equality replacement with the actual full body \(\widehat B_R\). This yields \(\widehat B_R(t)=F_R(B(t))\). The two instance identities are essential: they hold because the parameter has no occurrence of the body's distinguished slot.

Under the right assumption \(R\), use , with the supplied \(r\), to get \(F_R(B(t))\). Both branches have the same conclusion, so the case rule closes. This construction uses the original equality mode and accounts for the extra parameter disjunct; applying equality replacement directly to \((s=t)\lor R\) would not be a native rule.

Transform the supplied raw intuitionistic proof tree

For either equality mode, complete parameter \(R\) and supplied term \(r\) over \(X\), there is a specified operation on actual raw intuitionistic trees

\[\mathcal T_{R,r}:\operatorname{Derivation}_{\mathrm I}(\Gamma,A)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Derivation}_{\mathrm I}(\Gamma^{[R]},F_R(A)).\]

The output keeps the same logical-equality mode and translates every open assumption.

Recurse on the supplied finite tree, with \(R,r\) arbitrary at every name set. For each propositional constructor use the corresponding clause of . For the four quantifier constructors use , passing \(R^\uparrow,r^\uparrow\) to each protected child. For either equality constructor use . The classical-contradiction constructor is unavailable because the classical flag is false.

Every recursive call concerns an actual immediate premise of the input. Parameter explosion is the separately defined formula recursion, not a new inference rule. Formula and context identities only identify endpoints of the constructed trees. Thus these clauses cover the entire native rule grammar and return definite proof data from the supplied proof, rather than selecting a proof from an existence theorem.

Use the public reserve on the very same input certificate

For every complete parameter \(R\), a supplied intuitionistic public certificate \(p:\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) gives an actual certificate of \(\Gamma^{[R]}\Rightarrow F_R(A)\), in the same equality mode. No term over the original name set is required.

Unpack exactly the native public certificate supplied as \(p\). It is a raw tree over \(X\sqcup U\), where \(U=\{u_0,u_1,\ldots\}\), with endpoints \(\Gamma[\mathrm{inl}]\Rightarrow A[\mathrm{inl}]\). Apply the raw operation to that very tree, using parameter \(R[\mathrm{inl}]\) and term \(\operatorname{var}(u_0)\):

\[\mathcal T_{R[\mathrm{inl}],u_0}(p).\]

By the formula and context renaming identities, its endpoints are precisely \((\Gamma^{[R]})[\mathrm{inl}]\Rightarrow F_R(A)[\mathrm{inl}]\). Regard this output as the required public certificate. These equalities are the only endpoint transports. The countable public reserve and the one fresh name added inside a raw quantifier branch have distinct roles; neither is replaced by another proof representation.

The parameter's original free names remain in the left component, while \(u_0\) supplies the term needed by explosion's existential branch. The concluding formulas contain no reserve name. The construction therefore works even when the original language and displayed names provide no term.

Retain the actual finite support inside the image theory

Let \(T\) be any set of full formulas. A supplied intuitionistic theory certificate of \(T\vdash A\), with actual finite support \(\Gamma\subseteq T\), transforms into a theory certificate of

\[T^{[R]}\vdash F_R(A),\]

whose finite support is exactly \(\Gamma^{[R]}\) and whose proof is the public transformation of the supplied proof. In particular, \(T\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\) implies \(T^{[R]}\vdash_{\mathrm I}F_R(A)\).

A theory certificate consists of a finite allowed context \(\Gamma\), evidence that each member belongs to \(T\), and a public proof from \(\Gamma\). Take the finite image \(\Gamma^{[R]}\). If \(D\) belongs to it, then \(D=F_R(C)\) for some \(C\in\Gamma\subseteq T\); hence \(D\in T^{[R]}\). Apply to the actual proof field. These three fields specify the output certificate, with no appeal to the existence of an unrelated certificate. Taking existence gives the consequence assertion.

There is also a precise way to use another theory \(S\): if an actual \(S\)-certificate of \(F_R(C)\) is supplied for every \(C\in T\), replace the translated assumption leaves with those certificates. Only members of the finite input support occur; union their finite supports, weaken to that union, and perform the established proof replacement. The result is an actual \(S\)-certificate of \(F_R(A)\). This additional hypothesis concerns translated axioms, not merely the original axioms.

Can the raw construction omit the available term?

In the equality-disabled raw calculus, could the operation of work for every \(X,L,R\) without a supplied original term?

Show answerHide answer

No. Use the empty language, no free names, context \(\Gamma=\{\bot\}\), and \(A=\exists x\bot\). There is an actual raw proof of \(A\): take the falsum assumption and apply falsum elimination directly to \(A\). No existential-introduction step, and hence no witness term, occurs in that source proof.

Put \(R=\top\), where \(\top\) abbreviates \(\bot\to\bot\). The required translated endpoint would be

\[\{\top\}\Rightarrow\exists x\top.\]

It has no raw derivation over these original names. Interpret the empty language on the empty domain, with its unique assignment to the empty name set. The assumption \(\top\) is true and the existential conclusion is false. As in the established constant-free raw obstruction, the raw soundness induction applies to this empty structure. At a protected rule it checks an extended assignment only after a domain element has been supplied, so it does not assume the domain inhabited. It would send a raw proof of the displayed sequent to a false semantic implication.

This disproves the proposed unrestricted raw transformation. It does not challenge the public theorem: a public proof has a reserve variable available as a term and its semantics uses a nonempty domain to interpret the reserve. In that representation, \(\exists x\top\) has the usual certificate using \(u_0\). No original ground term is thereby produced.

Does logical preservation already preserve the original arithmetic theory?

If \(T\) is the native Heyting-arithmetic axiom theory, does alone give \(T\vdash F_R(A)\) from \(T\vdash A\), or an arithmetic conservativity theorem?

Show answerHide answer

No such conclusion follows from the logical theorem alone. Its output assumptions lie in \(T^{[R]}\), not automatically in \(T\). To return to \(T\), one must supply actual \(T\)-certificates of every required translated arithmetic axiom \(F_R(\theta)\), with the relevant free-parameter and closure conditions. The final paragraph of then replaces the finitely many used translated axioms and retains their finite arithmetic supports.

The existing arithmetic translated-axiom certificates do this for the specified Gödel–Gentzen map. That map double-negates atoms and existential conclusions and fixes falsum. It is not the parameterized map here, which adds \(R\) to atoms, retains existential quantifiers and sends falsum to \(R\). Those earlier certificates therefore cannot simply be reused as certificates of these different formulas.

Moreover, proof preservation supplies a theorem about \(F_R(A)\). Conservativity for an original conclusion class also needs an appropriate way to remove or specialize this translation for that class. Even propositionally the translated formula need not imply the original one. Thus logical translation, arithmetic axiom preservation and an arithmetic conservativity argument are distinct mathematical requirements.
