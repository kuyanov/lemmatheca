Expand native negation before choosing a left rule

A reduced formula is a shared propositional formula built from atoms, falsum, conjunction, disjunction and implication, with no primitive negation constructor. Write \(A^\circ\) for the established expansion, which replaces each \(\neg B\) by \(B^\circ\to\bot\) and commutes with the other constructors. It produces a reduced formula without changing its atom names. We use \(\neg B\) in examples only as an abbreviation for \(B\to\bot\).

A finite antecedent \(\Gamma\) is a finite set of reduced formulas. A goal is a sequent \(\Gamma\Rightarrow E\) with one reduced conclusion. Write \(\Gamma,A\) for \(\Gamma\cup\{A\}\). Whenever a left rule selects \(F\in\Gamma\), its remaining antecedent is \(\Delta=\Gamma\setminus\{F\}\). Thus \(F\notin\Delta\) and \(\Gamma=\Delta,F\). Repeated assumptions are represented once. Removing the selected principal, rather than silently retaining it among the remaining formulas, is essential to the decreasing measure below.

A retained implication can repeat the same backward goal

The unrestricted retained-principal implication-left rule admits a backward step whose first premise is identical to its conclusion. In particular, searching for \(\{p\to q\}\Rightarrow p\) may immediately repeat that same goal.

The earlier cut-free calculus permits an implication-left step with conclusion \(\Gamma\Rightarrow E\), principal \(A\to B\in\Gamma\), producer \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\), and continuation \(\Gamma,B\Rightarrow E\). Set \(\Gamma=\{p\to q\}\), \(A=E=p\), and \(B=q\). The producer is precisely the original goal. Repeatedly choosing that option produces an infinite unsuccessful search branch, even though every completed proof tree is finite. This is a property of backward search, and does not contradict cut elimination.

Use specialized implication-left rules

A G4ip certificate is a finite tree generated by the following rules. An atomic identity closes \(\Gamma\Rightarrow p\) when \(p\in\Gamma\), and falsum-left closes \(\Gamma\Rightarrow E\) when \(\bot\in\Gamma\). The right rules are

\[\frac{\Gamma,A\Rightarrow B}{\Gamma\Rightarrow A\to B}\;R\to,\qquad \frac{\Gamma\Rightarrow A\quad\Gamma\Rightarrow B}{\Gamma\Rightarrow A\land B}\;R\land,\qquad \frac{\Gamma\Rightarrow A_i}{\Gamma\Rightarrow A_0\lor A_1}\;R\lor_i.\]

For a selected principal and its remaining antecedent \(\Delta\), the conjunction- and disjunction-left rules are

\[\frac{\Delta,A,B\Rightarrow E}{\Delta,A\land B\Rightarrow E}\;L\land,\qquad \frac{\Delta,A\Rightarrow E\quad\Delta,B\Rightarrow E}{\Delta,A\lor B\Rightarrow E}\;L\lor.\]

The implication-left rules inspect the outer constructor of the implication's input. In the first rule \(p\) is an atom already in the remaining antecedent. Each displayed principal is absent from \(\Delta\).

\[\frac{\Delta,B\Rightarrow E}{\Delta,p\to B\Rightarrow E}\;L_{p\to}\quad(p\in\Delta),\qquad \frac{\Delta\Rightarrow E}{\Delta,\bot\to B\Rightarrow E}\;L_{\bot\to}.\]
\[\frac{\Delta,A\to(B\to C)\Rightarrow E}{\Delta,(A\land B)\to C\Rightarrow E}\;L_{\land\to}.\]
\[\frac{\Delta,A\to C,B\to C\Rightarrow E}{\Delta,(A\lor B)\to C\Rightarrow E}\;L_{\lor\to}.\]
\[\frac{\Delta,B\to C\Rightarrow A\to B\quad\Delta,C\Rightarrow E}{\Delta,(A\to B)\to C\Rightarrow E}\;L_{\to\to}.\]

There is no unrestricted implication-left, cut, or weakening constructor. The rule \(L_{\bot\to}\) merely discards an assumption provable without premises. It completes the constructor cases for the input of an implication.

Each specialized rule is an intuitionistic inference

Every G4ip rule transforms supplied certificates of its premises into a certificate in the established native intuitionistic calculus of its conclusion. In particular, all five specialized implication rules are sound without classical reasoning.

Atomic implication-left uses the available \(p\) and the principal \(p\to B\) to derive \(B\), then replaces the continuation's assumption \(B\) by that proof. Falsum implication-left is weakening. For the conjunction case, \((A\land B)\to C\) derives \(A\to(B\to C)\): assume \(A\), assume \(B\), form their conjunction and apply the principal, then discharge the two assumptions. For the disjunction case the principal derives both \(A\to C\) and \(B\to C\), by introducing the appropriate disjunct. Replace both assumptions in the supplied continuation by these two derived implications.

For the remaining case put \(F=(A\to B)\to C\). Under \(\Delta,F,A\), derive \(B\to C\) by assuming \(B\), forming the constant implication \(A\to B\), and applying \(F\). Weaken the first supplied premise into this context and replace its assumption \(B\to C\). It gives \(A\to B\), hence \(B\) under the still open \(A\). Discharge \(A\) to obtain \(\Delta,F\Rightarrow A\to B\). Apply \(F\) to obtain \(C\), and replace the assumption \(C\) in the second supplied premise. The result is \(\Delta,F\Rightarrow E\).

The other rules are ordinary introduction or assumption-elimination steps. The proved replacement operation composes their actual finite trees; it is used as a transformation, not added as a G4ip rule. Recursing through a supplied G4ip tree therefore gives a native certificate with the same endsequent.

Choose a weight that makes currying smaller

The formula weight \(w(A)\) and sequent weight \(W(\Gamma\Rightarrow E)\) are defined by

\[w(p)=w(\bot)=2,\qquad w(A\land B)=w(A)(1+w(B)),\qquad w(A\lor B)=1+w(A)+w(B).\]
\[w(A\to B)=1+w(A)w(B),\qquad W(\Gamma\Rightarrow E)=\sum_{F\in\Gamma}w(F)+w(E).\]

The conclusion contributes separately even if it also belongs to \(\Gamma\). Formula induction proves \(w(A)\geq2\): this is immediate for atoms and falsum, and each displayed composite expression is at least two when its child weights are. The asymmetric conjunction weight is deliberate. The ordinary number of constructors would give equal sizes to \((A\land B)\to C\) and \(A\to(B\to C)\).

Every backward premise has strictly smaller weight

Every premise of every G4ip rule has sequent weight strictly smaller than its conclusion. This holds for finite sets even when some replacement formulas were already present.

Write \(a=w(A)\), \(b=w(B)\), \(c=w(C)\), and \(e=w(E)\). All are at least two. Removing the selected principal removes its entire weight. Inserting replacement formulas adds at most the sum of their weights; an already present formula adds nothing. Thus it suffices to compare the displayed replacement sums.

For conjunction-left, \(a+b<a(1+b)\), since \(ab>b\). For disjunction-left either \(a\) or \(b\) is smaller than \(1+a+b\). Right conjunction and right disjunction replace their conclusion by a strict child of smaller weight. Right implication adds at most \(a\) to the context and replaces \(A\to B\) by \(B\); the needed inequality is \(a+b<1+ab\), because \((a-1)(b-1)\geq1\).

Atomic implication-left replaces \(1+2b\) by at most \(b\), while falsum implication-left only removes a positive weight. In the curried case the difference between principal and replacement weights is

\[\bigl(1+a(1+b)c\bigr)-\bigl(1+a(1+bc)\bigr)=a(c-1)>0.\]

For a disjunctive input, the replacement sum is \((1+ac)+(1+bc)\); the principal has weight \(1+(1+a+b)c\), larger by \(c-1\).

For \(L_{\to\to}\), the second premise replaces the principal by \(C\), which is smaller. The first replaces the principal and old conclusion by \(B\to C\) and \(A\to B\). Their difference is

\[\bigl(1+(1+ab)c+e\bigr)-\bigl((1+bc)+(1+ab)\bigr) =c+e-1+b\bigl(c(a-1)-a\bigr)>0.\]

Indeed \(c(a-1)-a\geq2(a-1)-a=a-2\geq0\), and \(c+e-1\geq3\). These cases exhaust the rules. A branch therefore contains fewer than its initial weight many premise steps.

Replace a removed principal in the earlier calculus

Let \(F\in\Gamma\), put \(\Delta=\Gamma\setminus\{F\}\), and let \(p\) be an earlier cut-free certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow E\). If the earlier calculus proves \(\Theta\Rightarrow F\) and \(\Delta\subseteq\Theta\), its replacement transformation gives a certificate of \(\Theta\Rightarrow E\).

Every old assumption is either \(F\) or belongs to \(\Delta\). Supply the displayed proof for \(F\), and identity followed by weakening for the others. The consumer recursion replaces these uses in the actual supplied tree. Equivalently, its one-distinguished-formula invariant applies because \(\Gamma\subseteq\Theta,F\). The resulting certificate uses only \(\Theta\) as its allowed context.

Obtain the smaller invertible premises from an old proof

If the earlier cut-free calculus proves the conclusion of \(R\to\), \(R\land\), \(L\land\), \(L\lor\), \(L_{p\to}\), \(L_{\bot\to}\), \(L_{\land\to}\), or \(L_{\lor\to}\), it also supplies certificates of all that rule's premises. The atomic rule still requires its available atom.

The two right inversions are the established inversions. For the left rules apply . A conjunction principal is derivable from its two components. A disjunction principal is derivable from either disjunct, giving the two requested continuations separately. An atomic implication \(p\to B\) is derivable from \(B\), by assuming \(p\) and weakening the proof of \(B\). The atom \(p\) remains present, because an atom is distinct from the selected implication.

The formula \(\bot\to B\) has a proof without assumptions. A curried implication \(A\to(B\to C)\) derives \((A\land B)\to C\) by projecting an assumed conjunction and applying both implications. Finally, \(A\to C\) and \(B\to C\) derive \((A\lor B)\to C\) by disjunction elimination under an assumed disjunction. In each case these proofs are in the exact remaining context enlarged by the specified replacements. The replacement lemma transports the original supplied conclusion proof there.

Describe the goals left after invertible reductions

A reduced goal \(\Gamma\Rightarrow E\) is irreducible if its context contains no falsum, conjunction, disjunction, implication with input falsum, conjunction or disjunction, or pair \(p,p\to B\); its conclusion is neither a conjunction nor an implication; and an atomic conclusion is absent from its context.

Consequently every context formula is an atom, an implication with atomic input, or an implication with implication input. The conclusion is an atom, falsum, or a disjunction. This follows by inspecting the five reduced formula constructors. Each excluded form is either a closing leaf or the conclusion of one of the smaller invertible reductions just established.

Measure the supplied old tree along its producer branches

The left-branch length \(\ell(p)\) of an earlier cut-free certificate is defined recursively. Leaves have length zero. An implication-left step has length one plus the length of its producer, ignoring the continuation. A conjunction-right or disjunction-left step has length one plus the maximum of its two child lengths. Every other unary step has length one plus its child's length.

This is a natural number computed from the actual finite proof tree. The maximum convention is needed when a branching rule lies along a subsequent producer branch. It does not mean the number of all nodes or the maximum height of the whole proof.

Move an unusable atomic implication past its producer's left step

For an irreducible goal, an earlier cut-free proof can be transformed into one whose last step is either a right-disjunction rule or implication-left with principal \((A\to B)\to C\). The transformation acts on the supplied proof and terminates by strictly decreasing its left-branch length whenever its last principal has atomic input.

The excluded context forms and conclusion forms rule out every other last rule, except implication-left on \(p\to B\). In that exceptional case the producer proves \(\Gamma\Rightarrow p\). Irreducibility gives \(p\notin\Gamma\), since the principal \(p\to B\) is present. This producer cannot end in an identity, falsum-left, a right rule, or a left rule other than implication-left. Write its last principal as \(D\to F\). Its two children are \(r:\Gamma\Rightarrow D\) and \(s:\Gamma,F\Rightarrow p\). Write \(q:\Gamma,B\Rightarrow E\) for the original continuation.

Use \(p\to B\) in the larger context \(\Gamma,F\), with producer \(s\) and continuation obtained by weakening \(q\) to \(\Gamma,F,B\). This gives \(\Gamma,F\Rightarrow E\). Now apply implication-left on \(D\to F\), with producer \(r\) and this newly constructed continuation. The resulting proof has the same endsequent. Its left-branch length is \(1+\ell(r)\), whereas the old tree had length \(2+\ell(r)\). Both principals are retained in this earlier calculus, so all the cited context inclusions are valid, even if the two principal formulas happen to coincide.

Repeat on this shorter tree if necessary. Natural-number descent ends, and the remaining last rule has one of the two asserted forms. This is the corrected shortest-left-branch argument in transformation form; it does not select a new unrelated proof of the goal.

Remove the principal from an implication-input producer

Suppose an earlier cut-free proof ends in implication-left with principal \(F=(A\to B)\to C\), and put \(\Delta=\Gamma\setminus\{F\}\). Its two children yield old-calculus certificates of precisely the G4ip premises

\[\Delta,B\to C\Rightarrow A\to B,\qquad \Delta,C\Rightarrow E.\]

The old continuation is a proof of \(\Gamma,C\Rightarrow E\). From \(C\) one derives \(F\) by assuming \(A\to B\) and weakening. The replacement lemma therefore removes \(F\), giving the second requested premise.

The old producer proves \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\to B\). Invert its right implication to obtain \(\Gamma,A\Rightarrow B\). In the intended new context \(\Delta,B\to C,A\), derive \(F\) as follows: assume \(A\to B\), apply it to the available \(A\), then apply \(B\to C\), and discharge the assumed \(A\to B\). Replace every use of \(F\) in that inverted producer by this proof. Its remaining assumptions lie in the new context, so it yields \(\Delta,B\to C,A\Rightarrow B\). Discharging \(A\) gives the first requested premise. The construction uses only the earlier proved native proof transformations.

Transform every reduced intuitionistic proof into G4ip

For every finite reduced context \(\Gamma\) and reduced formula \(E\), a supplied earlier cut-free certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow E\) gives a G4ip certificate of that same endsequent.

Use strong induction on \(W(\Gamma\Rightarrow E)\), allowing both the context and conclusion to vary. If falsum is present, close by falsum-left. If the conclusion is an atom in the context, close by atomic identity. Otherwise, if any invertible reduction in applies, transform the supplied old proof into its premise certificates. Every premise has strictly smaller sequent weight by . Apply induction to them and finish with that G4ip rule.

If none applies, the goal is irreducible. Apply to its supplied proof. A final right-disjunction step already has a smaller premise; transform it by induction and restore the same right rule. A final implication-left step with implication input supplies the two old premise certificates in . They are exactly the smaller premises of \(L_{\to\to}\). Transform both by induction and apply that rule.

Reduced syntax and irreducibility exhaust the remaining cases. The inner producer-branch descent used only an actual proof tree and natural-number decrease; the outer recursion decreases sequent weight. Neither recursion assumes G4ip cut, contraction, completeness, or a decision procedure.

Recover the full native intuitionistic consequence relation

For every finite native context \(H\) and native formula \(E\),

\[H\vdash_{\mathrm I}E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H^\circ\Rightarrow E^\circ\text{ has a G4ip certificate}.\]

Here \(H^\circ=\{A^\circ:A\in H\}\). Both directions use the established native language and its intuitionistic rules.

Forwards, the native expansion theorem gives the expanded deduction. Its formulas are reduced. The earlier syntactic correspondence yields a cut-free certificate, which converts to G4ip. Backwards, converts the G4ip tree into a native deduction of the expanded endsequent. The converse expansion bridge gives the original native deduction. Thus primitive native negation has been accounted for explicitly.

Weakening, compound identity and cut remain available as transformations

On reduced formulas, G4ip admits weakening and compound identity. If supplied G4ip certificates prove \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) and \(\Gamma,A\Rightarrow B\), a G4ip certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow B\) can be constructed. The corresponding finite-context and native-expanded variants follow as well.

Convert the supplied trees to the earlier native cut-free calculus. Apply its weakening or compound identity transformation, or its cut transformation. Convert the resulting reduced endsequent back using . This constructs trees using the G4ip primitives only. It does not make any of these operations primitive backward-search choices.

Exchange requires no operation because antecedents are sets. Likewise repeated occurrences in a finite list give the same finite set. The correspondence theorem proves that this representation still captures ordinary intuitionistic deduction, including arguments that use an assumption more than once.

A conjunction-input implication becomes two atomic applications

For pairwise distinct atoms \(p,q,r\), G4ip proves \(\{p,q,(p\land q)\to r\}\Rightarrow r\). Following the conjunction-input rule and then the two atomic-input rules gives successive backward goal weights \(19,17,11,8\), ending in atomic identity.

Replace \((p\land q)\to r\) by \(p\to(q\to r)\). Since \(p\) is present, replace this implication by \(q\to r\). Since \(q\) is present, replace that implication by \(r\). The goal \(\{p,q,r\}\Rightarrow r\) closes by atomic identity. Reading these three left rules upwards from that leaf constructs the required certificate.

Atoms have weight two. The conjunction \(p\land q\) has weight six, its implication to \(r\) has weight thirteen, and the curried replacement has weight eleven. The remaining implication \(q\to r\) has weight five. Adding the context weights and the separate conclusion weight gives the four asserted values.

Does removing a principal mean that an assumption can be used only once?

Does the absence of a contraction constructor turn G4ip into a logic in which an assumption may be used at most once?

Show answerHide answer

No. Right-conjunction uses the same allowed context in both premises, and a reduced formula \(A\to(A\land A)\) has a G4ip certificate: compound identity proves \(\{A\}\Rightarrow A\) twice, right-conjunction combines these proofs, and right-implication discharges \(A\). More generally the native correspondence recovers all ordinary intuitionistic deductions. The specialized left rules control a particular source of backward loops; they do not impose a one-use condition on every assumption.

Is decreasing branch length alone a complete search procedure?

What remains to turn the decreasing rule measure into a terminating procedure that finds a certificate whenever one exists?

Show answerHide answer

For each finite goal one must enumerate all applicable backward rules and every choice of principal or right disjunct, recursively solve every premise of a selected rule, and backtrack when a choice fails. There are finitely many context formulas and at most two premises per option. The weight bound makes every such branch finite, so exhaustive finite branching terminates. Completeness requires visiting all options or proving a restricted strategy complete; choosing one disjunct without backtracking is not justified. The present equivalence theorem supplies the missing connection between such successful G4ip trees and the established native consequence relation.

The explicit exhaustive search implements these choices and reconstructs actual trees. Its sufficient-fuel theorem proves that the endsequent weight suffices, including backtracking after a failed alternative.
