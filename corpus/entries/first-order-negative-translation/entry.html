Translate full formulas and their open assumptions

Fix a language \(L\), free names \(X\), and the full native formulas with primitive equality, negation, conjunction, disjunction, implication and both quantifiers. Use the intuitionistic native rules and their classical extension. Fix whether logical equality rules are enabled, using the same choice in both calculi.

A supplied raw derivation is a particular finite proof tree over \(X\). Write \(\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm I}A\) or \(\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm C}A\) for existence of the corresponding raw derivation from a finite context. Public certificates use the established disjoint auxiliary reserve. We extend the propositional proof interpretation by transforming formulas, hypotheses and every quantified rule together.

Put atoms, disjunctions and existentials under double negation

The Gödel–Gentzen translation \(A^{\mathsf N}\) is defined on full formulas at every available bound scope. Terms are unchanged. Its clauses are

\[\bot^{\mathsf N}=\bot,\qquad(s=t)^{\mathsf N}=\neg\neg(s=t),\qquad R(\bar t)^{\mathsf N}=\neg\neg R(\bar t).\]
\[\begin{aligned}(\neg A)^{\mathsf N}&=\neg A^{\mathsf N},\\ (A\land B)^{\mathsf N}&=A^{\mathsf N}\land B^{\mathsf N},\\ (A\to B)^{\mathsf N}&=A^{\mathsf N}\to B^{\mathsf N},\\ (A\lor B)^{\mathsf N}&=\neg\neg(A^{\mathsf N}\lor B^{\mathsf N}).\end{aligned}\]
\[(\forall xB)^{\mathsf N}=\forall xB^{\mathsf N},\qquad(\exists xB)^{\mathsf N}=\neg\neg\exists xB^{\mathsf N}.\]

Each call concerns a strict formula child, so this specifies a finite tree. Quantifiers retain their original bound slots, and every atomic argument tuple retains its actual term trees. The superscript refers to this constructive interpretation.

Commute translation with the actual scope maps

For every compatible scope substitution \(\sigma\),

\[(A\langle\sigma\rangle)^{\mathsf N}=A^{\mathsf N}\langle\sigma\rangle.\]

Consequently translation commutes with free substitution, free renaming, opening a body and instantiating it at a supplied term. Its free-name support is exactly that of the original formula.

Induct on the full tree, allowing both scopes and the replacement terms to vary. At an atom both orders substitute into the same original tuple or equality terms, then add the same two negations. Falsum is fixed. Every connective clause reapplies its constructor and any displayed outer negations to the same child results. At either binder, both orders use the protected extension of \(\sigma\), which fixes its newest slot. Apply the body hypothesis to that extension. These cases prove literal tree equality. The specified free, opening and instance maps give the consequences. A second formula induction gives support equality: added negations introduce no names, and the original unions and binder supports are retained.

Retain insertion, unions and protected contexts

Put \(\Gamma^{\mathsf N}=\{B^{\mathsf N}:B\in\Gamma\}\). This finite image satisfies

\[(\Gamma,B)^{\mathsf N}=\Gamma^{\mathsf N},B^{\mathsf N},\qquad (\Gamma\cup\Delta)^{\mathsf N}=\Gamma^{\mathsf N}\cup\Delta^{\mathsf N}.\]

Also \((\Gamma^\uparrow)^{\mathsf N}=(\Gamma^{\mathsf N})^\uparrow\), with the same identity for each lifted conclusion. Repeated image formulas are identified as context members.

Membership in an image of an insertion is membership in the old image or equality to the inserted image. Membership in the image of a union is membership in one of its two images. These give the first identities. Apply to each member for the lifted-context identity. Thus a discharged or protected assumption is translated into exactly the assumption required by the corresponding new rule.

Specify stability as a proof transformation

A stability construction for \(C\) transforms any supplied intuitionistic raw derivation of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow\neg\neg C\) into one of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow C\), uniformly in the finite ambient context. Discharging the premise gives an assumption-free proof of \(\neg\neg C\to C\); conversely that proof supplies the operation by weakening and implication elimination.

Double-negation introduction always has an intuitionistic construction: from a supplied proof of \(C\), assume \(\neg C\), obtain falsum and discharge. Stability supplies the reverse direction for the particular formula under consideration.

Eliminate double negation at falsum and negative formulas

Falsum and every formula \(\neg C\) have stability constructions. Hence every double-negated formula is stable.

The intuitionistic theorem \(\neg\bot\) follows by assuming falsum and discharging it. A proof of \(\neg\neg\bot\) contradicts that theorem and gives falsum. For \(\neg C\), start with \(\neg\neg\neg C\) and assume \(C\). Double-negation introduction from this new assumption gives \(\neg\neg C\), contradicting the original premise. Discharge \(C\), obtaining \(\neg C\). All ambient assumptions stay available. Apply this construction with \(\neg C\) as its child to cover \(\neg\neg C\).

Use stable factors and stable consequents

If \(C,D\) have supplied stability constructions, so does \(C\land D\). If \(D\) has one, so does \(C\to D\), for arbitrary \(C\).

Given \(\neg\neg(C\land D)\), assume \(\neg C\). An assumed conjunction would give \(C\) by projection and a contradiction, so it gives \(\neg(C\land D)\). The original premise contradicts this. Discharge \(\neg C\), obtaining \(\neg\neg C\), then use the supplied stability of \(C\). Repeat with the other projection for \(D\), and conjoin.

For implication assume \(C\), while retaining \(\neg\neg(C\to D)\). Under \(\neg D\), any assumed \(C\to D\) gives \(D\) and a contradiction. This yields \(\neg(C\to D)\), contradicting the original premise. Discharge \(\neg D\), use stability of \(D\), and discharge \(C\). Each operation is made in the indicated enlarged context and uses no classical step.

Stabilize a universal through an arbitrary protected parameter

If the opened body \(\operatorname{open}(B)\) has a supplied stability construction, then \(\forall xB\) has one.

Start with a proof of \(\neg\neg\forall xB\) from \(\Gamma\), and rename it into \(X\sqcup\{e\}\). In this lifted context assume \(\neg\operatorname{open}(B)\). An additional assumed lifted universal gives its instance at \(e\), exactly \(\operatorname{open}(B)\), by the scope identity. Thus the additional universal assumption leads to falsum; discharge it to obtain the negation of that lifted universal. This contradicts the lifted double-negated premise.

Discharge \(\neg\operatorname{open}(B)\). The supplied body stability now gives \(\operatorname{open}(B)\) from \(\Gamma^\uparrow\). Universal introduction concludes \(\forall xB\). The tag \(e\) is absent from every lifted side assumption; the temporary negative-body assumption has already been discharged.

Copy the rules whose translated outer constructor is unchanged

Assumptions, conjunction rules, implication rules, primitive negation rules and falsum elimination translate into actual intuitionistic derivations.

An assumption \(A\in\Gamma\) becomes its image assumption. For conjunction introduction or either projection, reapply that same rule to the translated children. Implication introduction translates its discharged assumption to \(A^{\mathsf N}\) by the insertion identity; implication elimination uses the two translated premises. Negation introduction similarly discharges \(A^{\mathsf N}\) after its translated falsum branch; negation elimination combines \(A^{\mathsf N}\) with \(\neg A^{\mathsf N}\). Consume a double-negated disjunction into a stable target

From supplied intuitionistic proofs of \(\neg\neg(C\lor D)\), and branches deriving stable \(E\) from either additional assumption \(C\) or \(D\), construct a proof of \(E\).

Assume \(\neg E\). Assume also \(C\lor D\) and use native case elimination. Each supplied branch, weakened to this context, gives \(E\) and hence falsum. Discharge the disjunction, obtaining \(\neg(C\lor D)\), which contradicts the supplied double-negated disjunction. Discharge \(\neg E\) to get \(\neg\neg E\), then apply the given stability construction.

For a translated original case rule take \(C=B^{\mathsf N}\), \(D=D_0^{\mathsf N}\) and \(E=A^{\mathsf N}\). The translated branch contexts are exactly the two required insertions, and supplies the target stability.

Keep the universal tag and the supplied existential term

Universal introduction, universal elimination and existential introduction have explicit translated derivations.

A universal-introduction premise translates to a proof of \(\operatorname{open}(B^{\mathsf N})\) from \((\Gamma^{\mathsf N})^\uparrow\), by the two scope identities. Reapply native universal introduction with the same protected tag. For universal elimination at \(t\), the translated universal gives \(B^{\mathsf N}(t)=(B(t))^{\mathsf N}\), using that very supplied term.

An existential-introduction child translates to \(B^{\mathsf N}(t)\). Introduce \(\exists xB^{\mathsf N}\) at \(t\), then double-negate that result. The new rule neither selects a fresh witness nor changes the original witness term.

Discharge the witness before stabilizing the conclusion

Translate an existential elimination whose major premise is \(\exists xB\), whose protected branch concludes \(C^\uparrow\), and whose final conclusion is \(C\).

The translated major is \(\neg\neg\exists xB^{\mathsf N}\). The translated branch concludes \((C^{\mathsf N})^\uparrow\) from \((\Gamma^{\mathsf N})^\uparrow\) and \(\operatorname{open}(B^{\mathsf N})\). Assume \(\neg C^{\mathsf N}\) in the ambient context. To refute \(\exists xB^{\mathsf N}\), assume it and perform native existential elimination. In its protected branch use the supplied translated branch, weakened by the lifted negative-conclusion assumption. Its conclusion contradicts that lifted assumption, giving falsum.

Falsum contains no witness tag, so existential elimination and discharge give \(\neg\exists xB^{\mathsf N}\). This contradicts the double-negated major. Discharge \(\neg C^{\mathsf N}\), obtaining \(\neg\neg C^{\mathsf N}\), and use its stability. All temporary witnesses have been eliminated before concluding \(C^{\mathsf N}\).

Replace equals after translating the equation premise

An enabled equality-reflexivity step translates by reflexivity followed by double-negation introduction. Equality replacement has the actual derived rule

\[\frac{\neg\neg(s=t)\qquad (B(s))^{\mathsf N}}{(B(t))^{\mathsf N}}.\]

Assume \(\neg(B(t))^{\mathsf N}\). Under a further actual equation assumption \(s=t\), apply the native equality-replacement rule to the translated one-slot body \(B^{\mathsf N}\). The scope identity takes the supplied \((B(s))^{\mathsf N}\) to its exact instance at \(s\), and replacement gives its instance at \(t\), namely \((B(t))^{\mathsf N}\). This contradicts the temporary negative-target assumption. Discharge the equation, obtaining \(\neg(s=t)\), which contradicts the original \(\neg\neg(s=t)\).

Discharge the negative-target assumption and apply stability of \((B(t))^{\mathsf N}\). Thus the double-negated equation is used through a contradiction branch, while the inner replacement uses an actual equation. Equality remains enabled exactly when it was enabled in the source tree.

Relate negative-conjunction and negative-universal clauses

The selected clauses retain native positive constructors under double negation. Other usual conventions use \(\neg(\neg C\land\neg D)\) and \(\neg\forall x\neg E\) in place of \(\neg\neg(C\lor D)\) and \(\neg\neg\exists xE\). Each pair has intuitionistic conversions in both directions.

Native case elimination and negation introduction give \(\neg(C\lor D)\) exactly when both \(\neg C\) and \(\neg D\) hold: each direction assumes a proposed disjunct and derives the corresponding contradiction. Transporting a contradiction through these two constructions gives the first pair of negative formulas. For the constructive quantified law, a proof of \(\neg\exists xE\) gives \(\neg E(e)\) at an arbitrary protected parameter: an assumed \(E(e)\) would introduce the forbidden existential. Universally introduce to get \(\forall x\neg E\). Conversely that universal refutes a supposed existential through its protected witness branch. These constructions work over the fixed raw names too. Negating through them gives the second pair. Thus these conventions supply the same constructive interpretation while keeping distinct literal trees.

Why must the hypotheses be translated too?

Does a classical proof from \(\Gamma\) automatically give an intuitionistic proof of \(A^{\mathsf N}\) from those same untranslated assumptions?

Show answerHide answer

No. In the specified eventual-predicate model, every world forces \(\forall x\neg\neg R(x)\) and \(\neg\forall xR(x)\). Take \(A=\neg\forall xR(x)\) and \(\Gamma=\{A\}\). There is a raw classical proof of \(A\) from \(\Gamma\): its assumption leaf. Its translation is \(A^{\mathsf N}=\neg\forall x\neg\neg R(x)\). This fails at every world, because its inner universal is already forced there. Kripke soundness therefore excludes an intuitionistic certificate of \(A^{\mathsf N}\) from \(\Gamma\), even though the source assumption is true.

The theorem gives the image context \(\Gamma^{\mathsf N}\). It can be used from \(\Gamma\) whenever actual intuitionistic proofs of all its translated assumptions are supplied; proof replacement then applies. Such additional constructions are available in many special cases, but the displayed counterexample prevents assuming them for an arbitrary first-order premise.
