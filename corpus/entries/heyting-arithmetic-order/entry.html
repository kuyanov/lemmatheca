Keep order arguments inside the original arithmetic theory

Use the native arithmetic language with \(0,S,+,\times\), logical equality, and every native connective. An HA order certificate is an actual finite HA deduction of one of the formulas below. It uses finitely many original arithmetic axioms and full induction instances. The letters \(x,y,z,a\) may denote original variables or supplied arithmetic terms; an existential witness is always introduced under a fresh bound name.

Write \(\mathrm{HA}\vdash_X A\) when the certificate has original free-name set \(X\) and no additional assumptions. Every construction also works in a fixed finite context \(\Gamma\): weaken closed arithmetic facts into that context, retain its assumptions, and combine only the finite supports actually used. We use the established addition instances, including both cancellation directions, rather than replacing an arithmetic deduction by a calculation in the standard natural numbers.

Express order by an additive witness

The non-strict order formula and strict order formula are the native abbreviations

\[x\leq y\;:=\;\exists d\,(d+x=y),\qquad x<y\;:=\;\exists d\,(d+Sx=y).\]

Thus \(x<y\) is literally the abbreviation \(Sx\leq y\). The quantified name \(d\) does not occur in the supplied terms. These definitions introduce no new relation symbol or arithmetic axiom. For example, a certificate of \(a+x=y\) gives a certificate of \(x\leq y\) by existential introduction with witness \(a\).

Conversely, an order premise may be used by existential elimination: work under \(d+x=y\) at a fresh parameter \(d\), derive a conclusion not containing \(d\), then discharge that witness branch. This is the native quantifier rule; the remaining context must stay independent of the fresh parameter.

Substitution changes the supplied terms and fixes the witness slot

For every capture-free term substitution \(\sigma:X\to\operatorname{Term}(Y)\),

\[(s\leq t)[\sigma]=(s[\sigma]\leq t[\sigma]),\qquad(s<t)[\sigma]=(s[\sigma]<t[\sigma]).\]

The corresponding identities hold for renaming original free names. Consequently a supplied order certificate can be substituted or renamed using the existing certificate operations.

Expand the existential abbreviation. Under its binder, lift \(\sigma\) so that it substitutes into the original names and fixes the new witness slot. Addition and equality commute with that lifted substitution, producing \(d+s[\sigma]=t[\sigma]\). Reclosing the same slot is precisely the first displayed formula. Successor also commutes with substitution, so the same calculation proves the strict identity. A renaming is the special substitution sending each name to its renamed variable. The native substitution transformation acts on the supplied proof tree; the closed arithmetic axioms remain copies of the same axioms.

A sum is zero only when both summands are zero

HA proves, uniformly in the two terms,

\[a+b=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a=0\land b=0.\]

Use the zero-or-successor alternative for \(b\). If \(b=0\), the equation \(a+b=0\) and the recursion equation \(a+0=a\) give \(a=0\). If \(b=Sc\), the sum is \(S(a+c)\), which cannot equal zero by the successor-nonzero axiom. That branch gives falsum, from which the required conjunction follows. Existential elimination removes the temporary predecessor \(c\). Conversely, substituting \(a=b=0\) reduces the sum to \(0+0=0\). These are equality substitutions and native disjunction cases, with no appeal to excluded middle for an arbitrary formula.

Zero is least and has no strict predecessor

For every arithmetic term \(x\), HA supplies certificates of

\[0\leq x,\qquad (x\leq0\Longleftrightarrow x=0),\qquad\neg(x<0).\]

For \(0\leq x\), use witness \(x\) and the recursion equation \(x+0=x\). If \(x\leq0\), open its witness \(d+x=0\); the zero-sum lemma gives \(x=0\). Conversely, \(x=0\) permits witness zero, using \(0+0=0\). Finally, a witness for \(x<0\) would give \(d+Sx=0\), whose second summand must be zero. This contradicts successor-nonzero. Discharge the assumed strict-order formula to obtain its native negation.

Cancel one successor in the order formulas

HA proves the following three equivalences, with the displayed witnesses unchanged:

\[Sx\leq Sy\Longleftrightarrow x\leq y,\qquad Sx<Sy\Longleftrightarrow x<y,\qquad x<Sy\Longleftrightarrow x\leq y.\]

The witness equation for the first left side is \(d+Sx=Sy\). The addition recursion equation changes it to \(S(d+x)=Sy\), and successor injectivity gives \(d+x=y\). Reversing this equality reasoning gives the converse with the same \(d\). Apply that first equivalence at \(Sx,y\) to obtain the second, using the definition of strict order. The third is the first equivalence itself, since \(x<Sy\) abbreviates \(Sx\leq Sy\). Each direction opens the input witness, performs this equality calculation, and introduces that witness into the required existential formula.

Split a non-strict witness into equality or strict inequality

For every \(x,y\), HA proves

\[x\leq y\Longleftrightarrow(x<y\lor x=y).\]

In particular, it proves \(x\leq x\) and converts every certificate of \(x<y\) into one of \(x\leq y\).

Open a witness \(d+x=y\) and apply zero-or-successor to \(d\). If \(d=0\), the proved left-zero law gives \(x=y\). If \(d=Se\), the left-successor and right-successor addition laws identify \((Se)+x\) with \(e+Sx\); hence \(e\) witnesses \(x<y\). Eliminate the predecessor branch and introduce the appropriate disjunct.

Conversely, a strict witness \(e+Sx=y\) gives the non-strict witness \(Se\), using the same two successor equations. An equality \(x=y\) gives witness zero. Taking reflexivity \(x=x\) gives \(x\leq x\). These constructions prove both directions, rather than replacing the existential premise by a choice of a standard numeral.

A successor bound adds exactly one point to an initial segment

HA proves, uniformly in \(x,y\),

\[x<Sy\Longleftrightarrow(x<y\lor x=y),\qquad x\leq Sy\Longleftrightarrow(x\leq y\lor x=Sy).\]

The first equivalence combines \(x<Sy\leftrightarrow x\leq y\) with the preceding witness split. For the second, split a proof of \(x\leq Sy\) into \(x<Sy\) or \(x=Sy\). The strict branch gives \(x\leq y\) by successor cancellation. Conversely, a witness \(d+x=y\) gives \((Sd)+x=Sy\), so \(Sd\) witnesses \(x\leq Sy\). An equality \(x=Sy\) supplies that order formula by reflexivity and equality substitution. Native disjunction elimination combines the alternatives. At bound zero the strict initial segment is empty by , so these successor laws also specify the empty-bound case.

Compose order witnesses by addition

HA has proof operations for the three implications

\[(x\leq y\land y\leq z)\to x\leq z,\qquad (x<y\land y\leq z)\to x<z,\]
\[(x\leq y\land y<z)\to x<z.\]

Open witnesses \(a+x=y\) and \(b+y=z\). Associativity gives \((b+a)+x=b+(a+x)=z\), so \(b+a\) witnesses the first conclusion. If the first input is strict, replace \(x\) in that calculation by \(Sx\). For the third implication the input equations are \(a+x=y\) and \(b+Sy=z\). The successor equations give

\[(b+a)+Sx=S((b+a)+x)=S(b+(a+x))=b+S(a+x)=z.\]

Thus \(b+a\) is again the strict witness. Each temporary witness parameter is absent from the final order formula and original context, so the two existential eliminations are legitimate. The supplied premise proofs are retained in the resulting finite deduction. Combining a strict-to-non-strict conversion with either mixed implication also gives transitivity of strict order.

A strict inequality excludes equality

HA proves \(\neg(x<x)\), and it proves

\[x<y\Longleftrightarrow(x\leq y\land\neg(x=y)).\]

A putative strict witness gives \(d+Sx=x\), hence \((Sd)+x=x\). Rewrite the right side as \(0+x\) and use right cancellation to obtain \(Sd=0\), contradicting successor-nonzero. This proves irreflexivity.

A strict certificate gives a non-strict one by the witness split. If \(x=y\), equality substitution would turn it into the impossible \(x<x\), so it also gives \(\neg(x=y)\). Conversely, split \(x\leq y\) into strict inequality or equality. Retain the strict branch; in the equality branch use its supplied negation and explosion. Thus the displayed equivalence uses only native logical operations.

Opposite non-strict inequalities force equality

HA proves \((x\leq y\land y\leq x)\to x=y\). Together with reflexivity and transitivity, this establishes the partial-order laws for the defined formula.

Open witnesses \(a+x=y\) and \(b+y=x\). Substitution and associativity give \((b+a)+x=x\). Rewriting the right side as \(0+x\) and cancelling \(x\) gives \(b+a=0\). The zero-sum lemma gives \(a=0\), and the first input equation reduces to \(x=y\). Remove the two witness branches and discharge the conjunction. No order comparison or general excluded middle is needed for this argument.

One induction constructs comparison for every pair

HA has one finite certificate of the universally closed trichotomy formula

\[\forall y\,\forall x\;\bigl(x<y\lor(x=y\lor y<x)\bigr).\]

The three alternatives are pairwise incompatible.

Induct on \(y\) in the full native body \(C(y):=\forall x\,(x<y\lor(x=y\lor y<x))\). Its inner variable is distinct from the protected induction parameter. For \(y=0\), take an arbitrary \(x\) and split it into zero or successor. Equality handles the zero case. If \(x=Su\), then \(u+S0=Su=x\), so \(u\) witnesses \(0<x\). Introduce the required disjunction and then universally introduce \(x\).

In the successor step assume \(C(y)\), and again take arbitrary \(x\). If \(x=0\), witness \(y\) proves \(0<Sy\), since \(y+S0=Sy\). If \(x=Su\), instantiate the induction hypothesis at \(u\). Its three alternatives are \(u<y\), \(u=y\), and \(y<u\). Successor cancellation transports them, respectively, to \(Su<Sy\), \(Su=Sy\), and \(Sy<Su\). Disjunction elimination therefore supplies comparison for \(x,Sy\). Eliminate the temporary predecessor, universally introduce \(x\), and finish the native induction construction. The result is the displayed closed formula, obtained from one specified full induction instance.

A strict alternative and equality contradict . Two opposite strict inequalities give \(x<x\) by mixed transitivity and strict-to-non-strict conversion, again a contradiction. Thus at most one alternative can hold, as claimed.

Order has internal decision disjunctions

For arbitrary supplied arithmetic terms \(s,t\), HA constructs certificates of

\[(s\leq t)\lor\neg(s\leq t),\qquad(s<t)\lor\neg(s<t).\]

It also proves \(\neg(t<s)\leftrightarrow s\leq t\) and \(\neg(t\leq s)\leftrightarrow s<t\).

Instantiate the closed comparison proof at the supplied two terms. For non-strict order, either \(s<t\) or \(s=t\) supplies \(s\leq t\). In the third case \(t<s\), an assumed \(s\leq t\) gives \(t<t\) by mixed transitivity, so it gives \(\neg(s\leq t)\). For strict order, the first comparison branch supplies the positive decision. Equality refutes a strict inequality by irreflexivity; the opposite strict branch refutes it by transitivity. These three native disjunction cases produce the required strict decision.

If \(s\leq t\), then \(t<s\) would contradict irreflexivity by mixed transitivity. Conversely, under \(\neg(t<s)\), comparison eliminates its third branch and the other two give \(s\leq t\). The second equivalence uses the same comparison: strict \(s<t\) excludes \(t\leq s\); under the negation of that latter formula, the equality and opposite-strict branches each give a contradiction, leaving \(s<t\). These arguments decide the defined order formulas even though their expansions contain existential quantifiers.

Adding the same term preserves and reflects both orders

HA proves the following equivalences for arbitrary arithmetic terms:

\[x+a\leq y+a\Longleftrightarrow x\leq y,\qquad x+a<y+a\Longleftrightarrow x<y,\]
\[a+x\leq a+y\Longleftrightarrow x\leq y,\qquad a+x<a+y\Longleftrightarrow x<y.\]

A witness \(d+x=y\) gives \(d+(x+a)=(d+x)+a=y+a\), so the same \(d\) proves preservation of non-strict order. Conversely, a witness \(d+(x+a)=y+a\) gives \((d+x)+a=y+a\); right cancellation supplies \(d+x=y\). Thus reflection also retains the actual witness.

For strict order use the identity \((d+Sx)+a=d+S(x+a)\), obtained from associativity and the left- and right-successor addition laws. A witness on either side can therefore be moved to an equation with a common final summand \(a\), which right cancellation removes. This proves the strict equivalence with the same witness. Finally, commutativity replaces \(a+x,a+y\) by \(x+a,y+a\), giving the left-addition versions. All equality chains use the existing finite equality-proof operations.

Close finite parameter lists and instantiate them again

For each law above, give its finitely many original variables a fixed order. The construction yields an HA certificate of its universal closure. Instantiating that supplied closed certificate at arbitrary arithmetic terms gives the corresponding law in every finite additional context \(\Gamma\).

The calculations and witness eliminations prove each open law without additional assumptions. Its finitely many arithmetic axiom copies are closed, so none contains the original free parameters. Apply native universal introduction successively along the fixed parameter list, using fresh protected tags and keeping these closed axiom copies fixed. This builds one finite closed certificate. For comparison the preceding induction has already produced such a certificate directly.

To apply a closed certificate, rename it into the desired original name set, use universal elimination at the supplied terms in the same ordered list, and weaken its empty additional context into \(\Gamma\). The scope identities in identify the resulting order formulas with the required literal term instances. No numeral evaluation or fresh induction at the supplied terms is needed. Every operation acts on the given finite proof tree and retains finite arithmetic support.

Work out a bound with two new endpoints

HA proves, for arbitrary terms \(x,y\),

\[x<SSy\Longleftrightarrow\bigl(x<y\lor(x=y\lor x=Sy)\bigr).\]

Apply the strict initial-segment split first at \(Sy\). It gives \(x<Sy\lor x=Sy\). Split the first branch at \(y\), obtaining \(x<y\lor x=y\), and introduce the displayed three-way disjunction. Conversely, each of the first two branches gives \(x<Sy\) by that split, hence \(x<SSy\) by the split at \(Sy\). The final equality branch also gives \(x<SSy\) by the latter split. Native disjunction elimination and introduction implement these two directions.

Must comparison choose a strict direction?

No. Set both terms equal to zero. Each strict alternative becomes \(0<0\), and HA has its negation by strict irreflexivity. In fact, assuming \(0<0\lor0<0\), disjunction elimination contradicts that same negation in either branch. Discharging the assumption gives an actual HA certificate of \(\neg(0<0\lor0<0)\). Equality is therefore an essential comparison case; it cannot be replaced by a strict choice.
