A formula, its code, and an instance

Work in the arithmetic language \(L_A=\{0,S,+,\cdot\}\), with Robinson arithmetic \(Q\) and its standard model \(\mathbb N_A\). Use the existing codes of formulas and sentences. We write \(\ulcorner A\urcorner\) for an external natural-number code and \(\overline{\ulcorner A\urcorner}\) for its numeral, a closed arithmetic term.

A formula \(B(x)\) below has at most the one free variable \(x=x_0\); it may also have bound variables. Its code uses the existing free-name label \(0\). The numeral instance \(B(\overline n)\) is the sentence obtained by capture-safe substitution of the numeral for that free variable. These three objects have different roles: \(B\) is a formula, \(\ulcorner B\urcorner\) is a number, and \(B(\overline n)\) is a sentence. Arithmetic terms do not contain formulas as subexpressions.

Substitute a code's own numeral

Use the primitive-recursive numeral-code function \(\operatorname{num}\) and the coded free-name substitution \(\operatorname{sub}(k,e,i,t)\). Its first argument is the number of in-scope quantified placeholders, so a complete formula has \(k=0\). Define the diagonal substitution function, a total numerical function, by

\[d(n):=\operatorname{sub}(0,n,0,\operatorname{num}(n)).\]

The procedure examines the expression coded by \(n\), replaces each free occurrence of \(x_0\) by the numeral for \(n\), and rebuilds the expression. Its existing checks return zero on malformed inputs. It can also process well-formed formulas with other free names, so we do not claim that every numerical input produces a sentence code. The identity needed here concerns codes of formulas whose only possible free name is \(x_0\).

The numerical operation agrees with substitution

The function \(d\) is primitive recursive. For every formula \(E(x)\) with at most the free variable \(x_0\),

\[d(\ulcorner E\urcorner)=\ulcorner E(\overline{\ulcorner E\urcorner})\urcorner.\]

The numeral-code function and substitution procedure are primitive recursive, so their displayed composition is primitive recursive. The substitution correctness identity says that this composition produces the code of precisely the indicated syntactic substitution. A numeral contains no quantified placeholders, so inserting it leaves all bound variables intact. Since \(E\) has no other free names, the result is a sentence.

No evaluation of the sentence is involved. The calculation is a finite transformation of symbols, whether the resulting sentence is true or false.

Arithmetic can prove each value of diagonal substitution

Fix once and for all a formula \(D(x,y)\) strongly representing \(d\) in \(Q\). Such a formula exists by representability of total computable functions. Thus, for every external natural number \(n\),

\[Q\vdash\forall y\,\bigl(D(\overline n,y)\leftrightarrow y=\overline{d(n)}\bigr).\]

In every model of \(Q\), including a nonstandard model, the possible outputs at a fixed numeral input therefore consist of exactly the correct standard numeral. This is the universal output identity, rather than a claim only about pairs of numerical inputs and outputs. It will control every witness in the existential formula constructed next. We do not need a single theorem of \(Q\) asserting totality at all its possibly nonstandard inputs.

Build the sentence through an auxiliary formula

Given an arithmetic formula \(B(x)\), define the auxiliary formula

\[E_B(x):=\exists y\,\bigl(D(x,y)\land B(y)\bigr).\]

Here \(B(y)\) is a capture-safe renaming of the possible free variable of \(B\); bound variables are unaffected. The new quantified witness \(y\) is fresh. Let \(e_B=\ulcorner E_B\urcorner\) and form its fixed-point sentence

\[G_B:=E_B(\overline{e_B}).\]

The construction first builds the finite formula \(E_B\), computes its code, and then substitutes that code's numeral into it. There is no circular definition of a string. The identity gives \(d(e_B)=\ulcorner G_B\urcorner\).

Every property of codes has a fixed-point sentence

For every arithmetic formula \(B(x)\) with at most one free variable, the constructed sentence \(G_B\) satisfies

\[Q\vdash G_B\leftrightarrow B(\overline{\ulcorner G_B\urcorner}).\]

Write \(e=e_B\) and \(g=\ulcorner G_B\urcorner=d(e)\). At the fixed input \(\overline e\), supplies

\[Q\vdash\forall y\,\bigl(D(\overline e,y)\leftrightarrow y=\overline g\bigr).\]

In any model of \(Q\), if \(G_B\) holds, its defining existential formula has a witness \(y\). The output identity forces \(y=\overline g\), and the other conjunct gives \(B(\overline g)\). Conversely, if \(B(\overline g)\) holds, choose \(\overline g\) as the witness; the same identity supplies \(D(\overline e,\overline g)\). Hence every model of \(Q\) satisfies the required equivalence. Completeness of the first-order calculus gives the displayed derivation. Equivalently, equality substitution and the existential rules turn the output-identity proof directly into it.

The fixed point is a provable equivalence between sentences. It is not an assertion that \(G_B\) literally contains its full own text, or that the two sides are the same syntactic expression. No consistency assumption on an extending theory was used.

The same sentence works in every extending theory

For the same \(G_B\), every arithmetic theory \(T\) containing \(Q\) proves

\[T\vdash G_B\leftrightarrow B(\overline{\ulcorner G_B\urcorner}).\]

A proof from \(Q\) remains a proof from \(T\) by weakening the allowed axioms. The construction of \(G_B\) depends on \(B\) and the already fixed \(D\), and not on \(T\). In particular \(T\) need not have an effective axiom enumeration or be sound or consistent.

Does self-reference force a paradox?

Apply the construction first to \(B(x):=(x=x)\), then to \(B(x):=\neg(x=x)\). What does \(Q\) prove about the two resulting sentences?

For the first formula, the right-hand side of the fixed-point equivalence is a reflexive equality of numerals, so \(Q\) proves it and therefore proves the resulting \(G_B\). For the second formula, the right-hand side is a false disequality of a numeral with itself. Equality reflexivity and the equivalence give \(Q\vdash\neg G_B\). Neither construction gives a contradiction. The formula \(\neg(x=x)\) asserts a false arithmetic property of a number; it does not express that the sentence having that code is false.

What a truth predicate would have to say

An arithmetic formula \(\operatorname{Tr}(x)\) is a truth predicate for the standard arithmetic structure \(\mathbb N_A\) if, for every arithmetic sentence \(\sigma\),

\[\mathbb N_A\models\operatorname{Tr}(\overline{\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbb N_A\models\sigma.\]

This requires one fixed formula to work for all sentences. It puts no condition on numbers that do not code sentences. Both satisfaction statements are made in our metatheory; the first evaluates the proposed arithmetic formula at a numerical input.

There is a separate, syntactic demand: an arithmetic theory \(T\) might be asked to prove every truth biconditional

\[T\vdash\operatorname{Tr}(\overline{\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner})\leftrightarrow\sigma.\]

Defining truth in \(\mathbb N_A\) and proving all these sentences in \(T\) are different requirements. The following two theorems address them separately.

A consistent arithmetic theory cannot prove the full truth scheme

If \(T\supseteq Q\) is consistent, no arithmetic formula \(\operatorname{Tr}(x)\) has all its truth biconditionals provable in \(T\).

Apply to \(B(x):=\neg\operatorname{Tr}(x)\), obtaining a sentence \(L\) with

\[T\vdash L\leftrightarrow\neg\operatorname{Tr}(\overline{\ulcorner L\urcorner}).\]

The proposed full scheme includes \(T\vdash\operatorname{Tr}(\overline{\ulcorner L\urcorner})\leftrightarrow L\). These two biconditionals imply a contradiction by classical propositional reasoning. To see this also in models, consistency gives a model of \(T\) by Henkin completeness. Soundness makes both biconditionals true there, forcing the truth value of \(L\) to equal its negation, which is impossible. No soundness assumption about \(T\)'s truth in \(\mathbb N_A\) is needed.

Arithmetic truth is not arithmetically definable

No formula \(\operatorname{Tr}(x)\) in the arithmetic language defines truth for all arithmetic sentences in \(\mathbb N_A\).

Again take the fixed point \(L\) for \(\neg\operatorname{Tr}(x)\). Since \(\mathbb N_A\models Q\), soundness gives

\[\mathbb N_A\models L\leftrightarrow\neg\operatorname{Tr}(\overline{\ulcorner L\urcorner}).\]

If \(\operatorname{Tr}\) defined truth, its defining requirement at the particular sentence \(L\) would also give

\[\mathbb N_A\models\operatorname{Tr}(\overline{\ulcorner L\urcorner})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbb N_A\models L.\]

Combining the two statements says that \(L\) is true exactly when it is false, a contradiction.

The contradiction rules out the proposed arithmetic truth formula. It does not make the surrounding mathematics inconsistent. We have used the external satisfaction relation already defined for arithmetic structures; that relation has not been identified with a formula of arithmetic.

Fixed numerical parameters do not supply a truth predicate

No arithmetic formula \(A(z_0,\ldots,z_{k-1},x)\), with a fixed finite tuple of standard numerical parameters \(a_0,\ldots,a_{k-1}\), defines truth on all sentence codes by

\[\mathbb N_A\models A(\bar a,\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbb N_A\models\sigma.\]

Replace each parameter variable by its numeral, giving the unary formula \(A(\overline{a_0},\ldots,\overline{a_{k-1}},x)\). It concerns fixed natural numbers in the standard model; no claim about arbitrary parameters of nonstandard models is being made.

The external set of true sentence codes

In the metatheory, define the set of true arithmetic sentence codes by

\[\operatorname{True}_A:=\{\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner:\sigma\text{ is an arithmetic sentence and }\mathbb N_A\models\sigma\}.\]

Injectivity of the sentence code gives \(\ulcorner\sigma\urcorner\in\operatorname{True}_A\) exactly when \(\mathbb N_A\models\sigma\). Non-sentence codes are absent. This set exists as a subset of \(\mathbb N\) in our metatheory. Its existence does not supply an arithmetic formula defining it.

No total recursive test decides arithmetic truth

The set \(\operatorname{True}_A\) is not decidable: there is no total recursive test for membership in it.

If it were decidable, numeralwise representation of recursive relations would give a formula \(A(x)\) for which \(Q\) proves every true numerical instance and the negation of every false one. By soundness in \(\mathbb N_A\), this formula would define membership in \(\operatorname{True}_A\) on every natural number.
