Ask for one arithmetic proof with variable bounds

Work in the original native language of Heyting arithmetic: terms use \(0,S,+,\cdot\), equality is logical, and \(\bot,\neg,\land,\lor,\to,\forall,\exists\) keep their separate constructors. Fix a finite ordered tuple of parameter names \(\bar y\). A bound such as \(t(\bar y)\) remains a term; its value is not replaced by an external numeral before the argument begins.

Every decision below is an actual HA certificate, using finitely many of the original closed axioms and intuitionistic deduction. We may work under a fixed finite additional context \(\Gamma\) over the parameter names. A new quantified input is fresh for \(\Gamma\). The parameter tuple and the additional context may both be empty.

Build bounded formulas and exclude the new name from its bound

A bounded arithmetic expression starts with falsum and comparisons \(s=t\), \(s\le t\), \(s<t\), for arithmetic terms. It is closed under native negation, conjunction, disjunction and implication, and under the four constructions

\[\forall x<t\,B,\qquad \exists x<t\,B,\qquad \forall x\le t\,B,\qquad \exists x\le t\,B.\]

In each construction, \(t\) uses only the names available before the new \(x\) is introduced; the body \(B\) may also use \(x\). This is the bound proviso. A later inner bound may depend on earlier quantified inputs. There is no constructor for an unbounded quantifier.

Order comparisons here are syntactic abbreviations with the already fixed additive meaning. Thus this bounded grammar is a way to organize a class of native arithmetic formulas, not an extension of HA by new axioms or nonlogical relation symbols.

Expand every bounded expression into the original formula tree

Write \(\mathcal E(B)\) for the native expansion. It retains equality, falsum and each Boolean connective. Its order atoms expand as \(s\le t\equiv\exists d(d+s=t)\) and \(s<t\equiv Ss\le t\), with fresh \(d\). Its quantified clauses are literally

\[\mathcal E(\forall x<t\,B)=\forall x\,(x<t\to\mathcal E(B)),\qquad \mathcal E(\exists x<t\,B)=\exists x\,(x<t\land\mathcal E(B)).\]
\[\mathcal E(\forall x\le t\,B)=\forall x\,(x\le t\to\mathcal E(B)),\qquad \mathcal E(\exists x\le t\,B)=\exists x\,(x\le t\land\mathcal E(B)).\]

The guards in these displays are themselves expanded by the order clauses. Their fresh difference witnesses have separate protected slots. In particular, the resulting native tree can contain quantifiers introduced by an order abbreviation. The property being asserted is membership in this explicitly expanded bounded class, not native quantifier-freeness.

For a full native body \(A(x,\bar y)\), abbreviate its strict existential and universal guards by

\[E_A(n)\equiv\exists x\,(x<n\land A(x)),\qquad U_A(n)\equiv\forall x\,(x<n\to A(x)).\]

The second is exactly the preceding entry's positive history \(H_A(n)\). We use \(P\leftrightarrow Q\) below as an abbreviation for the pair of native implications \((P\to Q)\land(Q\to P)\).

Expansion commutes with capture-free parameter substitution

If \(\sigma\) substitutes specified terms for the original parameters, then \(B[\sigma]\) is still a bounded expression and

\[\mathcal E(B[\sigma])=\mathcal E(B)[\sigma].\]

The analogous identity holds for renaming. Within each bounded quantifier, extend \(\sigma\) by fixing its new protected input; substitute only the old parameters in its bounding term.

Induct on the bounded expression. Equality and the Boolean constructors use the native scope identities; order atoms use the order substitution identities. At a bounded quantifier the old term map is lifted through the new binder, fixing its input. The bound has no occurrence of that input, so its instance is exactly the renamed or substituted old bound. The recursive body identity and the native universal or existential scope identity give the displayed expansion equality. This proves a syntactic identity, including below further nested binders.

Distinguish a decision disjunction from an external truth test

For a native formula \(C\), an internal decision is a certificate of \(C\lor\neg C\). For a predicate \(A(x,\bar y)\), the uniform input to finite search is a certificate of

\[D_A\equiv\forall x\,(A(x,\bar y)\lor\neg A(x,\bar y)).\]

This is the same explicit decision premise used in internal least-witness search. The aim is to construct it for bounded bodies, with their parameter values still arbitrary. A procedure evaluating one standard numerical assignment is a different assertion.

Decision certificates combine through every Boolean connective

Supplied decisions for native \(B,C\) construct decisions for \(\neg B\), \(B\land C\), \(B\lor C\), and \(B\to C\), in the same additional context. Falsum has a decision. Equality and order comparisons have their equality and order decisions at arbitrary terms.

Eliminate \(B\lor\neg B\). In its positive branch, assuming \(\neg B\) produces falsum, so one gets \(\neg\neg B\); in its negative branch keep \(\neg B\). These are the two alternatives for deciding \(\neg B\).

For a conjunction, two positive branches give \(B\land C\). A negative \(B\) refutes the conjunction by its left projection, and a negative \(C\) refutes it by its right projection. For a disjunction, either positive branch supplies the appropriate inclusion. If both children are negative, eliminate an assumed \(B\lor C\) and obtain falsum in either branch; this gives its negation.

For implication, a negative \(B\) yields \(B\to C\) by explosion under a temporary \(B\). A positive \(C\) yields it by weakening. The remaining case has \(B\) and \(\neg C\); applying an assumed \(B\to C\) then gives falsum, hence \(\neg(B\to C)\). Each branch concludes the required decision disjunction, and its temporary assumptions are discharged. Finally \(\neg\bot\) gives the right alternative of the falsum decision. These arguments test supplied proof disjunctions, without assuming excluded middle for arbitrary formulas.

A strict zero interval is empty; an inclusive zero interval contains zero

For every full native \(A\), HA constructs \(U_A(0)\), \(\neg E_A(0)\), and both directions of

\[(\forall x\le0\,A(x))\leftrightarrow A(0),\qquad (\exists x\le0\,A(x))\leftrightarrow A(0).\]

The zero-bound laws refute \(x<0\). Under that guard, explosion gives \(A(x)\); discharge and close the arbitrary \(x\) to obtain \(U_A(0)\). Any witness of \(E_A(0)\) contains the impossible guard, which gives its negation after existential elimination.

For an inclusive guard \(x\le0\), the same order laws give \(x=0\). From \(A(0)\), logical equality transports the proof to \(A(x)\), giving the inclusive universal. Conversely instantiate it at zero and use reflexivity. An inclusive existential gives \(A(0)\) by eliminating its witness and transporting along \(x=0\); its converse introduces the witness zero with reflexivity. No decision for \(A\) is used in these four conversions.

Increasing a strict bound adds exactly one endpoint

For every full native \(A\), HA constructs both directions of

\[E_A(Sn)\leftrightarrow E_A(n)\lor A(n),\qquad U_A(Sn)\leftrightarrow U_A(n)\land A(n).\]

For the existential, open a witness \(x\) below \(Sn\). The successor-segment split gives \(x<n\lor x=n\). The first branch retains the same \(A(x)\) and supplies \(E_A(n)\). The second transports it to \(A(n)\). Conversely a witness below \(n\) is below \(Sn\), and the endpoint \(n\) itself is below \(Sn\). Each branch introduces the existential with that specified witness.

The universal recurrence is the already proved history recurrence. In its reverse direction, open \(x<Sn\), split it in the same way, and use either the old universal or equality transport of the endpoint proof. In its forward direction, restrict the interval and instantiate at \(n\). Thus these recurrences preserve their full native bodies, including quantified ones, before any decision premise is supplied.

Reuse the proved internal finite-search dichotomy

Given \(D_A\), apply the preceding bounded-search operation at the specified bound term. Its certificate is

\[F_A(t)\equiv N_A(t)\lor\exists m\,(m<t\land M_A(m)),\]

where \(N_A(t)=\forall x(x<t\to\neg A(x))\) and \(M_A(m)=A(m)\land N_A(m)\). This is one actual native certificate with variable \(t\) and fixed parameters.

The original proof applies ordinary HA induction to the displayed search body. Its base uses the empty strict interval. At a successor it retains an existing least witness, or uses the new endpoint decision to extend the negative history or produce that endpoint as a least witness. We reuse that proof operation and its exact arbitrary-term instantiation. No induction axiom over arbitrary semantic predicates or external bound values is added here.

The search dichotomy decides a bounded existential

A supplied \(D_A\) constructs the decision \(E_A(t)\lor\neg E_A(t)\) at every specified bound term.

Eliminate the supplied \(F_A(t)\). In its left branch, suppose \(E_A(t)\) and open its protected witness \(m\). The negative history, instantiated at \(m\) and applied to \(m<t\), gives \(\neg A(m)\), contradicting the witness's \(A(m)\). Close the witness and discharge \(E_A(t)\). This constructs the negative decision alternative.

In the right branch, open \(m<t\land M_A(m)\). Its minimum component includes \(A(m)\), so the same \(m\) witnesses \(E_A(t)\). Introduce the positive alternative and close the protected witness. Both branches conclude the identical decision formula. Minimality of the supplied witness is more than this operation needs, but its positive component and exact bound are retained.

Search for a negative instance to decide a bounded universal

The same supplied \(D_A\) constructs an HA certificate of \(U_A(t)\lor\neg U_A(t)\), for every bound term \(t\).

First obtain \(D_{\neg A}\). At an arbitrary input, the supplied decision gives either \(A\), which refutes \(\neg A\) and hence proves \(\neg\neg A\), or \(\neg A\) itself. Close the input to obtain the required disjunction for the negative predicate. Apply finite search to that exact predicate, obtaining \(F_{\neg A}(t)\).

If the search supplies a witness \(m<t\) of \(\neg A(m)\), it refutes \(U_A(t)\): instantiating an assumed universal at \(m\) gives \(A(m)\). Close the witness and introduce the negative alternative.

Otherwise its negative history says \(\forall x(x<t\to\neg\neg A(x))\). Open arbitrary \(x<t\). Instantiate \(D_A\) at this same \(x\). Its positive branch gives \(A(x)\); its negative branch contradicts the supplied double negation, and explosion gives \(A(x)\) there too. Close the guard and the input, obtaining \(U_A(t)\) and the positive alternative. This proof constructs the alternatives directly; it does not infer a counterexample from an arbitrary negated universal.

Transport the decisions to inclusive bounds

From \(D_A\), HA also constructs decisions for \(\forall x\le t\,A(x)\) and \(\exists x\le t\,A(x)\).

The successor-bound law gives \(x\le t\leftrightarrow x<St\). Transform each guarded universal in either direction by opening an arbitrary input and converting its guard. Transform each guarded existential by retaining its witness and converting the guard in its conjunction. These operations give both directions between an inclusive formula at \(t\) and its strict counterpart at \(St\).

Use the strict decision at \(St\). In its positive branch, apply the appropriate conversion. In its negative branch, an assumed inclusive formula would convert to the refuted strict formula; discharge to obtain the inclusive negation. This transports an actual decision at the stated term, including \(t=0\), without changing the native conclusion to a different bound notation.

Reindex a fresh input before invoking the finite-parameter induction rule

During a recursive quantifier case the names consist of the fixed finite parameter tuple together with a fresh input. This is still a finite ordered tuple. To use the original finite-parameter induction operation, choose its explicit bijection with a numbered tuple, rename the body, bound and fixed context together, perform the operation there, and rename the resulting certificate back.

For \(k\) old names and one new name, keep the old \(k\) positions in their order and give the new input the final position in a tuple of length \(k+1\). The inverse sends that last position back to the protected input. Extend either name map under any further binder by fixing its separate bound slot. The two composites are the identity, so capture-free scope identities restore the original body and the specified bound term literally. Renaming also restores the same finite additional context and leaves the closed arithmetic axiom support unchanged.

Repeated nesting repeats this finite bookkeeping. A quantifier introduction still requires its input to be fresh for the lifted context; reindexing does not discharge or bypass that condition. Thus every use of numeric induction is an original HA instance with a specified finite parameter list, rather than a new schema on arbitrary name universes.

Every bounded formula has its uniform native HA decision

For each bounded expression \(B(\bar y)\) over a finite ordered parameter tuple, construct an actual HA certificate, with no additional assumptions, of

\[\mathcal E(B)(\bar y)\lor\neg\mathcal E(B)(\bar y).\]

Closing the tuple gives a certificate of its universal closure. These are uniform internal proofs with arbitrary parameter values and variable bounds.

Induct externally on the fixed bounded expression tree. Falsum, equality and the two order atoms use their existing decision certificates. Each Boolean constructor applies to the certificates already constructed for its children.

At a bounded quantifier, recursively decide its body with its new input left free. Lift the empty context and universally introduce that input; the result is the exact \(D_A\) for \(A=\mathcal E(\text{body})\). The bound term mentions only the older names. Apply the strict existential or universal decision operation, or its inclusive version, at this same bound term. Use the finite-name reindexing operation where the parameter list has been enlarged. The expansion clauses identify the resulting conclusion with the actual native expansion of this constructor.

This structural induction is finite recursion over syntax. The variable-length searches it invokes are already native induction proofs inside HA. Finally use the finite universal-closure operation in the specified parameter order. An empty parameter tuple requires no introductions. Every support union remains finite and contains only original HA axioms.

Keep the exact arbitrary-term instances of the uniform proof

For any specified tuple of arithmetic terms \(\bar s\), substituting \(\bar s\) for \(\bar y\) yields an HA certificate of \(\mathcal E(B[\bar s])\lor\neg\mathcal E(B[\bar s])\), under any finite additional context over the remaining names.

Apply the established certificate term-substitution operation to the decision certificate just constructed, then weaken its empty additional context to the chosen one. The expansion substitution identity identifies its literal conclusion with the stated native instance. Equivalently instantiate the closed universal certificate at the same term tuple. The terms may themselves contain variables, additions and products; no reduction to a list of external numeral assignments occurs.

Every expanded bounded formula is stable in HA

For every bounded \(B\), HA constructs a certificate of

\[\neg\neg\mathcal E(B)\to\mathcal E(B),\]

uniformly in its finite parameters, with the corresponding closed and arbitrary-term instances.

Under a temporary \(\neg\neg\mathcal E(B)\), eliminate the proved decision \(\mathcal E(B)\lor\neg\mathcal E(B)\). Retain the positive branch. The negative branch contradicts the temporary double negation, so explosion gives the same positive conclusion. Discharge the temporary assumption. Closing or substituting parameters uses the same actual certificate operations as before. Stability is a consequence of the internal decision; it is not an appeal to classical double-negation elimination.

A later bound may depend on an earlier quantified input

Consider the bounded expression

\[P(a)\equiv\forall x<a\;\exists y\le x\;\bigl(x=y+y\lor x=S(y+y)\bigr).\]

Its inner bound is the earlier input \(x\), not its own new \(y\). The construction gives an HA certificate of \(\mathcal E(P(a))\lor\neg\mathcal E(P(a))\), and of its stability, with \(a\) still a free parameter.

First decide both displayed equations with \(a,x,y\) free, then their disjunction. Close only \(y\), apply the inclusive existential decision at the term \(x\), and retain \(a,x\) as parameters. Close only \(x\), then apply the strict universal decision at \(a\). The finite reindexing at these two stages preserves these distinct roles.

At \(a=0\), the outer strict interval is empty, so gives the positive certificate directly. At a successor, gives both directions of

\[\mathcal E(P(Sa))\leftrightarrow\mathcal E(P(a))\land\exists y\le a\,\bigl(a=y+y\lor a=S(y+y)\bigr).\]

This example records the decision construction and its exact zero and successor behavior. Proving the positive parity assertion for every \(a\) would be an additional arithmetic argument.

Is this the same as deciding each numerical instance or building a coded proof?

How does this result compare with the later bounded-formula results for Robinson and Peano arithmetic?

Show answerHide answer

The Robinson-arithmetic result chooses a standard numerical tuple and proves the true instance, or refutes the false one, using its external truth. Here the parameters remain variables in one intuitionistic HA certificate of a decision disjunction. Induction inside arithmetic supplies the variable-bound search.

The later PA signed proof builders produce numerical proof codes and prove that the actual checker accepts the specified conclusions. Our output is a native HA deduction certificate; no quoted proof code, primitive-recursive code builder or internal checker-correctness assertion is constructed here. These results have different outputs and are not substituted for one another.

Nor does the construction decide whether an arbitrary formula is HA-provable. It applies to the stated bounded expression grammar and constructs \(B\lor\neg B\) after expansion. It is not a procedure choosing a proof of one disjunct for every open parameter assignment, and adding an unbounded existential has no finite bound to which the argument applies.

The new stability certificate removes double negation from the actual expanded bounded formula. The earlier conservation boundary also requires transformations between that formula and its specified negative translation. Such a translation changes the expanded order guards and the native existential and disjunction constructors. The quantifier-free conversion proof does not provide those new guarded-quantifier conversions.

The required order-guard conversions and bounded-quantifier conversions are established in the bounded negative-translation development. With these extra steps the arithmetic interpretation yields PA-to-HA conservation for a bounded matrix under a finite universal prefix. The stability certificates proved here supply the double-negation removal used by that argument.
