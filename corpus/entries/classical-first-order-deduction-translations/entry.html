Keep two classical proof presentations distinct

Fix a language \(L\), a free-parameter set \(X\), and the full scoped formulas. The small presentation has only falsum, equality, relation atoms, implication and universal quantification as primitive formula constructors, with the established classical deduction rules for its abbreviated connectives. The full classical presentation uses the primitive full connectives and the classical contradiction rule. Unless explicitly disabled, logical equality is enabled in both presentations.

Write \(\Gamma\Rightarrow_{\mathrm s}D\) and \(\Gamma\Rightarrow_{\mathrm C}A\) for raw derivations in the respective presentations. Contexts are finite sets. A supplied full derivation is an actual finite tree. For the small presentation, we will assert existence of a corresponding full tree, without identifying different proofs of the same sequent.

Use the expansion and inclusion \(c\) and \(i\). They leave term trees, free parameters and available bound slots unchanged. For a full formula define its expanded full formula by \(E(A)=i(c(A))\). For a context, \(c[\Gamma]\), \(i[\Gamma]\) and \(E[\Gamma]\) mean its finite images. The identity \(c(i(D))=D\) holds literally for every small formula; \(E(A)=A\) is not being assumed.

Inclusion commutes with the actual scope operations

For every small scoped formula \(D\) and compatible scope substitution \(\sigma\),

\[i(D\langle\sigma\rangle)=i(D)\langle\sigma\rangle.\]

Consequently inclusion commutes with free substitution, free renaming, opening available slots and instantiation at a supplied term. So does \(E\). Both maps retain the same free support and the equality-free and quantifier-free fragment tests.

Induct on the five small constructors, with source and target scopes arbitrary. An atom uses identical substituted terms on both sides, and falsum is unchanged. Implication uses its two child identities. Under a universal binder both traversals use the same protected lift of \(\sigma\); apply the body's induction hypothesis and restore that binder. Each special operation is the indicated scope substitution, so its identity follows. The support and fragment claims follow by the same induction and the corresponding expansion laws. Composing the inclusion identity with the expansion identity proves the assertions for \(E\), including under internal binders.

Distinguish primitive negation from its implication code

For a full formula \(A\), define its implicational negation by \(N(A)=A\to\bot\). This is an implication tree, whereas \(\neg A\) has primitive negation at its root. In either full deduction mode, these formulas have derived introduction and elimination conversions.

Given \(\neg A\), assume \(A\), use negation elimination to obtain falsum, and discharge \(A\) by implication introduction. This gives \(N(A)\). Conversely, given \(N(A)\), assume \(A\), use implication elimination, and discharge \(A\) by primitive-negation introduction. Both conversions work in any finite ambient context, are transformations of supplied trees, and use no equality or classical rule.

The classical connective codes in the full syntax are

\[K(A,B)=N(A\to N(B)),\qquad D(A,B)=N(A)\to B,\qquad H(B)=N(\forall x\,N(B)).\]

The argument of \(H\) is a one-slot body; its displayed universal binder binds that slot. These are exactly the full trees obtained by including the small abbreviations for conjunction, disjunction and existence. In particular, the selected disjunction code is \(N(A)\to B\).

Derive the conjunction rules for the implication code

In full classical deduction, proofs of \(A\) and \(B\) give a proof of \(K(A,B)\). A proof of \(K(A,B)\) gives a proof of either \(A\) or \(B\). All outputs have the same ambient context as the supplied proofs, and the operations work with equality disabled.

For introduction, assume \(h:A\to N(B)\). Apply \(h\) to the supplied proof of \(A\), then apply the resulting \(N(B)\) to the supplied proof of \(B\). Discharging \(h\) gives \(K(A,B)\).

For the first projection, use classical contradiction with temporary \(\neg A\). Under an additional \(A\), negation elimination gives falsum; under any further \(B\) this still gives falsum. Discharge \(B\) and then \(A\), obtaining \(A\to N(B)\). The supplied \(K(A,B)\) now gives falsum, so classical contradiction yields \(A\). For the second projection temporarily assume \(\neg B\). Under \(A\) and then \(B\), derive falsum from \(B,\neg B\); discharge both temporary assumptions and again apply \(K(A,B)\). Classical contradiction yields \(B\). Weakening supplies the ambient proofs in every temporary context.

Derive case analysis for the selected disjunction code

A proof of either \(A\) or \(B\) gives a proof of \(D(A,B)\). A proof of \(D(A,B)\), a branch proof of \(C\) from \(\Gamma,A\), and a branch proof of \(C\) from \(\Gamma,B\) give a proof of \(C\) from \(\Gamma\), in the full classical presentation with either equality mode.

From \(A\), assume \(N(A)\), derive falsum, and use explosion to obtain \(B\); discharge \(N(A)\). From \(B\), simply weaken its proof by the temporary \(N(A)\) and discharge that assumption.

For elimination, temporarily assume primitive \(\neg C\). Under an additional \(A\), the first branch gives \(C\), hence falsum. Discharge \(A\) by implication introduction, giving \(N(A)\). Apply the supplied \(D(A,B)\) to obtain \(B\). Replace the temporary \(B\)-assumptions in the second branch by that proof; it gives \(C\), contradicting \(\neg C\). Classical contradiction discharges \(\neg C\). Every branch application is the established proof replacement operation, so this derivation introduces no additional primitive inference.

Supply a term to the existential code

Let \(B\) be a full one-slot body and \(t\) a term over the current free parameters. A supplied derivation of \(B(t)\) gives one of \(H(B)\), in either full deduction mode and either equality mode.

Assume \(\forall x\,N(B)\). Universal elimination at the same term \(t\) gives \(N(B(t))\), because instantiation commutes with implication and falsum. Apply this formula to the supplied proof of \(B(t)\), obtaining falsum. Implication introduction discharges the universal assumption and gives \(H(B)\). The term is unchanged, so no existence assumption about ground terms has been added.

Discharge the witness branch of the existential code

Suppose \(\Gamma\Rightarrow_{\mathrm C}H(B)\), and suppose a supplied branch derives \(C^\uparrow\) from \(\Gamma^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(B)\). Here the upward arrow renames original parameters into the left component of \(X\sqcup\{e\}\), so \(e\) occurs in neither the ambient assumptions nor \(C^\uparrow\). Then \(\Gamma\Rightarrow_{\mathrm C}C\). Equality may be disabled.

Temporarily assume primitive \(\neg C\), and lift this enlarged context to the fresh parameter \(e\). Under an additional \(\operatorname{open}(B)\), the supplied branch, weakened by the lifted \(\neg C\), gives \(C^\uparrow\). Negation elimination gives falsum. Discharge the opened body by implication introduction to obtain \(N(\operatorname{open}(B))\).

Opening commutes with the code \(N\). The last formula is therefore exactly \(\operatorname{open}(N(B))\), and universal introduction gives \(\forall x\,N(B)\) under \(\Gamma,\neg C\). Its freshness condition holds: \(e\) is absent from every lifted original assumption and from the lifted temporary \(\neg C\). Apply the supplied \(H(B)\), weakened to that context, to derive falsum. Classical contradiction discharges \(\neg C\) and concludes \(C\).

This construction consumes the supplied witness branch; it does not choose an object satisfying \(B\). Its only quantified introduction is the protected universal rule, so it works even when the original parameter set supplies no term.

Expand a supplied full derivation into the small calculus

A supplied full raw derivation of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\), in either deduction mode and either equality mode, gives a small classical derivation of \(c[\Gamma]\Rightarrow c(A)\).

Recurse on the full tree. Map each formula by \(c\) and use the corresponding established small rule. Full conjunction becomes the small abbreviated conjunction, and full disjunction becomes its selected small abbreviation; those are exactly the end formulas of the small conjunction and disjunction rules. Primitive negation becomes small negation. The classical rule, if it occurs, is copied. A disabled rule has no input case to transform; enabling more rules in the target causes no difficulty.

For a term instance use \(c(B(t))=c(B)(t)\), with the same term. For a protected fresh branch use \(c(\operatorname{open}(B))=\operatorname{open}(c(B))\) and the corresponding context identity. Hence the transformed branch still obeys the actual freshness restriction. Equality replacement uses \(c(B)\) as its full small-syntax body, not merely an atomic substitute. These are literal scope identities. The recursion consequently gives a derivation in the original small calculus, without appeal to truth or completeness.

Convert a full formula to its expansion and back by proofs

For every complete full formula \(A\), every finite full context \(\Gamma\), and either equality mode, there are specified tree operations

\[\Gamma\Rightarrow_{\mathrm C}A\ \longmapsto\ \Gamma\Rightarrow_{\mathrm C}E(A),\qquad \Gamma\Rightarrow_{\mathrm C}E(A)\ \longmapsto\ \Gamma\Rightarrow_{\mathrm C}A.\]

Use strong induction on logical depth, with every free-parameter set and ambient context arbitrary. At falsum, equality and relation atoms the formula is unchanged, so keep its supplied proof. The recursive calls below concern strict child depths; opening a quantified body changes its free-parameter set but preserves its depth.

For implication, convert an assumed expanded antecedent back to its original child, apply the original implication, and convert its consequent forward. Discharge the expanded antecedent. The reverse operation exchanges the two directions. For primitive negation, the forward operation assumes the expanded child, converts it back, and obtains falsum from the original negation; implication introduction gives its code \(N(E(A))\). Conversely assume the original child, convert it forward, apply the coded negation, and discharge by primitive-negation introduction.

For conjunction, project the original proof, convert both child proofs forward, and apply coded conjunction introduction. Conversely use the coded projections, convert both children back, and use primitive conjunction introduction. For disjunction, apply primitive case analysis and the two coded introductions in the forward direction. In the reverse direction apply coded case analysis and primitive disjunction introduction after converting the selected child back. Ambient proof replacement and weakening perform every indicated use of a supplied child conversion.

For a universal formula, lift its proof and context to a fresh \(e\), instantiate at \(e\), apply the child conversion to the opened body, and introduce the universal again. The scope identities identify the converted body with the opening of its expansion; the new parameter remains absent from the ambient context. Use the reverse child conversion for the reverse direction.

For an existential formula, eliminate its primitive witness with a fresh \(e\), convert that opened body forward, and apply coded existential introduction at \(e\). Its conclusion is the lift of the desired closed code, so primitive existential elimination discharges \(e\). Conversely apply coded existential elimination: convert the opened expanded body back and introduce the primitive existential at \(e\). Both witness conclusions exclude \(e\). The already proved macro rules supply the required classical steps. No equality rule is used by any conversion, so either equality mode is retained.

Compare arbitrary full end formulas with the small calculus

For every finite full context \(\Gamma\) and complete full formula \(A\), a full classical raw derivation of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\) exists if and only if \(c[\Gamma]\Rightarrow_{\mathrm s}c(A)\) is derivable. Logical equality is enabled in this equivalence.

Expansion proves the forward implication. For the converse, gives existence of a proof of \(E[\Gamma]\Rightarrow E(A)\). For every \(B\in\Gamma\), its assumption proof from \(\Gamma\), converted by , supplies a proof of \(E(B)\) from \(\Gamma\). Use those proofs in the replacement operation for the translated tree. It gives \(\Gamma\Rightarrow E(A)\); the reverse round-trip operation gives \(\Gamma\Rightarrow A\). If several source formulas have the same expansion, any corresponding supplied proof has the same required end formula, so finite image contexts cause no ambiguity.

Transport the same auxiliary reserve in both presentations

The raw translations induce translations between the public deduction certificates that permit a disjoint reserve \(U=\{u_0,u_1,\ldots\}\). A supplied full certificate gives a small certificate after expansion. A small certificate gives existence of a full classical certificate after inclusion. Their end contexts and conclusions have exactly the same original parameters.

Let \(j:X\to X\sqcup U\) be the left inclusion. A full certificate is a raw proof whose endsequent is \(j[\Gamma]\Rightarrow A[j]\). Its expanded endsequent is \(j[c[\Gamma]]\Rightarrow c(A)[j]\), because expansion commutes with renaming. This is precisely a small certificate. In the other direction include the small raw reserve proof, and use \(i(D[j])=i(D)[j]\) and the image-context identity. The reserve stays in its right-hand component; no auxiliary name enters an original end formula. The same argument applies to the two round-trip operations on full certificates.

The two public classical consequence relations agree

For every finite small \(\Gamma,D\), public derivability of \(D\) from \(\Gamma\) is equivalent to existence of a full classical certificate of \(i(D)\) from \(i[\Gamma]\). For every finite full \(\Gamma,A\), public full classical derivability is equivalent to small public derivability of \(c(A)\) from \(c[\Gamma]\). Both equivalences use logical equality.

Retain finite support for arbitrary sets of full assumptions

Let \(T\) be any set of complete full formulas. Full classical consequence \(T\vdash_{\mathrm C}A\), with logical equality, holds if and only if small classical consequence \(c[T]\vdash_{\mathrm s}c(A)\) holds. Here \(c[T]=\{c(B):B\in T\}\), and both consequence relations mean existence of a public certificate from a finite supporting subset.

A finite full support \(\Gamma\subseteq T\) expands to the finite support \(c[\Gamma]\subseteq c[T]\). Use the finite public equivalence. Conversely a small proof uses a finite support \(\Delta\subseteq c[T]\). For each of these finitely many formulas choose a full preimage in \(T\); their finite collection \(\Gamma\) satisfies \(\Delta\subseteq c[\Gamma]\). Weaken the small proof to \(c[\Gamma]\), apply the finite public equivalence, and retain \(\Gamma\subseteq T\) as its full support. This finite selection does not require one uniform choice for every member of the possibly infinite set \(T\). It proves the stated consequence equivalence.

The classical extension of Heyting arithmetic gives an arithmetic specialization of this correspondence: its expanded axiom set is exactly the earlier PA theory, including all induction instances.

Composing this classical bridge with first-order negative translation interprets the established smaller classical presentation in intuitionistic native deduction from translated assumptions.

Replacing translated arithmetic axioms by their constructive arithmetic proofs gives the exact PA-to-HA negative interpretation.

The translated proofs retain substituted end formulas

Let \(\sigma:X\to\operatorname{Term}(Y)\) be any free-parameter substitution. Substituting a supplied full proof and then expanding gives a small proof of exactly \(c[\Gamma][\sigma]\Rightarrow c(A)[\sigma]\). Substituting a small proof and then including gives existence of a full proof of exactly \(i[\Gamma][\sigma]\Rightarrow i(D)[\sigma]\). The same assertions hold for public certificates, whose reserve names are left fixed.

Apply the corresponding proof substitution operation first, then the appropriate translation. Its endpoints are the displayed ones by the inclusion and expansion substitution identities. In every quantified branch the substitution is lifted by protecting the new right-tagged parameter, so the same instance and opening identities apply after translation. For a public certificate extend \(\sigma\) by the identity on the auxiliary reserve. These assertions identify the resulting endsequents; they do not assert equality between different proof trees or require a particular normal form for them.

Does proof equivalence recover the original full tree?

Do the round-trip proof conversions imply \(E(A)=A\) as full formula trees?

No. Take \(A=\bot\land\bot\). Its root is primitive conjunction, while \(E(A)=N(\bot\to N(\bot))\) has implication at its root. The trees are unequal. Their classical interderivability is instead supplied by the conjunction macro rules and the round-trip construction. In contrast, \(c(i(D))=D\) really is a syntactic identity for a small tree \(D\), because both maps copy its five constructors. These are different conclusions about different composites.

Does changing presentation remove the need for auxiliary names?

In the empty language with no displayed free parameters, the full formula \(A=\exists x(\bot\to\bot)\) has a public certificate. Does its expansion therefore have a raw small-presentation proof with no available parameter?

No. The given full certificate uses an auxiliary variable term in existential introduction. Copy it into the classical mode and expand it to obtain a small public certificate of \(c(A)\). Its auxiliary name is still available internally.

If a raw small proof of \(c(A)\) existed over the empty parameter set, inclusion would give existence of a raw full proof of \(E(A)\). The reverse round-trip operation would give a raw full proof of \(A\). This contradicts the empty-domain raw obstruction already established for \(A\), which applies even with classical contradiction and logical equality enabled. The translations preserve the raw/public distinction; a change of formula presentation supplies no term missing from the original raw proof.
