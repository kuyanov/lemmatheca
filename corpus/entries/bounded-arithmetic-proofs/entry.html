Separate evaluating a term from proving its value

Fix an arithmetic term \(t(\vec x)\). Evaluation computes a number \(t(\vec n)\); the function \(v_t\) constructed here returns a proof code whose conclusion is

\[t(\overline{\vec n})=\overline{t(\vec n)}.\]

The displayed equation contains numeral terms. In the correctness assertion, however, \(\vec n\) are variables used by the code-building functions. PA proves one universally closed assertion that the actual checker accepts \(v_t(\vec n)\) with the computed conclusion code.

Proof

Induct outside arithmetic on the fixed term \(t\). A variable becomes its input numeral, and zero remains zero, so reflexivity gives both cases. For \(Su\), retain the certificate normalizing \(u\), apply successor congruence, and use that the numeral constructor satisfies \(\operatorname{num}(n+1)=\operatorname{succ}(\operatorname{num}(n))\).

For \(u+w\), first normalize both subterms. Congruence gives a proof of \(u(\overline{\vec n})+w(\overline{\vec n})=\overline m+\overline n\), where \(m=u(\vec n)\) and \(n=w(\vec n)\). Append the addition certificate below and equality transitivity. Multiplication is the same construction with its own numerical certificate. The finite structural recursion on \(t\), composition and the numerical recursions produce primitive-recursive programs.

The correctness proof in any PA model uses the induction hypotheses for the subterms and the checked congruence and transitivity constructors. The numerical recursions require PA induction on their numeric input, which is supplied in the next two lemmas. Hence the assertion holds in every PA model, and completeness gives the stated PA derivation. □

The addition builder follows the recursive equations

The specified primitive-recursive addition builder returns an accepted certificate for \(\overline m+\overline n=\overline{m+n}\) at arbitrary internal inputs in every PA model.

Proof

Let \(A(m,n)\) be a certificate for \(\overline m+\overline n=\overline{m+n}\). Its recursion has a fixed base constructor and a fixed step constructor:

\[A(m,0)=\operatorname{AddBase}(m).\]
\[A(m,n+1)=\operatorname{AddStep}(m,n,A(m,n)).\]

Here \(\operatorname{AddBase}\) specializes the fixed PA proof of \(\forall x\,(x+0=x)\). The step specializes \(x+Sy=S(x+y)\), applies successor congruence to the previous equation, and composes the two equalities. Its resulting conclusion is exactly

\[\overline m+S\overline n=S(\overline m+\overline n)=S\overline{m+n}=\overline{m+(n+1)}.\]

In any PA model, induct on \(n\) with invariant: the checker accepts \(A(m,n)\) with the code of the displayed numerical addition equation. The base uses the certificate for the fixed axiom and universal elimination. At the successor, the specialization, congruence and transitivity preservation lemmas verify the exact new record. The conclusion-code identity uses the arithmetic equation \(m+(n+1)=S(m+n)\) and the internal numeral recursion.

Every component of the invariant is arithmetically defined; \(m\) is an allowed parameter. Thus this is PA induction at arbitrary internal \(n\), not an external traversal through ordinary natural numbers. □

For example, the certificate for \(\overline2+\overline2=\overline4\) retains the base proof \(\overline2+0=\overline2\), extends it to \(\overline2+\overline1=\overline3\), and extends it once more. The program builds these proof records; it does not simply evaluate the two sides and mark the equation accepted.

Multiplication reuses the addition builder

The specified primitive-recursive multiplication builder returns an accepted certificate for \(\overline m\cdot\overline n=\overline{mn}\) at arbitrary internal inputs in every PA model.

Proof

Define \(P(m,n)\) to produce a certificate for \(\overline m\cdot\overline n=\overline{mn}\). Start by specializing \(x\cdot0=0\). At the successor, specialize \(x\cdot Sy=x\cdot y+x\), retain \(P(m,n)\), use congruence for addition, and append \(A(mn,m)\). The equality chain is

\[\overline m\cdot S\overline n=\overline m\cdot\overline n+\overline m=\overline{mn}+\overline m=\overline{mn+m}.\]

The recursion \(P(m,0)=\operatorname{MulBase}(m)\), \(P(m,n+1)=\operatorname{MulStep}(m,n,P(m,n))\) is primitive recursive. In each PA model the induction invariant is acceptance with the exact multiplication-equation code. The base is checked axiom specialization. The step uses the preceding record, the verified addition builder, congruence and transitivity. PA's recursive multiplication equation identifies the final numeral with \(\overline{m(n+1)}\). Definable induction proves the invariant for all internal inputs. □

Build certificates on both sides of a comparison

There are specified primitive-recursive builders for \(\overline m=\overline n\), its negation, \(\overline m\le\overline n\), and its negation. PA proves the correctness implication for each builder on its corresponding branch. All four implications retain the actual code-building and checker graphs.

Proof

For \(m=n\), the two numeral codes coincide, and reflexivity gives the equality certificate. For \(m\le n\), compute \(d=n-m\) by truncated subtraction. PA proves \(d+m=n\). The addition certificate proves \(\overline d+\overline m=\overline n\); existential introduction with witness \(\overline d\) proves \(\overline m\le\overline n\), using the definition \(m\le n\iff\exists d\,(d+m=n)\).

When \(m>n\), compute \(e=\operatorname{pred}(m-n)\). PA proves \(m=S(n+e)\). A fixed PA theorem states \(\forall x\,\forall y\,\neg(S(x+y)\le x)\). Specialize it at \(\overline n,\overline e\), normalize \(S(\overline n+\overline e)\) to \(\overline m\), and use equality substitution. This yields a certificate for \(\neg(\overline m\le\overline n)\). The arithmetic identities used for this branch hold at arbitrary internal values; the proof is not a search through the digits of an ordinary numeral.

Finally, if \(m\ne n\), totality of order says that either \(m\nleq n\) or \(n\nleq m\). Use the negative order builder in the rejected direction. A fixed PA proof says that equality would imply that order relation, so implication elimination gives \(\overline m\ne\overline n\). The actual program selects the direction by the Boolean order test. Its internal correctness follows by the two cases, the numerical normalization lemmas and the checked proof constructors. Completeness gives a PA proof of each universally quantified correctness implication. □

Accumulate a bounded truth test by a Boolean loop

Fix a bounded formula \(D(\vec x)\). We construct a primitive-recursive test \(d_D\), taking values in \(\{0,1\}\), such that PA proves \(d_D(\vec x)=1\leftrightarrow D(\vec x)\). Equality uses the numerical equality test, and \(s\le t\) is tested by \(s\mathbin{\dot-}t=0\), with truncated subtraction. Falsum has value zero. Implication uses Boolean implication: its output is zero exactly when its antecedent test is one and its consequent test is zero.

For a Boolean test \(q(\vec x,i)\), define a loop over the strict initial segment:

\[L_q(\vec x,0)=1,\qquad L_q(\vec x,j+1)=L_q(\vec x,j)\,q(\vec x,j).\]

The test of \(\forall i\le n\,B(\vec x,i)\) is \(L_{d_B}(\vec x,n+1)\). The test of \(\exists i\le n\,B(\vec x,i)\) is \(1-L_{1-d_B}(\vec x,n+1)\). First evaluate a term bound \(t(\vec x)\), then use its value for \(n\).

Proof

Within any PA model prove simultaneously by definable induction that \(L_q(\vec x,j)\) is Boolean and

\[L_q(\vec x,j)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall i<j\;q(\vec x,i)=1.\]

The zero case has no smaller inputs. At the successor, a product of two Boolean values equals one exactly when both equal one. Split \(i<j+1\) into \(i<j\) or \(i=j\) to prove both directions of the invariant. The existential test is the Boolean negation of the universal test of the negated matrix. An external structural induction on the fixed formula combines these loops with the atomic and connective cases. Every resulting internal equivalence holds in every PA model, so completeness gives the stated PA proofs. □

Strict bounds are reduced without changing the quoted conclusion

The bounded formula convention also permits \(\forall z<t\,B\) and \(\exists z<t\,B\). The core loops use inclusive bounds. Normalize a strict bounded quantifier by

\[\forall z<t\,B\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\forall z\le t\,(z\ne t\to B),\]
\[\exists z<t\,B\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\exists z\le t\,(z\ne t\land B).\]

The term \(t\) does not contain the quantified variable. These identities hold also when \(t=0\): the universal guard has no satisfying value and the existential guard is never true. Recursive normalization replaces inner strict bounds as well, producing a formula \(D^*\) in the inclusive bounded grammar. PA proves \(D\leftrightarrow D^*\), by the elementary order equivalence \(z<t\leftrightarrow(z\le t\land z\ne t)\) and induction on the fixed formula.

Use the truth test for \(D^*\), and compose its correctness with that equivalence. For proofs, code equality is more demanding. A certificate for \(D^*(\overline{\vec x})\) is not yet a certificate for the literal original sentence \(D(\overline{\vec x})\). Fix a PA proof of \(\forall\vec x\,(D^*(\vec x)\to D(\vec x))\). The native specialization program specializes it at the internally constructed numeral terms. Combine that specialized implication with the normalized certificate by the checked modus-ponens program. The negative builder uses the fixed implication \(\neg D^*\to\neg D\) in the same way.

The original formula and its conversion proof are fixed outside arithmetic. The specialization and modus-ponens operations have internally proved correctness at arbitrary input values. Their output therefore has the original numeral-substitution code, including its literal strict-bound syntax. The signed-builder conclusion below applies to both bounded-quantifier conventions.

A bounded search retains the latest witness

A failed universal test or a successful existential test needs a particular internal witness. The following primitive-recursive loop is sufficient for a Boolean test \(q\):

\[W_q(\vec x,0)=0.\]
\[W_q(\vec x,j+1)=\begin{cases}j,&q(\vec x,j)=1,\\ W_q(\vec x,j),&q(\vec x,j)=0.\end{cases}\]

Put \(w_q(\vec x,n)=W_q(\vec x,n+1)\). If some \(i\le n\) has \(q(\vec x,i)=1\), then \(w_q(\vec x,n)\le n\) and \(q(\vec x,w_q(\vec x,n))=1\). No property is required when the bounded search has no witness.

Proof

Use the invariant: if a witness exists below \(j\), the retained value is below \(j\) and satisfies \(q\). The zero case has false antecedent. At \(j+1\), if \(q(\vec x,j)=1\), the update chooses \(j\) and the conclusion follows directly. Otherwise every witness below \(j+1\) is strictly below \(j\), so the previous retained witness works. The invariant is arithmetically definable with \(\vec x\) as parameters; PA induction proves it for all internal \(j\). Substituting \(j=n+1\) gives the assertion. □

The loop retains the latest successful index. Leastness is unnecessary: all subsequent proof constructors need only a witness together with its verified bound.

Boolean branches become literal proof records

The signed proof-builder construction covers falsum and arithmetic equality and order atoms, and is closed under implication.

Proof

For falsum, the positive implication has false antecedent, while a fixed proof of \(\neg\bot\) supplies the negative builder. An equality or order atom first normalizes its terms by , uses the appropriate numerical comparison certificate, and transports the conclusion back along the normalization equalities.

For \(B\to F\), test \(F\) first. If it is true, retain its positive proof, add the temporary assumption \(B\), and discharge it by implication introduction. If \(F\) is false and \(B\to F\) is true, \(B\) is false: retain its negative proof, assume \(B\), derive falsum, obtain \(F\) by falsum elimination, and discharge \(B\). This explains both truth branches of the actual selector.

When \(B\to F\) is false, \(B\) is true and \(F\) is false. Retain \(c_B^+\) and \(c_F^-\); under the temporary assumption \(B\to F\), implication elimination produces \(F\), contradicting its negative proof. Discharging that temporary assumption proves \(\neg(B\to F)\).

Each construction is a fixed composition of native rule constructors. The checker preservation lemmas verify its children, contexts and conclusion. Conjunction, disjunction and negation can be read as the fixed logical abbreviations of the shared formula syntax; they require no additional primitive proof rule.

Prove that an element below a numeral is one of its predecessors

Define finite-case formulas by \(E_0(z):=(z=0)\) and \(E_{j+1}(z):=E_j(z)\lor z=\overline{j+1}\). A primitive-recursive builder returns a certificate for

\[\forall z\,(z\le\overline j\to E_j(z)).\]

Proof

The base is a fixed PA proof of \(z\le0\to z=0\). For the successor, specialize the fixed PA theorem \(z\le Su\to(z\le u\lor z=Su)\) at \(u=\overline j\). The proof-code recursion builds the nested disjunction and the corresponding proof at the same time. The base and successor records are checked using the literal syntax recursions and the fixed fresh auxiliary constant. Definable induction proves that invariant in every PA model. For an internal nonstandard value of \(j\), the formula code is an internal iterated-disjunction code. We do not pretend that it is an ordinary finite formula written externally. The assertion about its code and checker acceptance is nevertheless an ordinary fixed arithmetic formula.

A true bounded universal uses a recursive certificate loop

Given signed builders for a fixed bounded body, a primitive-recursive certificate loop proves its numeral-bound universal on the branch where every tested instance is true.

Proof

Suppose the signed builders for \(B(\vec x,z)\) have already been constructed. Temporarily use a numeral bound \(\overline n\). Let \(u_B(\vec x,j)\) be the code of \(\forall z\le\overline j\,B(\overline{\vec x},z)\), computed by the numeral and quantified-formula constructors. Let \(U_B(\vec x,j)\) be the certificate assembled for \(\forall z\le\overline j\,B(\overline{\vec x},z)\). Its recursion is

\[U_B(\vec x,0)=\operatorname{Base}(c_B^+(\vec x,0)),\]
\[U_B(\vec x,j+1)=\operatorname{Step}(j,U_B(\vec x,j),c_B^+(\vec x,j+1)).\]

The base uses the finite-case proof for zero. With fresh \(c_0\), assume \(c_0\le0\), derive \(c_0=0\), and transport the positive proof of \(B(\overline{\vec x},0)\) by equality substitution. Discharge the assumption and universally introduce the open variable.

At the successor, the fixed decomposition \(c_0\le\overline{j+1}\to(c_0\le\overline j\lor c_0=\overline{j+1})\) gives two cases. Instantiate the preceding bounded universal in the first; transport the endpoint proof in the second. Disjunction elimination, implication introduction and universal introduction close the proof.

Inside any PA model use the invariant

\[\bigl(\forall i\le j\;d_B(\vec x,i)=1\bigr)\to\operatorname{Acc}(U_B(\vec x,j),u_B(\vec x,j)).\]

The notation in the conclusion denotes the code-building operation just described, with its numeral-bound code computed from \(j\). The base uses the signed hypothesis at zero. For the step, the antecedent restricts to all \(i\le j\), giving the previous accepted record, and supplies the endpoint test. The positive body hypothesis and the checked step constructor finish the step. All syntax, freshness and context conditions were verified before applying induction. This is definable PA induction with \(\vec x\) as parameters. □

Restore the original bound term in the conclusion code

For either quantifier and either sign, the specified bound-transport procedure converts an accepted numeral-bound proof into an accepted proof with the original closed term as its bound.

Proof

The four preceding constructions use the numeral \(\overline n\), where \(n=t(\vec x)\). Their target, however, must have the original closed term \(t(\overline{\vec x})\) as its bound. Equal values alone do not make these two formula codes identical.

The term-normalization builder produces a certificate for \(t(\overline{\vec x})=\overline n\). Append equality symmetry, giving the direction needed to transport a proof from the numeral-bound formula to the term-bound formula. Use equality substitution with the one-slot formula

\[H(u):=\forall z\,(z\le u\to B(\overline{\vec x},z)),\]

or its negation, or the corresponding existential matrix. Instantiating \(H\) at \(\overline n\) gives the previously accepted conclusion; instantiating it at \(t(\overline{\vec x})\) gives the required conclusion. This applies uniformly to all four signs and quantifiers.

There are two distinct slots under the quantifier: the bound variable \(z\) and the placeholder \(u\) for equality substitution. When the formula is moved under this additional slot, its existing quantified indices are shifted. The literal rewrite identities identify both instances after this shift and after removing the substitution slot. The terms used here are closed original-language terms, so no auxiliary name or additional free variable is introduced. The checked equality-substitution constructor now proves acceptance with precisely the original term-bound code. □
