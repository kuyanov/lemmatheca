Subsequences and endpoint lengths

Fix distinct real numbers \(a_1,\ldots,a_N\). A subsequence selects indices \(i_1<\cdots<i_k\), preserving their order. It is increasing when \(a_{i_1}<\cdots<a_{i_k}\), and decreasing when all inequalities reverse.

Let \(p_i\) and \(q_i\) be the greatest lengths of increasing and decreasing subsequences ending at \(a_i\). These maxima exist: there are finitely many choices, including the singleton \(a_i\). Label position \(i\) by \((p_i,q_i)\).

Distinct endpoint labels

The endpoint labels of Subsequences and endpoint lengths are pairwise distinct.

Proof

Take \(i<j\). If \(a_i<a_j\), append \(a_j\) to a longest increasing subsequence ending at \(a_i\), giving \(p_j\geq p_i+1\). Otherwise \(a_i>a_j\), and the same extension gives \(q_j\geq q_i+1\). Either way, the labels differ. □

Erdős–Szekeres theorem

For positive integers \(m,n\), every sequence of \(mn+1\) distinct real numbers contains an increasing subsequence of length \(m+1\) or a decreasing subsequence of length \(n+1\).

Proof

If neither exists, every label lies in \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}\). This set has only \(mn\) elements, whereas Distinct endpoint labels supplies \(mn+1\) different labels. The pigeonhole principle gives a contradiction. □

Can values repeat?

Does Erdős–Szekeres theorem remain true for strictly monotone subsequences if repeated values are allowed?

Show answerHide answer

No. Take \(m=n=1\) and the sequence \(0,0\). Its length is \(mn+1=2\), but it has no strictly increasing or strictly decreasing subsequence of length two.
