Add one composition rule to the actual trees

Use the scoped signed formulas, dual \(A^\perp\), finite-set contexts, and seven primitive rules. Write \(\mathcal C_X(\Gamma)\) for their raw trees. A cutful raw certificate has all seven constructors, with cutful children, and the additional constructor

\[\frac{p:\vdash\Gamma,A\qquad q:\vdash\Delta,A^\perp}{\vdash\Gamma\cup\Delta}\;\mathrm{cut}_A.\]

Thus cuts may occur inside a conjunction branch or beneath a protected quantifier. A recursive embedding copies every cut-free constructor into this enlarged family. Weakening also rebuilds these enlarged trees, including both children of a cut; it does not become an extra constructor.

A public cutful certificate uses the same auxiliary reserve as before: it is a cutful raw tree over \(X\sqcup U\), ending in precisely the left-renamed displayed context.

Keep set membership separate from a displayed occurrence

The desired cut operation accepts actual cut-free \(p\in\mathcal C_X(\Gamma,A)\) and \(q\in\mathcal C_X(\Delta,A^\perp)\), and returns a member of \(\mathcal C_X(\Gamma\cup\Delta)\). We construct it by logical rank followed by proof height. Calls on a smaller-rank formula may have larger trees. A call at the same rank must descend to a strict premise tree; renaming the other tree preserves its height.

Contexts are sets. If \(A\in\Gamma\), then \(p\) already weakens to the answer. If \(A^\perp\in\Delta\), the same is true of \(q\). More generally, the recursive procedures allow a supplied endsequent \(\Sigma\subseteq\Gamma,A\), rather than demanding a uniquely distinguished occurrence. Temporary components are retained in \(\Gamma\) even when one equals \(A\). This inclusion invariant handles contraction without deleting an occurrence that another branch still needs.

For example, commuting a disjunction rule retains both of its components in the recursive side context. Afterwards that actual disjunction rule is reapplied and its conclusion is weakened to the requested context. Equality of a component and the cut formula merely makes the temporary side context larger; it never invalidates the recursion.

Invert bottom, binary formulas and a specified universal instance

The following are operations on actual cut-free trees, with arbitrary finite target \(\Delta\):

  • From \(\Sigma\subseteq\Delta,\bot\), produce \(\mathcal C_X(\Delta)\).
  • From \(\Sigma\subseteq\Delta,B\land C\), produce both \(\mathcal C_X(\Delta,B)\) and \(\mathcal C_X(\Delta,C)\).
  • From \(\Sigma\subseteq\Delta,B\lor C\), produce \(\mathcal C_X(\Delta,B,C)\).
  • From \(\Sigma\subseteq\Delta,\forall xB\), produce \(\mathcal C_X(\Delta,B(t))\).

The last operation accepts a specified term \(t\) over \(X\). It does not choose a witness for an existential formula.

Induct on the supplied tree, allowing the target context and the formula to be inverted to vary. At an identity or truth leaf the witnessing literals or truth survive in the target, since none is the inverted nonliteral constructor. At a nonprincipal conjunction rule, retain its two components in the corresponding recursive targets, transform both children, and reintroduce the conjunction. Do the same with the single premise of disjunction or bottom introduction. The original nonprincipal formula already belongs to the target, so its final insertion changes nothing.

At a nonprincipal existential rule retain its actual instance and its specified witness. At a nonprincipal universal rule lift the entire target into the old-name component, retain the opened principal body, and recurse there. Reapply universal introduction. The inverted formula and any newly requested components are lifted too, so the protected tag is absent from the side context.

At a principal bottom rule skip the root and apply the induction hypothesis to its child. At a principal conjunction choose the required child; at a principal disjunction use its child with both components retained. Any old copy of the inverted formula in the side context is removed by that same recursive call, rather than being silently left behind.

For a principal universal rule, its premise has \(\Lambda^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(B)\), with \(\Lambda\subseteq\Delta,\forall B\). Apply the induction hypothesis to this smaller premise, inverting the lifted \(\forall B\) at the lifted term \(t\), while retaining \(\operatorname{open}(B)\). Now substitute \(t\) for the protected tag and fix every old name. Both the retained opened body and the requested lifted instance become \(B(t)\); lifted \(\Delta\) becomes \(\Delta\). The actual proof-substitution operation therefore supplies precisely \(\Delta,B(t)\). These cases exhaust the seven constructors and also explain why retained copies require the generalized invariant.

Replace a literal at its identity leaves

Cut is admissible when its formula is a signed relation literal, \(\bot\), or \(\top\).

For a positive literal \(a\), traverse the tree offering \(a\), carrying the supplied tree offering \(\bar a\). Maintain the inclusion invariant with \(a\) as the removable offer. At an identity leaf whose witnessing pair is different, both witnesses remain in the target, giving the same identity leaf. If its positive witness is \(a\), its negative witness \(\bar a\) belongs to the retained side context; consequently the supplied opposite-literal tree weakens to the answer. A truth leaf retains its truth witness.

Every nonleaf rule is nonprincipal for a literal. Transform its children, retaining its displayed components or instance, and reapply that rule. Beneath a universal rule rename the entire supplied opposite-literal tree into the left component and retain the opened body. This is a strict child call; renaming preserves height. The new tag remains protected. A negative literal exchanges the two supplied trees once and uses the same construction with its positive opposite.

For a cut on bottom, bottom inversion transforms the first tree to \(\Gamma\), which weakens to \(\Gamma\cup\Delta\). For a cut on truth, invert bottom in the second tree instead. Neither argument selects a tree from validity.

Reduce binary cuts to their smaller components

Assume cuts on every smaller-rank formula have already been constructed, over every parameter set. Then cuts on \(B\land C\) and \(B\lor C\) can be constructed.

For \(B\land C\), inversion of the first tree gives \(\Gamma,B\) and \(\Gamma,C\). Inversion of the opposite disjunction in the second gives \(\Delta,B^\perp,C^\perp\). Cut the first of these on \(B\), retaining \(C^\perp\); the result is \(\Gamma\cup\Delta,C^\perp\). Cut this against \(\Gamma,C\) on \(C\), then identify repeated unions. Both cut formulas have rank strictly below \(B\land C\).

For \(B\lor C\), inversion gives \(\Gamma,B,C\), while the opposite conjunction gives \(\Delta,B^\perp\) and \(\Delta,C^\perp\). First cut on \(B\), retaining \(C\), then cut on \(C\). The target is again \(\Gamma\cup\Delta\). The inversion operations include all nonprincipal propositional and quantified permutations, including collisions of a temporary component with an old side formula. No bound on the heights of the resulting smaller-rank cuts is required.

Move a universal cut through the existential-side tree

Fix a producer \(p\in\mathcal C_X(\Gamma,\forall xB)\). Assuming all smaller-rank cuts, traverse a consumer \(q\in\mathcal C_X(\Sigma)\) with \(\Sigma\subseteq\Delta,\exists xB^\perp\), seeking \(\mathcal C_X(\Gamma\cup\Delta)\). The traversal is by strict height descent, with all parameter sets, bodies and side contexts arbitrary.

Identity and truth leaves retain their witnesses in \(\Delta\), since an existential formula is neither a literal nor truth. At a nonprincipal conjunction, recurse on each child with its component retained in the target side context, and reapply conjunction introduction. At a nonprincipal disjunction retain both components; at bottom introduction recurse on the child and reinsert bottom. Each principal formula already belongs to \(\Delta\).

At a nonprincipal existential \(\exists C\), retain its actual instance \(C(s)\), recurse on its smaller child, and reintroduce \(\exists C\) with the same term \(s\). This case assumes \(\exists C\ne\exists B^\perp\); the equal case is the principal reduction below.

At a universal rule with body \(C\), lift the producer tree by \(X\hookrightarrow X\sqcup\{e\}\). Its offered formula is the lifted \(\forall B\), and its side context is \(\Gamma^\uparrow\). Recurse on the consumer's premise with target side context \(\Delta^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(C)\). The result is \((\Gamma\cup\Delta)^\uparrow,\operatorname{open}(C)\), so genuine universal introduction applies. Its principal \(\forall C\) already belongs to \(\Delta\). Renaming preserves rank and the producer's exact height, while the consumer child has smaller height; hence the secondary measure decreases. Nested quantifier rules protect their own newer tags in turn.

Replace a protected parameter by the consumer's witness

In the preceding traversal, a principal existential step uses a specified term \(t\) and a child offering \(B^\perp(t)\). Its side context may still contain \(\exists B^\perp\).

First apply the same strict-height traversal to that child, retaining \(B^\perp(t)\) in its target context. This removes every retained copy of the original existential offer and gives \(\Gamma\cup\Delta,B^\perp(t)\). Universal inversion of \(p\) at the actual \(t\) gives \(\Gamma,B(t)\). By the scope laws, \(B^\perp(t)=B(t)^\perp\). Cut these two trees on \(B(t)\), then weaken repeated contexts away. Substitution preserves logical rank, so

\[\operatorname{rk}(B(t))=\operatorname{rk}(B)<\operatorname{rk}(\forall B).\]

When the producer itself ends in universal introduction, its inversion is exactly the protected substitution \(e\mapsto t\), fixing all old names. Thus the principal pair is the ordinary quantified reduction, with an extra preliminary child traversal precisely when finite-set contraction retains the cut formula.

For a cut on \(\exists B\), use the supplied \(\forall B^\perp\) tree as the universal producer and traverse the supplied existential tree. At its principal witness \(t\), inversion gives \(\Delta,B^\perp(t)\), and the smaller child gives \(\Gamma\cup\Delta,B(t)\). Their instance cut again has rank \(\operatorname{rk}(B)\). This is the opposite orientation of the same defined traversal; it is not a recursive cut call on an unchanged rank and height.

Compose supplied cut-free trees without adding cut

For every complete signed \(A\), there is an actual tree operation

\[\mathcal C_X(\Gamma,A)\times\mathcal C_X(\Delta,A^\perp)\longrightarrow\mathcal C_X(\Gamma\cup\Delta).\]

Use strong induction on \(\operatorname{rk}(A)\), uniformly over parameter sets and contexts. Literals and constants use ; binary constructors use . Either quantifier uses the height recursion of and the smaller-rank instance cuts of . All recursive height calls concern strict children, with the other supplied tree unchanged or height-preservingly renamed. All other recursive cuts have smaller rank. The inversion proofs account for permutations on the universal producer, while the consumer traversal accounts for the other side. This defines the operation using only well-founded recursions and the seven primitive constructors.

Remove every cut while retaining the exact endsequent

Every cutful raw certificate of \(\Gamma\) transforms into a cut-free raw certificate of the identical \(\Gamma\). The same holds for public certificates. Consequently adding cut changes neither raw nor public derivability.

Recurse on the cutful tree. Copy an identity or truth leaf. At each of the five other primitive constructors, recursively transform its children and reapply that same constructor; the quantifier branch keeps its exact extended parameter type. At a cut, recursively eliminate both children and apply . The resulting tree contains only the original seven constructors.

For public certificates perform this raw operation over \(X\sqcup U\). No reserve name is deleted and the concluding context is unchanged. The reverse transformation is the recursive embedding of cut-free trees. These give both derivability equivalences. The theorem asserts a terminating construction, without a proof-size estimate or a claim that every possible reduction sequence terminates.

Specify the native equality-disabled system exactly

Let \(\Lambda\) be a finite set of equality-free full formulas and \(F\) an equality-free conclusion. Set

\[E(\Lambda,F)=\{N^-(G):G\in\Lambda\}\cup\{N^+(F)\}.\]

Native deductions mean the actual classical first-order trees with equality rules disabled. Before induction, apply their pure-endpoint construction, so every formula encountered is equality-free. Write \(G^+=N^+(G)\) in this paragraph's sequent calculations; then \(N^-(G)=(G^+)^\perp\). These are signed trees, whereas the preceding deduction conversions use their full readings.

Translate every propositional deduction rule into cutful trees

A pure classical native raw deduction \(\Lambda\Rightarrow F\), with equality disabled, yields a cutful raw certificate of \(E(\Lambda,F)\).

Induct on the native tree. An assumption uses the compound identity for its positive signed tree, then weakening. Conjunction introduction uses its two translated children and conjunction introduction. For a conjunction projection, construct the consumer \((B\land C)^\perp,B\) from the identity for \(B\), retaining \(C^\perp\) and introducing the dual disjunction; cut it against the conjunction child. The other projection interchanges the factors.

A disjunction introduction weakens its child's sequent by the other disjunct and applies disjunction introduction. For case elimination the branches offer \(B^\perp,F^+\) and \(C^\perp,F^+\), in the common negative ambient context. Conjunction introduction combines them into \((B\lor C)^\perp,F^+\); cut against the major disjunction. Implication introduction is disjunction introduction on the branch's offers \(B^\perp,C\), because the positive implication is \(B^\perp\lor C\). For implication elimination, build a consumer of the implication: weaken the translated antecedent to offer \(B\) alongside \(C\), and use compound identity to offer \(C^\perp\) alongside \(C\). Conjunction introduction gives \(B\land C^\perp\) alongside \(C\), the dual of the positive implication. Cut against the translated implication.

For negation introduction the branch offers the negative antecedent and bottom. Cut away bottom against a truth leaf; what remains is exactly the positive normalization of that negation. Negation elimination cuts the two complementary signed trees, then inserts bottom. Falsum elimination first cuts away its offered bottom against truth, then weakens by the requested conclusion. Classical contradiction has a branch with bottom and the positive normalization of its target, since the discharged assumption is its negation. Remove bottom in the same way. These are literal normalization clauses, not identifications of primitive negation with a signed dual reading. No equality constructor is available in the chosen native mode.

Translate the four native quantifier rules with their actual scopes

The same native-to-cutful translation extends through all four quantified constructors, retaining their exact protected contexts.

Universal introduction translates its opened-body branch in the extended parameter set. The normalization scope identities make the result precisely the lifted negative assumptions and the opened positive body. Reapply the sequent universal rule. Existential introduction translates its actual instance and reuses its actual term.

For universal elimination at \(t\), let \(B\) be the normalized body. Compound identity gives \(B(t),B(t)^\perp\); existential introduction on the second offer gives \(B(t),\exists B^\perp\). Cut this consumer against the translated universal. The output is exactly the positive normalization of the native instance.

For existential elimination, the translated protected branch offers the opened \(B^\perp\), the lifted ambient negative context, and the lifted positive conclusion. Regard that lifted conclusion as part of the side context and apply universal introduction to \(B^\perp\). Its new tag is absent from that side context by the native rule's hypothesis. The resulting \(\forall B^\perp\) is the dual consumer for the translated major existential; cut them. Thus all four constructors retain their native scope maps and the equality-disabled mode.

Read a sequent tree as a native contradiction

For a signed sequent \(\Sigma\), let \(R(\Sigma)=\{\neg r(A):A\in\Sigma\}\). Every cut-free raw tree of \(\Sigma\) yields an actual classical native deduction \(R(\Sigma)\Rightarrow\bot\), with equality disabled.

Induct on the seven-rule tree. An atomic identity supplies \(\neg a\) and \(\neg\neg a\), which give falsum. A truth leaf supplies \(\neg\neg\bot\), contradicting the theorem \(\neg\bot\). At conjunction introduction, each child refutation gives its factor by classical contradiction under the retained side assumptions. Conjoin the factors and contradict the principal negative conjunction assumption. At disjunction introduction the negated principal disjunction supplies the negation of each factor: assume that factor, introduce the disjunction, contradict and discharge. Replace the child's two negative assumptions by these deductions. Bottom introduction ignores the additional \(\neg\bot\) assumption and weakens the child refutation.

At universal introduction lift the negative side assumptions. In its protected branch assume the negation of the opened body and apply the child's refutation, obtaining that body by classical contradiction. Universal introduction now produces the principal universal, which contradicts its retained negation. Its protected tag is absent from the lifted side context.

At existential introduction with \(t\), its principal negation yields the negation of the instance: assume that instance, introduce the existential at \(t\), contradict and discharge. Substitute this deduction for the corresponding assumption in the child's refutation. All replacements are the existing actual native assumption-replacement operation. Thus every clause constructs a finite tree, including both quantified clauses.

Obtain exact correspondence with pure classical deductions

For equality-free finite \(\Lambda\) and equality-free \(F\), a native classical raw deduction \(\Lambda\Rightarrow F\) with equality disabled exists exactly when a cut-free raw certificate of \(E(\Lambda,F)\) exists. The same equivalence holds for their public certificates.

The forward operation is the preceding native rule translation followed by cut elimination. For the converse apply to the supplied encoded tree, then work in the native context \(\Lambda,\neg F\). For each encoded alternative supply the corresponding negative reading required by that refutation.

If the alternative is \(N^-(G)\) with \(G\in\Lambda\), assume its reading, use the negative backward conversion to obtain \(\neg G\), and contradict the ambient \(G\). Discharge the reading to obtain its negation. If the alternative is \(N^+(F)\), assume its reading, use the positive backward conversion to obtain \(F\), contradict the ambient \(\neg F\), and discharge. Assumption replacement inserts these actual deductions in the refutation, giving falsum from \(\Lambda,\neg F\). Classical contradiction concludes \(F\). The signed readings themselves are equality-free and every converter retains equality-disabled mode.

For public inputs perform these operations over the same \(X\sqcup U\). Reading and normalization commute with left renaming, so the encoded endsequent is exactly the required reserved one. This proves correspondence with the stated native fragment. It makes no assertion about deleting equality rules from an equality-enabled native proof with pure endpoints.

Allow actual instances of scoped subformula occurrences

A scoped subformula occurrence of \(A\in\mathcal N_X(0)\) is obtained by repeatedly choosing a child constructor. Below each quantifier its newest slot remains available; thus an occurrence \(C\) can lie in \(\mathcal N_X(n)\) for \(n>0\). Reflexivity includes \(A\) itself.

For an endsequent \(\Gamma\) over \(X\), a complete formula \(D\) over any parameter set \(Y\) is an instantiated subformula of \(\Gamma\) if there are \(A\in\Gamma\), a scoped subformula occurrence \(C\in\mathcal N_X(n)\) of \(A\), and an actual scope substitution \(\sigma:X\sqcup\{z_0,\ldots,z_{n-1}\}\to\operatorname{Term}(Y)\), with \(D=C\langle\sigma\rangle\).

Terms, argument tuples, signs and variable occurrences are retained in this equation. It is not a statement only about formula shapes. Allowing all \(Y\) includes the successive protected extensions inside a proof tree; allowing actual terms includes existential witnesses.

Keep the resulting tree inside instantiated endsequent subformulas

Every formula in every displayed sequent of a cut-free raw certificate of \(\Gamma\) is an instantiated subformula of \(\Gamma\), at that sequent's actual parameter type. Consequently every cutful certificate has a cut-free result with this property. The assertion also holds over the public reserve.

Start at the root: each of its members is its own subformula under the identity substitution. Propagate the property from a conclusion to each premise. A retained side formula uses the same representation. A conjunction or disjunction component selects the corresponding child of its representing occurrence; scope mapping preserves that constructor. Bottom introduction adds no premise formula to check.

For an existential premise, select the body's occurrence and extend the representing substitution by assigning its newest slot the actual witness term. For a universal premise, select the body's occurrence, lift the existing term images into the old-name component, and assign its newest slot the protected tag. These are precisely the actual instantiation and opening scope maps. Lift every side formula by composing its existing representation with the same left renaming.

Selecting a child preserves scoped subformula occurrence, and composing scope substitutions preserves the literal equation representing each instance. Induction down the finite tree therefore gives the claim at every sequent, including through nested changes of parameter type. Apply it to the tree returned by for the cutful assertion. The collection of permitted instances can be infinite because there can be infinitely many witness terms; this theorem supplies no finite first-order search space.

For a leading existential block with a quantifier-free matrix, witness extraction turns the cut-free tree into a finite list of actual term instances and a propositional factor proof. Leading universal parameters can be opened first; this restricted extraction does not assert a finite search space for unrestricted quantified formulas.

Neither cuts nor the reserve prove an empty sequent

There is no cutful raw or public certificate of the empty sequent, and none of a singleton bottom sequent.

Eliminate all cuts. A raw tree ending in the empty sequent has no possible last constructor: neither leaf has a witness and each other constructor inserts its principal formula. A tree ending in singleton bottom gives such an empty tree by bottom inversion. For public certificates the same argument is made over the reserved parameter set. Thus this consistency conclusion follows from the actual transformation and the primitive last-rule analysis.

Why not reduce a principal quantified pair immediately?

Suppose the existential consumer ends with witness \(t\), but its side context still contains the very existential cut formula. Is substituting \(t\) into the universal producer and making one smaller instance cut sufficient?

Show answerHide answer

No. The smaller instance cut would leave the old existential side formula in the result. Set insertion does not remember which occurrence was principal. The construction first recursively transforms the strict consumer child with its instance retained in the target side context. That removes the original existential offer even if it remains in that child's side set. Only then does the lower-rank instance cut remove the retained instance. This is the reason for the height induction inside the rank induction.

Does cut elimination manufacture a missing ground witness?

Can a public certificate be turned into a raw certificate over just its displayed parameters by eliminating cuts?

Show answerHide answer

No. The operation retains the actual parameter type of its input. In a constant-free language the public excluded-middle existence certificate can use a reserved variable as its witness, while the corresponding raw proof over no parameters is impossible because no term exists. Cut elimination transforms the public tree over that same reserve. Universal inversion also requires a supplied term; it cannot be invoked to choose a nonexistent ground term.
