Permit disjunctions in inputs, but not in the output position

Use the native propositional formulas with atoms \(p_0,p_1,\ldots\), falsum, conjunction, disjunction, implication and negation. A Harrop formula is generated by the following rules: every atom and \(\bot\) is Harrop; a conjunction of two Harrop formulas is Harrop; \(D\to H\) is Harrop whenever \(H\) is Harrop and \(D\) is any formula; and \(\neg D\) is Harrop for every \(D\). There are no other Harrop formulas.

Thus \((p\lor q)\to r\) and \(((p\lor q)\to r)\land\neg s\) are Harrop, whereas \(p\lor q\) and \(r\to(p\lor q)\) are not. The restriction concerns the positions from which a left rule can pass information to its continuation. It does not forbid every occurrence of disjunction. Native negation is included directly; its equivalent reading \(D\to\bot\) has a Harrop consequent as well.

Require every allowed assumption to be Harrop

A Harrop context is a finite set \(\Gamma\) all of whose members are Harrop. Write \(\Gamma,D\) for \(\Gamma\cup\{D\}\). A Harrop context may be inconsistent: for example, \(\{p,\neg p\}\) is Harrop. Consistency is not part of the definition.

Every subcontext of a Harrop context is Harrop. If \(X\land Y\in\Gamma\), both \(X\) and \(Y\) are Harrop, so \(\Gamma,X,Y\) is Harrop. If \(X\to Y\in\Gamma\), the consequent \(Y\) is Harrop, so \(\Gamma,Y\) is Harrop. These statements follow by inspecting the rule that can generate the displayed outer connective. No disjunction belongs to a Harrop context.

Keep the actual cut-free tree and its retained assumptions

Let \(\mathcal P(\Gamma,C)\) be the cut-free certificate set of the retained-principal intuitionistic calculus. Its members are finite proof trees, rather than assertions that a proof exists. Atomic identity, falsum-left and the native logical rules are its primitive rules. There is no cut constructor in \(\mathcal P\).

We will use the following left-rule notation. A membership witness \(h:X\land Y\in\Gamma\) and a tree \(q\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,X,Y,C)\) form \(L_{\land}(h,q)\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,C)\). For \(h:X\to Y\in\Gamma\), trees \(u\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,X)\) and \(q\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,Y,C)\) form \(L_{\to}(h,u,q)\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,C)\). The principal formula remains allowed in each premise.

If \(h:\bot\in\Gamma\), write \(L_{\bot}^{C}(h)\) for the falsum-left tree with conclusion \(C\). If \(h:\neg X\in\Gamma\) and \(u\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,X)\), write \(L_{\neg}^{C}(h,u)\) for negation-left with conclusion \(C\). These two rules already permit every conclusion. Finally, \(R_{\lor}^{0}(u)\) and \(R_{\lor}^{1}(v)\) introduce respectively the left and right disjunct from the supplied child tree.

Return a side together with its proof

A disjunct packet for \(\Gamma,A,B\) is either a pair \((0,u)\) with \(u\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,A)\), or a pair \((1,v)\) with \(v\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,B)\). Denote this tagged union by

\[\mathcal D_{\Gamma}(A,B)=\mathcal P(\Gamma,A)\sqcup\mathcal P(\Gamma,B).\]

The tag identifies which formula the returned tree proves. A tag-preserving lift of maps \(f:\mathcal P(\Delta,A)\to\mathcal P(\Gamma,A)\) and \(g:\mathcal P(\Delta,B)\to\mathcal P(\Gamma,B)\) sends \((0,u)\) to \((0,f(u))\), and \((1,v)\) to \((1,g(v))\). It cannot replace a left packet by a right packet merely because both formulas happen to be provable.

Only six last-rule shapes need to be handled

If \(\Gamma\) is Harrop and \(p\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,A\lor B)\), the last rule of \(p\) is one of the two disjunction-right rules, falsum-left, negation-left, conjunction-left or implication-left. In the conjunction-left and implication-left cases, the premise retaining the conclusion \(A\lor B\) again has a Harrop context.

Atomic identity concludes an atom. Conjunction-right, implication-right and negation-right have different outer conclusion symbols. Disjunction-left would require a disjunctive principal formula in \(\Gamma\), which excludes. These observations leave exactly the six stated shapes.

Conjunction-left adds its two Harrop components to its premise context. Implication-left adds only its Harrop consequent to the continuation context. Its other premise may prove an arbitrary antecedent, and we impose no Harrop condition on that antecedent. We will keep this other premise without trying to extract anything from it.

Rebuild a left rule around the selected factor

Suppose \(h:X\land Y\in\Gamma\). The conjunction lift sends a packet for \(\Gamma,X,Y,A,B\) to a packet for \(\Gamma,A,B\), by applying \(L_{\land}(h,-)\) to its proof and keeping its tag. It is well-defined because conjunction-left permits either chosen conclusion.

Suppose \(h:X\to Y\in\Gamma\), and retain a specified \(u\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,X)\). The implication lift sends a packet for \(\Gamma,Y,A,B\) to a packet for \(\Gamma,A,B\), by applying \(L_{\to}(h,u,-)\) to its proof and keeping its tag. The same original antecedent tree \(u\) is used on either side. For instance a selected left factor is rebuilt by the rule

\[\frac{u:\Gamma\Rightarrow X\qquad v:\Gamma,Y\Rightarrow A}{\Gamma\Rightarrow A}\ L_{\to}.\]

No assumption is silently discarded by this reconstruction. The output antecedent is exactly \(\Gamma\), with the original principal formula still available.

Follow the disjunction spine of the supplied tree

For a Harrop context \(\Gamma\), define the Harrop disjunction extractor \(E_{\Gamma}^{A,B}\) on \(p\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,A\lor B)\) by the following recursion on that supplied finite tree.

\[E(R_{\lor}^{0}(u))=(0,u),\qquad E(R_{\lor}^{1}(v))=(1,v).\]
\[E(L_{\bot}^{A\lor B}(h))=(0,L_{\bot}^{A}(h)),\qquad E(L_{\neg}^{A\lor B}(h,u))=(0,L_{\neg}^{A}(h,u)).\]

At \(L_{\land}(h,q)\), recursively extract from the continuation \(q\), then apply the conjunction lift. At \(L_{\to}(h,u,q)\), recursively extract from \(q\), then apply the implication lift with that same \(u\). The appropriate premise context determines the subscript of each recursive call. All other last-rule shapes are excluded by .

This is a terminating definition. Every recursive call uses the original strict continuation subtree \(q\). Rebuilding a left rule happens after the call and does not initiate another call. The invariant is that the current input proves the original fixed disjunction from a Harrop context, and its returned packet proves one fixed disjunct from that identical context. The context-closure facts above preserve this invariant whenever the recursion moves upward.

Extract a factor in the same Harrop context

For every finite Harrop context \(\Gamma\) and every supplied \(p\in\mathcal P(\Gamma,A\lor B)\), the specified recursion returns

\[E_{\Gamma}^{A,B}(p)\in\mathcal D_{\Gamma}(A,B).\]

At a disjunction-right constructor it returns exactly its side and its child tree. At conjunction-left and implication-left it preserves the recursively selected side and rebuilds that same left rule around the factor.

Induct on the input tree, retaining its context in the induction assertion. The two right-introduction cases already supply the requested child. Falsum-left and negation-left supply a proof of the left factor, with the same assumption or contradictory antecedent tree, as prescribed in the recurrence.

For conjunction-left, the continuation has a Harrop context, so the induction hypothesis supplies a packet there. Its conjunction lift returns the same side and a valid proof from \(\Gamma\). For implication-left, apply the induction hypothesis only to the continuation. Reapply implication-left with the unchanged original proof of its antecedent. This uses no extraction assertion for that possibly non-Harrop antecedent. Each tag and each stated constructor equation follows from its defining case. The last-rule classification exhausts the proof.

In particular, inconsistent Harrop contexts cause no exception. The extractor chooses the left side in the contradiction cases and returns an actual proof of it.

The equality-free first-order grammar also admits universal formulas. Its disjunct extractor adds a universal-left continuation case, while existential extraction retains either a term with its instance proof or a proof of falsum in the same Harrop context.

Normalize a supplied cut certificate before extracting

Let \(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{cut}}(\Gamma,C)\) denote the same retained-principal calculus with its additional primitive cut constructor, and let \(\operatorname{ce}\) be its established cut-elimination operation. For an input \(p\in\mathcal P^{\mathrm{cut}}(\Gamma,A\lor B)\), the cut-input extractor is the exact composition

\[E^{\mathrm{cut}}_{\Gamma}(p)=E_{\Gamma}(\operatorname{ce}(p)).\]

For Harrop \(\Gamma\), its result is a disjunct packet in that same context, because cut elimination preserves both the antecedent and the concluding disjunction. No selected side is assigned to an unfinished normalization stage: the output is precisely the side chosen from the actual normalized tree.

Recover factor existence for the established natural calculus

For every finite Harrop context \(\Gamma\), ordinary intuitionistic derivability satisfies

\[\Gamma\vdash A\lor B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (\Gamma\vdash A)\text{ or }(\Gamma\vdash B).\]

The exact presentation theorem supplies existence of a cut-free certificate from the left-hand derivability assertion. Applying the extractor gives existence of one factor certificate, and the same presentation theorem transfers it back to natural deduction. Conversely, introduce the appropriate disjunct in the given natural deduction.

This conclusion is an existence assertion about deductions. The data extraction statement in takes an actual tree as input and specifies the factor it returns; a bare derivability assertion does not specify which proof tree supplied that input.

The same factor-existence implication holds when an arbitrary, possibly infinite set \(T\) consists entirely of Harrop formulas.

By finite support, a deduction of \(A\lor B\) uses a finite \(\Delta\subseteq T\). That subcontext is Harrop, so the finite result supplies a deduction of one factor from \(\Delta\); weakening gives it from \(T\).

Discharge a fixed Harrop assumption after the choice

Suppose \(\Gamma\) is Harrop and \(H\) is Harrop. A supplied cut-free certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow H\to(A\lor B)\) gives a tagged cut-free certificate of either \(\Gamma\Rightarrow H\to A\) or \(\Gamma\Rightarrow H\to B\). Consequently,

\[\Gamma\vdash H\to(A\lor B)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (\Gamma\vdash H\to A)\text{ or }(\Gamma\vdash H\to B).\]

Apply implication-right inversion to the actual input tree, producing a tree of \(\Gamma,H\Rightarrow A\lor B\). This is a Harrop context. Apply \(E_{\Gamma,H}\), and apply implication-right to the selected factor tree, preserving its tag. Thus the specified operation is inversion, followed by extraction, followed by tag-preserving implication introduction. The presentation theorem yields the displayed existence version.

With \(\Gamma=\varnothing\), disjunction introduction also gives a theorem of \((H\to A)\lor(H\to B)\). The hypothesis here is that the actual antecedent \(H\) is Harrop. Its behavior under formula substitution must be checked separately; it is not an extra primitive rule of arbitrary deductions.

On numbered atoms, the unrestricted implication–disjunction schema has the same added-axiom consequences as Dummett’s principle. That result compares two sets of extra assumptions. The construction here retains its stated Harrop hypotheses on the supplied sequent trees.

A disjunction inside an implication input causes no difficulty

For distinct atoms \(p,q,r,s,t\), let \(I=(p\lor q)\to r\), \(H=I\land\neg s\), and \(\Gamma=\{p,H\}\). There is a cut-free tree \(d\) proving \(\Gamma\Rightarrow r\lor t\) for which \(E_{\Gamma}(d)\) selects the left factor. Its factor proof retains both the conjunction-left and implication-left inferences.

Both assumptions of \(\Gamma\) are Harrop. Put \(\Delta=\Gamma,I,\neg s\), the premise context for conjunction-left at \(H\). In \(\Delta\), the atom leaf for \(p\) followed by left disjunction introduction gives a specified tree \(u\) of \(p\lor q\). In \(\Delta,r\), the atom leaf for \(r\) followed by left disjunction introduction gives a tree \(v\) of \(r\lor t\).

Apply implication-left with principal \(I\) to \(u,v\), then conjunction-left with principal \(H\). The resulting \(d\) has the stated endsequent. Extraction first passes through conjunction-left, then through the implication's continuation. At \(v\) it returns the actual atom leaf proving \(r\). The two lifts reconstruct implication-left using the same \(u\), then conjunction-left. The result is a proof of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow r\), even though \(u\)'s conclusion \(p\lor q\) is not Harrop.

The chosen side depends on the supplied proof

There are two cut-free proofs of the same closed disjunction for which the extractor returns different tags.

Let \(T=\bot\to\bot\), and let \(u\in\mathcal P(\varnothing,T)\) be implication-right applied to the falsum assumption leaf. The two inputs \(R_{\lor}^{0}(u)\) and \(R_{\lor}^{1}(u)\) both prove \(T\lor T\) from the empty Harrop context. The defining equations give respectively \((0,u)\) and \((1,u)\). Thus the map reads the supplied tree; it does not choose a canonical side from the concluding formula alone.

The Harrop hypothesis cannot simply be dropped

For distinct atoms \(p,q\) and \(\Gamma=\{p\lor q\}\), there is a cut-free certificate of \(\Gamma\Rightarrow p\lor q\), but neither \(\Gamma\vdash p\) nor \(\Gamma\vdash q\).

Compound identity gives the certificate of the disjunction. A Boolean valuation with \(p\) false and \(q\) true satisfies the assumption and refutes \(p\); the reversed valuation refutes \(q\). Boolean soundness excludes both factor deductions, as in the existing ordinary-assumption counterexample. The context's sole formula is not Harrop, and a cut-free proof may use disjunction-left there. That is precisely the branching case excluded by the last-rule lemma.

Does every formula substitution preserve the Harrop condition?

If \(H\) is Harrop, must every uniform substitution instance \(H[\sigma]\) also be Harrop? What does this imply for the choice rule?

Show answerHide answer

No. The atom \(p_0\) is Harrop, while the substitution \(\sigma(p_0)=p_1\lor p_2\) makes it non-Harrop. The extraction theorem applies to the substituted proof only if its resulting context is still Harrop.

A sufficient restriction is that every atom image is Harrop. Induction on a Harrop formula proves preservation: conjunction uses both component hypotheses; implication needs only its consequent hypothesis; and every native negation remains Harrop regardless of its substituted argument. The arbitrary implication input therefore introduces no additional restriction.

Without such a condition the generalized choice inference is not uniformly admissible. Substitute \(p_1\lor p_2\) for the Harrop antecedent atom in the premise \(p_0\to(p_1\lor p_2)\), leaving \(p_1,p_2\) fixed. The resulting premise is identity, while the conclusion becomes

\[((p_1\lor p_2)\to p_1)\lor((p_1\lor p_2)\to p_2).\]

At the root of a two-leaf Kripke fork, let one leaf force only \(p_1\) and the other only \(p_2\). Each implication fails at the root because of the opposite leaf, so their disjunction has no theorem proof by Kripke soundness. The older rule with antecedent \(\neg C\) is different: every substitution retains that outer negation and hence the Harrop condition.

Does the theorem choice justify a new rule inside an open branch?

If \(H\) is Harrop and an arbitrary context \(\Sigma\) deduces \(H\to(A\lor B)\), may it infer \((H\to A)\lor(H\to B)\) by this result?

Show answerHide answer

The result just proved requires the whole allowed context to be Harrop. It does not justify this use for arbitrary \(\Sigma\). Even \(H=\neg p\) gives the existing four-world counterexample: the root forces \(K=\neg p\to(q\lor r)\) but not \((\neg p\to q)\lor(\neg p\to r)\). Thus \(\Sigma=\{K\}\) deduces the displayed premise by assumption but cannot deduce the proposed conclusion.

The obstruction is visible syntactically: \(K\) is not Harrop, because its implication consequent is a disjunction. The theorem-preserving rule for a negated antecedent remains substitution-admissible, but admitting it as a general open-branch inference would strengthen intuitionistic deduction. Under a Harrop \(\Sigma\), by contrast, the exact implication-choice construction above applies.
