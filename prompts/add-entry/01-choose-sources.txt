Read README.md, corpus/README.md, docs/entry-sources.md, and
docs/formalization-experiments.md. Inspect nearby active entries and the taxonomy.
Respect existing repository instructions and preserve unrelated work.

1. Inspect the actual sources. Identify the chapters, sections, or pages supporting
   each proposed definition, result, example, and proof. Verify bibliographic
   details; distinguish sources inspected from recommendations not yet accessible.
   Do not invent citations or claim to have read unavailable material.
2. Propose one coherent reading unit with explicit prerequisites and boundaries.
   Avoid repeating explanations already available in another entry; propose links
   where the reader can reasonably follow them. Keep enough context for this entry
   to be readable. Do not expand the topic just to match available library lemmas.
3. Briefly inspect the existing node registry and pinned Lean/mathlib sources for
   likely matches. Prefer existing formalizations over rebuilding foundations.
   Flag representation differences and likely missing results, without treating
   a matching declaration name as evidence of mathematical correspondence.
4. Prepare original exposition, not a transcription of the source. Identify source
   credit and any figure reuse requirements. Prefer references suitable for the
   intended reuse, and do not copy substantial passages or figures without a basis
   for doing so.

Return the proposed entry ID, scope, outline, existing entries to link, and a
source-to-outline map with precise locations. Include proposed based_on citation
objects using the current entry schema, and distinguish supplied facts from open
questions. These citations will be written into entry.json at the drafting step.
Do not create the entry, formal nodes, proofs, or review records in this step.
